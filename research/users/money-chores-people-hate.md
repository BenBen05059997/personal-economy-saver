# Recurring money admin people hate, beyond tax (research as of 3 Nov 2026)

Tags: [measured] survey or dataset, [community] forum post, [vendor] company claim, [inference] my reading. Quotes are 15 words or fewer.
Method note: 35 searches plus 8 page fetches. Web search is US-indexed, so Germany, India, Bangladesh and Nigeria are thin. Reddit and MoneySavingExpert forum threads were mostly not retrievable; community evidence is the weakest layer and is flagged as such.

## Summary (10 lines)

1. [measured] UK "life admin" is about 5 hours a week (Starling, 2,000 adults, Apr 2026); 85% say they struggle to sort it; they paid about £346 in late fees and fines. Commissioned survey, self-reported.
2. [measured] Unwanted subscriptions are the best-measured pain. UK government estimates 9.7M unwanted contracts and £1.6bn a year; 3.6M come from trials that roll over (gov.uk consultation, 2023-24).
3. [measured] Cancelling hurts: 75% of Australians with subscriptions had a bad cancellation experience, 1 in 10 gave up and kept paying (CPRC, Aug 2024). US: 42% forgot a service they had stopped using (C+R, 1,000 adults, 2022).
4. [measured] Staying put costs money. The UK "loyalty penalty" was £3.9bn to £4.1bn a year across five markets (CMA / Citizens Advice, 2018); the FCA banned insurance price-walking in Jan 2022 and expected £4.2bn savings over 10 years.
5. [measured] Missed or late payments are mainstream: 37% of US adults paid a late fee in 12 months (NerdWallet, 2,061 adults, 2024); 48% paid a bill late (LendingTree via press, Dec 2025); US overdraft/NSF fees were still $5.8bn in 2023 (CFPB).
6. [measured] Split money is a big source of friction: 16% of UK couples' money arguments are about contributions to bills (Opinium for Aviva); 51% of 18-24s argued about splitting a bill (YourMoney).
7. [measured] Remittance senders send often and pay a lot: 46% of US remittance-app users send at least monthly (Consumer Reports, Apr 2025); the global average cost of sending $200 is about 6.4% to 6.5% (World Bank, 2025), twice the UN 3% goal.
8. [measured] Rate resets are a dated cliff: about 1.8M UK fixed-rate mortgages end in 2026 (UK Finance, Dec 2025); Australia had a similar fixed-rate cliff in 2023.
9. [measured] Money sits unclaimed or unused: A$2.7bn unclaimed with ASIC (2026); roughly $21bn to $27bn of US gift cards unused (Bankrate); 11.9% of loyalty points expire unused (Antavo, 2026).
10. [inference] Nearly every chore above is a future-dated obligation or a missed deadline. A 30-day runway that lists dated events and flags "this one needs a decision by X" is aimed at the right problem; no mainstream app joins subscriptions, renewals, split debts and client payments into one dated list with a cash forecast.

## Sections

### 1. Life admin and who does it
- [measured] Starling (2,000 UK adults, Mortar Research, Apr 2026): 5 hours a week, 85% struggling, £346 average in late fees and fines. Tasks missed include MOTs and jury summons. https://www.starlingbank.com/news/admin-nights-can-save-brits-thousands (vendor-commissioned, but I read the release).
- [measured] Admiral MultiCover: 13 days a year, about 34 minutes per weekday. https://www.admiral.com/press-office/stressed-out-time-poor-brits-spend-13-days-a-year-on-life-admin-and-running-errands (search summary, not re-read).
- [measured] US financial management: CIT/Harris 2021 7 h/month; Digit 2021 4 h/month; NW Mutual with BLS time-use 2019 only 1.2 h/month. Spread shows definitions drive the number. https://uk.news.yahoo.com/study-americans-spend-104-hours-144300460.html
- [measured] Who does it: Australia (ABS 2024) women 4h53 vs men 3h52 of unpaid work a day, but that is all housework. https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/women-spent-more-time-men-unpaid-work-may. Germany (2025, n=2,675): women do most planning; men think it is equal. Italy (2025, 415 couples): 63% of women say they organise. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11426. Finance only: Austria (BIPA, 2026) 51% of men say they handle household finance tracking alone or mostly. https://rewe-group.at/en/newsroom/2026/06/mental-load-in-austria-women-bear-a-greater-burden-and-feel-it-much-more-intensely. New York Life (2024): married men and women both say about 6 h/week on household finance (secondhand).
- Gap: no clean India or Germany finance-only time study found. [inference] "Admin" figures are inflated by commissioned surveys; use the direction (hours a month, not minutes) with care.

### 2. Subscriptions
- UK: government estimate of 9.7M unwanted contracts and £1.6bn a year, 3.6M from trials that roll over, 1.3M from auto-renewals. Rules (DMCC Act 2024 Part 4): reminders, a renewal cooling-off period and single-step cancellation, with CMA enforcement expected in 2027 (law-firm notes disagree on dates). https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/retail-law-roundup-april-2026/subscriptions-and-dmcc-act
- US: FTC Click-to-Cancel final rule (16 Oct 2024) was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025 on procedural grounds; a Feb 2026 client alert says the FTC restarted with an ANPRM (single source). State auto-renewal laws still apply. https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/consumer-trading-unfair-trading/1652134/eighth-circuit-cancels-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule
- US: C+R Research (1,000 adults, 2022): 54% off by $100+ a month; 42% forgot a stopped subscription. CNET 2024 (secondhand): 48% forgot to cancel a free trial.
- Australia: CPRC "Let Me Out" (Aug 2024): 75% bad cancel experience. EU: the Digital Fairness Act is targeted for 2026 and names hard cancellation and trial conversion (no percentage found).
- [community] r/canva post: "Charged $200 for annual subscription after forgetting to cancel trial", 5 points, resolved by chat support. One anecdote only. https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1psluuj/snapshots/2025-12-23T06%3A50%3A01.483745Z
- Apps: Rocket Money (cancel and negotiate; vendor claims $740 average for Premium, includes paid negotiation at 40% success fee) and others. [vendor] https://www.rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/does-rocket-money-work. Solved partially; they find charges but do not tie them to your runway.

### 3. Bills: switching, bill shock, autopay
- Loyalty penalty: CMA found £3.9bn lost across five sectors (mobile, broadband, home insurance, mortgages, savings), energy excluded because of the price cap. https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/citizens-advice-issues-super-complaint-as-loyal-customers-continue-to-be-penalised-by-over-4-billion-a-year/
- Energy fix ending: suppliers must write 42 to 49 days before; you can switch penalty-free in the last 49 days (Money to the Masses, 2026). [inference] This is a perfect dated event for a 30-day runway.
- Which? (Oct 2023, 9,025): 1 in 5 said direct debit set too high. Direct debits mean the user's cash is tied up and payday-to-bill timing is uncertain.
- Ofcom's pounds-and-pence rule helps knowing future rises; the April rise is predictable. https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/technology/broadband-mobile-networks/ofcoms-inflation-linked-mid-contract-price-hike-ban-heres-how-it-affects-you
- India: UPI AutoPay mandate failures are a payments-rails problem; RBI issued a Digital Payments e-Mandate Framework on 21 April 2026 (single source). I could not verify the 55% to 90% failure claim against NPCI.
- Gap: no survey I found measures "bill-due-before-payday" directly. Late-payment surveys above are the proxy. [inference]

### 4. Insurance and renewals
- UK: FCA price-walking ban from 1 Jan 2022; Which? (31 Aug 2022) said many still paid more to renew. https://insurancetimes.co.uk/news/insureds-facing-higher-renewal-prices-despite-fcas-price-walking-ban-which/1442191.article. GoCompare: one in five drivers auto-renew without checking quotes.
- US: J.D. Power 2025: 57% shopped in the year; Motley Fool 2025: only about 4% switched in March. People look but rarely move.
- Germany: Kfz deadline is 30 November with one month's notice; 2.2M planned to switch. Today (3 Nov) is inside the window. https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4827905/Kfz-Versicherung--Wechsel--Stichtag-30-November/
- Health insurance: German statutory fund rate rises give a 2-month special-termination right. [measured/legal] https://www.aerzteblatt.de/archiv/vertraege-richtig-kuendigen-7a2e9f60-4468-4359-92dd-207d01fd6ee1
- Known solutions: comparison sites do the quote; none own the "remind me 49 days out, then show the new total" ritual. [inference]

### 5. Splitting money
- Couples: UK 38% split 50/50; only 40% of those in a relationship advocate an even split (MoneySuperMarket, secondhand). Opinium/Aviva: 16% of arguments are about contributions. Fidelity 2026 Couples study (fielded Oct to Nov 2025): 58% of couples do not split evenly; 60% of the partner with less responsibility worry about taking over (PDF jumbled; verify). https://www.planadviser.com/younger-couples-more-likely-to-work-together-on-finances/
- Friends: Venmo-commissioned: 65% found owing more stressful than being owed. Forbes Advisor/OnePoll: 47% split bills on apps in new ways due to inflation (secondhand).
- [community] Roommate threads (r/AmItheAsshole, news write-ups): missing November rent and a refusal to repay. Etiquette norms: wait a week before nudging, $5 minimum. https://dailydot.com/redditor-tells-roommates-mom-about-1400-in-unpaid-rent-after-covering-missed-payments-i-literally-just-answered-a-question
- Known: Splitwise (free app: groups, simplify debts, recurring expenses; Pro adds receipt scanning, currency conversion). I could not verify its user numbers. It tracks IOUs but does not move money; settling is on another app. https://www.splitwise.com/ [vendor]. India example: a Gurugram couple splitting rent and Swiggy bills went viral (Business Today, Jul 2025).

### 6. Remittances
- Consumer Reports (Apr 2025, nationally representative; tested India, Mexico, Nigeria, Philippines apps Sept 2025): 46% of US app users send monthly. Score: Wise 60, Remitly 60, Western Union 54, Xoom 52, MoneyGram 45. A 1% US tax on cash remittances started 1 Jan 2026. https://www.consumerreports.org/money/digital-payments/remittance-apps-high-costs-hidden-fees-privacy-concerns-a428-a4287935586/
- World Bank: global average 6.49% (Q1 2025), 6.36% (Q3 2025); target is 3%. I could not read the corridor tables (403). https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/RPW_main_report_and_annex_Q325.pdf
- Fees change behaviour: each $1 of fee cut raised monthly sending about $25, by more frequent sends (El Salvador field experiment).
- Philippines: BSP surveys on recipients show spending on daily needs and debt, not sender worries. Bangladesh: hundi persists on cost and speed; official fee cited at $9.40 per $100 (news, undated).
- Gap: no sender survey on "payday timing" or "monthly ritual" found. [inference] The monthly send is a fixed dated outflow that should sit in a runway.

### 7. Rent, deposits, mortgage resets
- UK deposits (Generation Rent, 2025): 23% unfair deductions; 4% to 20% used formal dispute (sources differ); 46% did not know they could. Opinium sample 2,000.
- US rent: pandemic-era Apartment List data is old. Fed 2025 (secondhand): median rent $1,200; 33% of low-income renters behind.
- Mortgage: UK about 1.8M fixes ending in 2026, up from 1.6M in 2025; average 2-year fix 5.75% (May 2026, Moneyfacts via press). Standard variable about 7.5%. https://ukfinance-newsroom.prgloo.com/news/uk-finance-mortgage-market-forecast-2026-2027. Australia 2023: about $95bn fixed loans expiring in three months. https://www.mpamag.com/au/news/general/150000-households-set-to-tumble-off-mortgage-cliff/454563
- [inference] A reset date plus the new payment is the cleanest "known future shock" in this whole file.

### 8. Fees, overdraft, BNPL, FX
- CFPB: overdraft/NSF revenue $5.83bn in 2023, down 51% from 2019; NCLC (June 2026) says overdraft fees are rising again without the CFPB rule (Congress repealed it). https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026.06_Issue-Brief_Overdraft-Fees-Rising-In-Absence-of-CFPB-Rule-1.pdf
- BNPL: CFPB (Dec 2025) reports multi-loan visibility is a concern; LendingTree figures above are survey-based.
- FX: DCC is dismissible at the terminal; see table.

### 9. Receipts, warranties, returns, points, gift cards, unclaimed money
- Strong numbers for gift cards and unclaimed funds; weak for receipts and warranties (no credible survey found). Loyalty: Antavo-based 2026 report, 26.2% of points unspent, 11.9% expire; the $10bn figure is an extrapolation. https://couponsinthenews.com/2026/03/03/quiet-quitters-are-losing-10-billion-a-year-in-loyalty-savings/
- UK dormant assets and US NAUPA totals: only blog figures found, not used. Australia ASIC: A$2.7bn, no time limit on claims.

### 10. Medical and school bills
- US: JAMA Health Forum (2023, 1,100+ adults) shows disputing works but is under-used; roughly 20% had a disputed or unaffordable bill. Zelis (2023): half of those with errors saw at least $200 incorrect, 70% spent 2+ hours. KFF: about $220bn medical debt.
- Schools: only vendor evidence (ParentPay, ParentMail). [inference] The pain is many small, irregular, dated asks.

### 11. Moving house and country
- UK: 39 organisations, about 10 hours (2015, old). Homemove (2020s) says over 30. Nothing found on closing and opening accounts across countries or on multi-currency life beyond Wise-style forum chat (moveandmoney posts looked promotional and were not used).
- [community] Forum posts say the real cost is not the fee but the exchange-rate markup, e.g. "free" transfers hiding 2%. https://www.expat.com/en/forum/asia/philippines/1101583-wise-exchange-rate.html

### 12. Freelancer and creator income (core to the product, not tax)
- 29% of freelance invoices late (Bonsai); 18.5 days late (UK, n=1,002); IPSE 20 days a year chasing. Freelancers Union 2015 figures are old. This supports "nudge a late client" as a first-class action.

## What a 30-day runway app can take off people

Ranked by people affected x frequency x pain. Rank is [inference]; inputs are cited above.

1. Subscription and trial radar: list every recurring charge with next date and amount; flag trials about to convert and annual renewals. (UK 9.7M unwanted contracts; AU 75% bad cancel; US 42% forgot.) Action: one-tap "cancel or keep" with a link.
2. "Will this bill clear on the day?": compare each autopay date to forecast balance, warn before low-balance days, suggest moving a date or a transfer. (US 37% late fee; 48% paid a bill late; India AutoPay failures; overdraft $5.8bn.)
3. Renewal and fix-ending alarms: energy fix end (42 to 49 day window), car and home insurance, broadband out-of-contract, Kfz 30 Nov. Show "if you do nothing, you pay about X more". (Loyalty penalty £3.9bn to £4.1bn; 57% US shoppers.)
4. Late client chase: nudge or invoice, see the shortfall change. (29% of invoices late, 18.5 days.) Already the product's action; strongest PayPal fit.
5. Shared-expense settle-up: who owes whom, one request on the 1st or after a trip, with payment through PayPal. (Couples, roommates, friends; 16% of UK couple arguments about contributions.)
6. Monthly remittance planner: put the family send on the calendar, show fee and recipient amount, flag a cheaper provider, keep the send ahead of rent. (46% of US app users monthly; 6.4% cost.)
7. Rate reset and rent-increase heads-up: date, old and new payment, effect on safe-to-spend. (1.8M UK mortgages in 2026; AU cliff 2023; US low-income renters 33% behind.)
8. Variable bill sanity check: flag direct debits or bills that jump or sit far above the true cost. (1 in 5 UK DD too high.)
9. BNPL and instalment roll-up: one dated list across providers. (63% hold several; 47% paid late.)
10. Hidden fee radar: FX markups and DCC, ATM and overdraft fees, with "avoid next time" notes. (UK £26M a day claimed by a vendor; AU 60% paid foreign fees.)
11. Refunds, deposits and unclaimed money: remind to dispute a deposit deduction, claim a refund window, use a gift card or points before expiry. (46% UK renters unaware they can dispute; $21bn to $27bn gift cards.)
12. Medical and school bills: capture due date and amount from a photo or email, flag duplicate or odd charges. (38% find medical bills confusing; many small irregular school payments.) Lowest confidence; evidence is thin.

Caveats: most percentages come from commissioned or press-reported surveys, not original reports I could read; the strongest primary-source items are the gov.uk consultation, CPRC, CFPB, CMA/Citizens Advice, FCA, UK Finance and ASIC. Where I could not verify (Fidelity 2026, Mint autopay, FTC restart, ABS details) the text says so.

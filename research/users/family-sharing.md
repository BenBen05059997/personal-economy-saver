# Family sharing: what couples and families need

Research for letting a partner join a household and sharing a view with family.
Method: web search plus a few help-center fetches. Several help pages (Monarch) returned 403, so some facts come from search snippets and third-party pages. Marked [inf] = my inference. Marked [gap] = not found.

Honest limits: few real Reddit threads surfaced (search returned mostly SEO pages). Pain points below lean on help-center design choices and review summaries, not a Reddit count. "How often they come up" in the ranking means how often across the sources I found.

## 1. How apps let a second person in

### Honeydue (US, couples)
- Invite by email; each partner has own login. If the invite went to a different email than the partner signed up with, you cannot see each other; fix = cancel and re-invite the right address. https://support.honeydue.com/en/articles/3179633-i-invited-my-partner-after-signing-up-myself-but-we-can-t-see-each-other-what-do-we-do
- Per-account privacy: owner sets "Partner can see" to balances + transactions, balances only, or nothing. https://support.honeydue.com/en/articles/3283211-how-to-change-what-my-partner-can-see
- Per-transaction privacy: any single transaction can be hidden from the partner ("useful for a surprise gift"). Same page.
- Complaints are about bank sync, generic "purchase" categories and slow support, not about the privacy controls. https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/honeydue-review/ (review site, may be affiliate)

### Monarch Money (US)
- Settings > Members > invite by email; invitee needs a new email (cannot already have a Monarch account). Each person has own login. Accounts are assigned an owner at invite time. https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048393452-Add-Members-to-an-Existing-Account (via search summary)
- Members have the same visibility and management rights. You cannot hide accounts or transactions. https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/20926382202004-Monarch-for-Couples (via search summary)
- A member can remove themselves, or the owner can remove them; accounts added under a removed member's login are removed too. Changing the admin needs support. Same source.
- "Shared Views": tag accounts/transactions personal or shared and filter by ownership; budget stays one shared budget; everyone still sees everything. https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/42228648365076-Shared-Views-in-Monarch (via search summary)
- Member cap reported as 5 by one review, "unlimited" by another; unverified. https://useorigin.com/resources/blog/finance-apps-that-let-you-add-a-partner-in-2026

### YNAB Together (US)
- Manager (subscriber) invites up to 5 people from Settings; one subscription, each person gets own login. Members cannot manage sharing, delete the plan or Fresh Start. https://www.ynab.com/introducing-ynab-together-for-your-shared-financial-journey/
- Manager picks which plans to share; members can keep their own plans. https://ynab.com/features/subscription-sharing
- In 2022 a staff reply said sharing part of a budget was not possible; no newer confirmation found. https://libreddit.winscloud.net/r/ynab/comments/yd7hkh/new_feature_ynab_together/iuw37vh/?context=3

### Splitwise (global)
- Invite friends by email or SMS and add bills before they claim the account; a link invite is also reported. Group members can be added by name only from the website. https://splitwise.uservoice.com/knowledgebase/articles/206028-why-do-i-have-to-add-friends-in-order-to-use-split
- Users ask for "guest" members with no invite. https://splitwise.uservoice.com/forums/162446-general/suggestions/6336816-add-the-ability-to-add-guests
- Pro about $3-5/month or $30-40/year, varies by country; app store is the price authority. https://getfinny.app/blog/splitwise-pricing-2026

### Copilot Money (US)
- No real multi-user. Official way: partner enters your email at login, you forward the Magic Link email. Warning: every logged-in device has full control. https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/4523792-sharing-your-account-with-a-partner
- Tips: one category group per person, tags for whose transaction. Same page.
- Shared budgets is an open feature request. https://roadmap.copilot.money/feature-requests/p/couplefamily-support-shared-budgets

### Goodbudget (US, envelopes)
- Register a Household; partner logs in with the same username/password on their own device. Both edit. Free syncs 2 devices, Premium 5. https://goodbudget.com/?p=12636

### Zeta, Tandem (US couples)
- Zeta: joint banking; each transaction marked personal or shared; notes on a transaction as reminders to the partner. https://www.trendhunter.com/trends/couples-finance-app (weak source)
- Tandem: split expenses by income-based ratio, settle in app, no joint account needed; about $3-12/month. https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/17/tandem-modern-couples-app-fintech
- Invite flow and roles for both: [gap].

### Japan
- Money Forward ME "シェアボード": a Premium host invites a guest; host chooses which accounts, cards, brokerage to share; guest must be a Money Forward ME user. Free plan: 4 linked accounts, 1 year of history. https://www.appbank.net/2025/09/17/iphone-application/2823615.php
- Zaim "ペア家計簿": share only the financial services you choose with a partner (cards, e-money, shopping data added Feb 2025). Older way: two people share one login. https://paymentnavi.com/?p=156610 and https://thebridge.jp/2015/12/zaim-multi-account
- OsidOri (400k+ couples): personal and shared books in one app, warikan (split) and settlement, bank sync, no web version, premium about ¥480/month. https://media.monex.co.jp/articles/-/17364 and https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20230130/93863.html. Invite method (code vs link) and private-item rules: [gap].
- Others: おカネレコプラス keeps personal books visible only to the owner; まねーぼ and マネタメ offer multiple books (personal / shared / pocket money). https://lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp/?p=10655 and https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id1599687071
- Pairy / 2Pay / シェアリー: not verified; I did not find official pages. [gap] (シェアルー exists: https://apps.apple.com/JP/app/id1475406336)

## 2. Pain points couples and families report

- **Privacy of personal spending.** Honeydue built per-account and per-transaction hiding for it. A 2013-2025 pattern in Japan: personal vs shared books or choose-which-accounts. A guide warns that unclear personal/shared boundaries expose private spending. https://lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp/kakeibo-app-sharing-tips/
- **Surveillance feels like distrust.** Constant visibility of a partner's purchases is called monitoring that breeds resentment; hidden or reclassified spending erodes trust. https://www.inkl.com/news/9-money-saving-habits-that-secretly-signal-you-don-t-trust-your-partner (news aggregator, weak)
- **One person does all the work.** The partner who sets up the app and keeps it current carries the load and resentment; one Redditor says his wife never uses the tools he builds. https://getfinny.app/blog/track-spending-as-couple-2026 and https://www.inkl.com/news/8-money-saving-tips-couples-swear-by-until-one-partner-starts-cheating-the-system
- **Duplicates, gaps, unclear owner of entry.** Two editors cause double entries; when nobody owns input, balances drift. A weekly check fixed one failure case. https://lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp/kakeibo-app-sharing-tips/
- **Who pays what.** Fair split is not always 50/50; income-proportional split and a joint bills pot plus personal money are the common answers. https://jobble.com/articles/finance-tips-for-couples-especially-when-one-has-an-irregular-income and Tandem above. Japanese styles: fixed contribution, split by cost category, or pooled with allowance (お小遣い制). https://www.habitto.com/blogs/kakeibo-kyoyu-app-fufu-couple/
- **Irregular income.** Guides say budget on the lowest month or a 12-month average, and agree up front how the irregular earner's swings change "fun money". https://jobble.com/articles/finance-tips-for-couples-especially-when-one-has-an-irregular-income and https://millo.co/budgeting-for-freelancers-managing-irregular-income-effectively
- **Too many features.** Some Japanese couples quit because the app was too complex; simple is preferred. https://www.habitto.com/blogs/kakeibo-kyoyu-app-fufu-couple/
- **Money talk itself.** Sources recommend a monthly sit-down check; apps are framed as a way to cut arguments. https://lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp/kakeibo-app-sharing-tips/
- **Surprise bills.** [gap] No source found tying this directly to couples; [inf] it follows from the freelancer case: the partner is surprised when a client pays late.

## 3. Sharing a summary with family who will not install

What apps offer (found):
- Monarch: Shared Views only inside a multi-member household; no read-only link or PDF found. Advisor read-only access is claimed by a third-party review only. https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/42228648365076-Shared-Views-in-Monarch and https://tooliverse.ai/tools/monarch-money
- Copilot: forward a login link (full control, not a read-only view). https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/4523792-sharing-your-account-with-a-partner
- Splitwise: non-users can be added to bills by email/SMS and claim later. https://splitwise.uservoice.com/knowledgebase/articles/206028-why-do-i-have-to-add-friends-in-order-to-use-split
- Read-only expiring link, monthly PDF, CSV: [gap] none of the apps checked advertise them for family.

What people do (found):
- Japanese couples use LINE Note to share household info; articles recommend a shared app over screenshots. No source found for LINE screenshots; [inf] it is common but undocumented here. https://library.libecity.com/articles/01JG3BTTSA381V410Z55JYSZ8H
- Monthly sit-down review of the shared numbers. https://lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp/kakeibo-app-sharing-tips/

[inf] The gap (people want a view for a parent or partner without an account, and apps mostly force an account or a full-control login) is an opening for a read-only link or LINE-friendly summary.

## 4. Privacy and safety

- Shared financial access is a known coercive-control tool: surveillance of purchase history through shared banking, changing shared passwords to cut off benefits, repurposing legitimate apps to monitor. https://www.techsafety.org/financial-abuse-and-technology and https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10463032
- NNEDV asks of any app: who gets the data, can the user control further sharing, can transaction history be hidden, can the user avoid importing contacts. Same techsafety page.
- Safety planning advice: use accounts and devices only you can access; changing settings can alert the abuser. Same page.
- Design lessons visible in the apps:
  - Monarch: a member can leave on their own. Good. But a leaving member's accounts are removed, and you cannot hide accounts. https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/20926382202004-Monarch-for-Couples
  - Copilot: a forwarded login gives full control to every device. Bad for revocation. https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/4523792-sharing-your-account-with-a-partner
  - Honeydue and Money Forward: owner chooses what each partner sees. Good. https://support.honeydue.com/en/articles/3283211-how-to-change-what-my-partner-can-see
- [inf] A read-only link must not expose: account numbers or bank-sync tokens; the owner's personal (not shared) items; client names and invoice amounts unless the owner picks them; PayPal ids, emails, invite codes; any edit or approve action (money-moving actions must never work from a link); full transaction history when a summary is enough. It should expire, be revocable in one tap, show who created it, and be viewable without login.
- [inf] A leaving member must be able to leave alone, without owner approval, and should not be notified of hidden items. The owner should see a plain list of who has access and since when.

## What matters most

Ranked by how often the need appears across the sources above:

1. **Control what the other person sees** (per account, per item, personal vs shared). Honeydue, Money Forward, Zaim, OsidOri, Japanese guides, NNEDV all centre on it.
2. **Who owes whom / fair split** (income-based share, shared bills, settle up). Splitwise, Tandem, OsidOri, couples guides.
3. **Own login, equal use, easy leave** (partner joins with their own account; either side can leave). Honeydue, Monarch, YNAB, Goodbudget; leave and revoke from NNEDV and Monarch.
4. **Spread the work** (one person should not be the only bookkeeper; simple entry, clear owner). Guides and one Reddit voice; Japanese sources on entry rules.
5. **Irregular income made visible** (lowest-month baseline, "if the client is late" shown to the partner). Freelancer guides; apps barely cover it, so [inf] this is the product's edge.

Weakly sourced but worth noting: a view for family without an account (section 3, mostly gaps).

## Invite and share patterns

1. **Email invite, join with own login.** Honeydue, Monarch, YNAB, Splitwise, Money Forward. Owner or manager can remove; member can usually leave. Partner must not already have an account in some apps (Monarch).
2. **Owner picks what is shared.** Per account or per transaction (Honeydue), choose-which-services (Money Forward, Zaim), personal vs shared books (OsidOri, おカネレコプラス). Default for hidden items matters.
3. **Share the login or a magic link.** Goodbudget, Copilot, old Zaim. Easy, but no roles, no audit, and revoking means changing the password or logging out devices. Risky for safety reasons.
4. **Entered by one, claimed by the other.** Splitwise lets one person add bills for someone who has not joined yet, who can claim later. Fits the "family who will not install" case.
5. [inf] Not found in any app, likely valuable: **read-only, expiring, revocable link or summary message** (LINE friendly) with no login and no actions.

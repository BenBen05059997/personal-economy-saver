# Wealth moves: United Kingdom and Germany

Researched Oct 31, 2026. Every figure carries a source and the date it was checked. "Verified primary" means I read the figure on the government, regulator, statute or institution page myself. "Secondary only" means it came from a trade, press or adviser page, or from a search-result summary of a primary page I could not open. "Not verified this session" means it is background knowledge I could not check before the search budget ran out. Treat those as "unknown, verify" until someone checks them.

Worked examples assume the app's first user: a sole-trader freelancer paid through PayPal, with profit of about £40,000 (UK) or €45,000 (Germany), single, no children, unless the row says otherwise.

## United Kingdom

### Summary: four highest-value moves for a ~£40k freelancer

1. Context: at £40,000 profit in 2026/27 you pay about £5,486 income tax and £1,646 Class 4 NI (gov.uk rates). You are a basic-rate taxpayer, so most moves are worth about 20–28p per £1, and much more where the government adds a bonus.
2. **Move 1: Stop the PayPal cross-border leak.** On a payment from a US client converted to GBP, PayPal's published business fees are 2.9% + 1.99% + £0.30 to receive, plus 3% to convert: about 7.9% in total.
3. → If half of £40k arrives that way, that is about £1,580 a year. Bank-transfer invoicing or a multi-currency account cuts most of it, and every fee you still pay is a deductible expense.
4. **Move 2: Lifetime ISA, if you are 18–39.** £4,000 in a year gets a £1,000 government bonus (25%). It is the best guaranteed return here for a first home or retirement at 60, but withdrawing for anything else costs 25% of the pot, which leaves you 6.25% below what you paid in.
5. **Move 3: Personal pension with relief at source.** Every £80 you pay becomes £100 in the pot (£4,000 → £5,000). The limit is 100% of profit and the annual allowance is £60k. Carry forward of three past years matters only in a big year.
6. → If profit later goes above £50,270, you get extra relief through Self Assessment, and pension contributions are one of the few ways to bring income back under the £60k Child Benefit charge line.
7. **Move 4: Claim every allowable expense and get ready for MTD.** That covers the home-working flat rate (up to £26 a month), the PayPal and conversion fees, equipment and software. Making Tax Digital quarterly updates start on 6 April 2027 for gross income over £30k (gov.uk), so a clean ledger now saves fees later.
8. Also check: Marriage Allowance (£252 a year, backdatable to 2022/23); voluntary NI for low-profit or abroad years (£189.80 for a Class 2 year, roughly £358 a year of State Pension for life); and the car-finance redress letters (average about £830 per agreement, payments expected in 2027).
9. Biggest leaks to flag: overdrafts at roughly 35–40% EAR (secondary), credit card minimum payments, BNPL taken before 15 Jul 2026 (no new protections), and claims firms taking up to 36% of car-finance compensation.
10. Regulatory line: the app can show these rules and calculate them with the user's own numbers. Naming a specific investment, pension or fund as right for that user is FCA-regulated advice. "Targeted support" (live since 6 Apr 2026) is open only to authorised firms.

### Lifetime ISA (LISA)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** UK residents who open the account and make the first payment before age 40 (from age 18). Contributions are allowed until 50. It suits a first-time buyer purchasing a home at or below £450,000 with a mortgage, or someone saving for age 60+.
- **What it is:** A savings or investment ISA where the government adds 25% to whatever you pay in, up to £4,000 a year. You can use it penalty-free to buy your first home (at least 12 months after your first payment) or after 60. Take it out for anything else and the provider keeps 25% of what you withdraw.
- **Value:** bonus = 25% × contributions (max £4,000/yr → max £1,000/yr). Inputs: age, annual amount, purpose. Example: £4,000 paid in during 2026/27 → £1,000 bonus → £5,000. An unauthorised withdrawal of all £5,000 costs £1,250, so you get £3,750 back: £250 (6.25%) less than you paid in. Over 20 years at the maximum, the bonus alone is £20,000.
- **Time to money:** the bonus is paid within weeks (claimed monthly by the provider). The home or age-60 payout takes years.
- **Risk:** behavioural (the 25% withdrawal charge if plans change). Market risk too if you choose a stocks & shares LISA.
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** 5 Apr 2027 for the 2026/27 £4,000. The first payment must be made before you turn 40.
- **Traps and caveats:** 25% charge on unauthorised withdrawals, and also when you transfer to a non-LISA ISA before 60. A home over £450,000 triggers a 20% charge (gov.uk). You must buy with a mortgage, and the money goes direct to the conveyancer. Mortgages from relatives are excluded. A cash LISA held to 60 can lag inflation.
  - **Reform:** HM Treasury consulted (23 Jun – 17 Aug 2026) on a "First Time Buyer ISA" to replace the LISA, targeted for around April 2028. It would have no withdrawal charge, with the bonus paid only at purchase. Bonus, caps and price cap are not yet set, and existing LISAs would carry on. No government response was found as of Oct 31, 2026.
  - Contributions count inside the £20k ISA allowance.
- **Why people miss it:** They assume "ISA = cash savings". Self-employed people with no workplace pension rarely hear that it doubles as a retirement product with a 25% top-up.
- **What we may say:** We can state the rules and run the bonus and penalty maths on the user's numbers. Saying "you should open a LISA with provider X" or picking the investments inside it is a personal recommendation on a designated investment, which is regulated advice. Generic "people in your situation often compare a LISA with a pension" is guidance.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** The account must be funded in GBP. Income earned abroad is fine while you are UK-resident. If you move abroad you cannot pay in further, but the account stays open.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/lifetime-isa (£4,000/yr, 25%, max £1,000/yr, first payment before 40, contributions to 50, ISA allowance £20,000 for 2026 to 2027), checked Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/lifetime-isa/withdrawing-money-from-your-lifetime-isa (25% charge, exceptions, 12-month rule, £450,000 cap with 20% charge, mortgage/conveyancer rules), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/06/hmt-publishes-consultation-on-the-proposed-first-time-buyer-isa/ and the consultation document https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a33c4586422bec01b117765/FTB_ISA_Condoc.pdf (FTB ISA proposal, responses due 17 Aug 2026), Oct 31, 2026
  - "Age 18" minimum and "April 2028 target": secondary (https://www.landc.co.uk/insight/government-to-replace-lifetime-isa-with-new-first-time-buyer-isa), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (allowance, bonus, charge, £450k cap); secondary only (FTB ISA timing, minimum age 18)

### Stocks & Shares ISA (2026/27 allowance)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** UK residents aged 18+ with money they will not need for 5+ years.
- **What it is:** A wrapper around investments (funds, shares). Growth, dividends and withdrawals are free of UK income tax and capital gains tax. You can put in up to £20,000 a year across all your ISAs. Unused allowance is lost at 5 April.
- **Value:** tax saved = dividend tax + CGT you would otherwise pay on the same investments outside the ISA. It grows with balance and time. Example: £300/month for 20 years in a global index fund. Outside an ISA, a basic-rate taxpayer pays 8.75% dividend tax above the £500 dividend allowance and CGT above the £3,000 exemption (rates not verified this session). Inside the ISA, nothing. The bigger win is behavioural: it is the default home for long-term money beyond pension and LISA.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market (values fall as well as rise, and you can lose money)
- **Effort:** an evening to open, then ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** tax-year end, 5 Apr 2027
- **Traps and caveats:** Platform and fund fees compound. Compare percentage versus flat fees at your balance. "ISA" does not mean safe. Watch for unregulated "ISA-like" schemes and Innovative Finance ISA lending risk.
  - **From 6 Apr 2027:** under-65s will be barred from transferring stocks & shares ISA money into a cash ISA (gov.uk factsheet, seen via search). The overall £20k stays.
- **Why people miss it:** They think investing needs lots of money or an adviser, or they leave everything in cash.
- **What we may say:** We can explain the wrapper, the allowance and the fee maths, and show historic ranges with a risk warning. We may not tell the user which fund or platform to buy (that is a personal recommendation, regulated advice under FCA PERG 8.28). Financial promotions of specific products also fall under FCA rules.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** No change while UK-resident. Pay in GBP.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/lifetime-isa (states the ISA allowance is £20,000 for 2026 to 2027), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fiscal-events-2026-factsheets/isa-reform-2027-anti-circumvention-rules-factsheet (transfer restriction from 6 Apr 2027; seen only in a search summary, not opened), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (£20k); secondary only (2027 transfer rule); not verified this session (dividend allowance £500, CGT exemption £3,000, dividend basic rate 8.75%)

### Cash ISA and the Personal Savings Allowance (cash cap falls in April 2027)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** UK residents 16+ (cash ISA) holding emergency or short-term savings.
- **What it is:** Interest inside a cash ISA is tax-free. For 2026/27 you can still put the full £20,000 into cash ISAs. From 6 April 2027, people under 65 can only put £12,000 a year into cash ISAs. The other £8,000 can still go into stocks & shares. Balances built before then are not affected.
- **Value:** tax saved = interest above your Personal Savings Allowance × your rate. Example: £15,000 at 4% earns £600, which is under the basic-rate £1,000 allowance (not verified this session), so the ISA saves nothing today. At £30,000 it earns £1,200, and £200 of that would be taxed at 20% (£40). The real value is "use this year's £20k cash allowance before the cap" for people with large cash balances or who expect to become higher-rate.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** none (FSCS-protected up to the limit per banking licence; limit not verified this session)
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** 5 Apr 2027 is the last tax year with a £20k cash limit for under-65s
- **Traps and caveats:** Withdrawals from a non-flexible ISA lose the allowance. Teaser rates drop. Many cash ISAs pay less than the best taxable easy-access accounts.
  - For reference, Bank Rate was 3.75%, held on 17 Sep 2026 (secondary). NS&I Direct Saver pays 3.75% from 18 Aug 2026.
- **Why people miss it:** Most basic-rate savers do not need it yet, and the 2027 cap is not widely understood.
- **What we may say:** Compare rates and tax treatment with the user's balance. Naming a deposit account is not "advice on investments" (deposits are not designated investments), but promotions rules and any affiliate commission must be disclosed.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None
- **Sources:**
  - Cash ISA £12,000 under-65 cap from 6 Apr 2027 and over-65 £20,000: GOV.UK factsheet above (search summary) plus secondary https://www.closesavings.co.uk/personal/news/cash-isas-how-they-work-and-whats-changing-in-april-2027, Oct 31, 2026
  - NS&I rates: https://www.nsandi.com/about-us/press-releases/2026/boost-for-premium-bonds-including-around-308k-extra-tax-free-prizes-plus-improved-rates-four-other-nsi-savings-products (18 Aug 2026), Oct 31, 2026
  - Bank Rate 3.75%: secondary https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/10990/, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** secondary only (2027 cap, though repeated by several bank sites); verified primary (NS&I rates); not verified this session (PSA £1,000/£500, FSCS limit)

### Help to Save (50% government bonus)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** UK residents receiving Universal Credit who (with any partner) had take-home pay of at least £1 in the last monthly assessment period. Self-employed UC claimants qualify if they had any take-home earnings that month. If you stop claiming later, you keep the account.
- **What it is:** Save £1–£50 a calendar month for 4 years. The government adds 50p per £1 of your highest balance, paid at the end of year 2 and year 4. Max bonus £1,200.
- **Value:** bonus = 50% of the highest balance reached (year 1–2), then 50% of the growth in highest balance (year 3–4). Example: £50/month for 48 months = £2,400 saved + £1,200 bonus = £3,600.
- **Time to money:** years (bonuses at 24 and 48 months)
- **Risk:** behavioural (withdrawing lowers the "highest balance" bonus base; closing early loses the next bonus and you cannot reopen)
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** must be on UC with earnings in the month you apply. The June 2026 consultation response (secondary summary) says the scheme becomes permanent and widens from April 2028 to UC households with children or caring responsibilities.
- **Traps and caveats:** Savings count as capital for UC: £6,000–£16,000 reduces UC, and above £16,000 ends it. The bonus itself is not counted as income for UC (secondary). One account per lifetime.
- **Why people miss it:** Freelancers on UC in a lean month do not know a single £1 of take-home earnings qualifies them.
- **What we may say:** Eligibility checklist and bonus maths: information only, not regulated.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Earnings for UC are profit in GBP. Foreign-currency receipts must be converted. You must live in the UK.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income and https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income/eligibility (£50/month, 50p per £1, bonuses at years 2 and 4, £1 take-home pay condition), Oct 31, 2026
  - Consultation response PDF (unreadable to my tool) https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a3a600a33bc5beefd3c476b/Help_to_Save_Reform_Delivery_Consultation_Response_23_June.pdf; summary via secondary search results, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (mechanics, eligibility); secondary only (permanence, 2028 widening, capital treatment)

### Personal pension tax relief for the self-employed, and carry forward

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed UK taxpayers under 75 with relevant UK earnings (trading profit).
- **What it is:** When you pay into a personal pension or SIPP, the provider claims 20% basic-rate relief from HMRC and adds it. Higher and additional-rate taxpayers claim a further 20% or 25% through Self Assessment.
  - Relief is limited to 100% of your earnings that year. The annual allowance is £60,000.
  - Unused allowance from the previous 3 tax years can be carried forward for a big year (you must have been a scheme member in those years; not verified this session).
- **Value:** pot = your payment ÷ 0.8 at basic rate. Inputs: profit, rate band, contribution.
  - Example A (£40k profit): pay £4,000 → £5,000 in the pot. When you draw it, 25% is usually tax-free (not verified this session).
  - Example B (£60k profit): £9,730 of profit is taxed at 40%. A gross contribution of £9,730 (you pay £7,784) also earns £1,946 extra relief through Self Assessment, and keeps income below the High Income Child Benefit Charge start.
  - Carry forward example: a £100k-profit year could take up to £100k gross relief if the past three years' unused allowances cover it.
- **Time to money:** years (access from 57 from 2028; not verified this session)
- **Risk:** market and behavioural (locked until pension age)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** payment must be made by 5 Apr to count for that tax year. Higher-rate relief is claimed on the return (31 Jan).
- **Traps and caveats:** Relief does not reduce Class 4 NI. Contributions above 100% of profit get no relief. Flexibly accessing a pension triggers the Money Purchase Annual Allowance (lower limit). Avoid pension "liberation" scams promising early access.
  - For UC claimants, pension contributions are deducted from self-employed earnings (not verified this session).
- **Why people miss it:** No employer auto-enrols them. Higher-rate freelancers often never claim the extra relief on the return.
- **What we may say:** Show relief maths and allowance headroom using the user's profit. Recommending a specific pension or fund, or that they should transfer pensions, is regulated advice. Pension transfers have extra rules.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign-client profit is still relevant UK earnings while UK-resident. If non-resident, you can only get relief on up to £3,600 gross for 5 years (not verified this session).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/annual-allowance (£60,000; taper over £200k/£260k; carry forward 3 years), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/pension-tax-relief (100% of earnings; 20% at source; extra 20%/25% via SA; £2,880 net with no earnings), Oct 31, 2026
  - Carry forward and earnings cap interaction: secondary https://www.quilter.com/help-and-support/technical-insights/technical-insights-articles/contributions-and-the-earnings-limit, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (allowance, relief rates, 100% cap); not verified this session (MPAA amount, pension age 57, UC treatment, 25% tax-free)

### Voluntary National Insurance to fill State Pension gaps

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:**
  - Self-employed people with profits under £7,105 in a year (voluntary Class 2 at £3.65/week).
  - Anyone else with a gap year: Class 3 at £18.40/week.
  - Freelancers with profits of £7,105+ are treated as having paid Class 2 and need do nothing for that year.
- **What it is:** Each qualifying year adds about 1/35 of the full new State Pension (£241.30/week) to your pension for life. Low-profit start-up years and years abroad leave gaps you can buy back cheaply.
- **Value:** pension gained ≈ £241.30 ÷ 35 = £6.89/week ≈ £358/year for each year filled, for life.
  - Class 2 year costs £189.80 → it pays for itself within about 7 months of retirement.
  - Class 3 year costs £956.80 → it pays for itself in about 2.7 years.
  - Inputs: NI record, years to State Pension age.
- **Time to money:** years (paid from State Pension age)
- **Risk:** low (the rules could change; only worth it if a year is missing and would raise your pension)
- **Effort:** an evening (check record at gov.uk "Check your National Insurance record")
- **Deadline or season:** gaps can normally be filled for the past 6 tax years (deadline details at gov.uk/voluntary-national-insurance-contributions/deadlines; not verified this session). Class 2 for 2025/26 is paid via the return by 31 Jan 2027 (secondary).
- **Traps and caveats:** Check first: some years add nothing, for example if you will reach 35 years anyway or have contracted-out years.
  - From 2026/27 people living abroad can no longer pay cheap voluntary Class 2 for periods overseas, only Class 3 (secondary, ACCA).
  - Scams: HMRC never asks for NI top-up payments by text.
- **Why people miss it:** People assume freelancers automatically get State Pension credit, which is true only when profit is £7,105 or more.
- **What we may say:** Showing the record maths is information. The State Pension is not an investment, so this is not regulated advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** The UK-resident freelancer is unaffected. If you move abroad, Class 2 is gone (Class 3 only).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates (Class 2 £3.65, SPT £7,105, Class 4 6%/2%, £12,570/£50,270), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/voluntary-national-insurance-contributions/rates (2026/27 Class 2 £3.65, Class 3 £18.40), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/new-state-pension/what-youll-get (£241.30/week, 35 years), Oct 31, 2026
  - Abroad change: secondary https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2026/March/Changes-to-voluntary-national-insurance-contributions.html, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (rates, £241.30); secondary only (abroad rule, 2025/26 deadline); not verified this session (6-year window)

### Marriage Allowance

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Married couples or civil partners. One partner earns under the £12,570 Personal Allowance. The other is a basic-rate taxpayer (income about £12,571–£50,270; Scotland £12,571–£43,662).
- **What it is:** The lower earner hands £1,260 of their unused allowance to the partner, cutting the partner's tax by up to £252 a year. You can backdate to the 2022/23 tax year.
- **Value:** £252 × eligible years. Example: a freelancer on £40k with a student or carer spouse earning £6k claims now for 2026/27 plus 2022/23–2025/26 → up to £252 now plus 4 × £252 = £1,008 backdated (only for years both conditions held).
- **Time to money:** weeks (backdated amount refunded by cheque or bank; current year through the tax code or return)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** the 2022/23 year drops out of the backdating window at 5 Apr 2027 (4-year rule implied by the gov.uk page; confirm)
- **Traps and caveats:** It does not work if the higher earner pays 40%. Paid "tax refund" agents take a cut of a free 10-minute claim.
- **Why people miss it:** Couples assume it is automatic. It is not.
- **What we may say:** Eligibility check and £ estimate. Information only.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None (UK tax residents)
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/marriage-allowance (£1,260, up to £252, backdate to 6 Apr 2022, income bands), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### Trading and property allowances (£1,000 each)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Individuals with small side income: selling services or crafts, casual jobs, renting out a driveway or room (property allowance), and similar. Not for income from your employer, your own company or a partnership.
- **What it is:** The first £1,000 of trading income and the first £1,000 of property income are tax-free and need no Self Assessment registration. Above £1,000 you can deduct £1,000 instead of actual expenses.
- **Value:** tax saved = min(£1,000, income) × your rate, or (£1,000 − actual expenses) × rate when claiming partial relief. Example: a £2,500 side gig with £150 of real costs → deduct £1,000 instead → £850 more deduction × 20% = £170 income tax saved (plus Class 4 if profit passes £12,570).
- **Time to money:** months (via the return)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** registration by 5 Oct after the tax year if over £1,000
- **Traps and caveats:** For a full-time freelancer (£40k), real expenses almost always beat £1,000. You cannot use both the allowance and actual expenses for the same income.
  - The planned rise of the reporting threshold to £3,000 is not in force. The start date is unconfirmed (reported as 2027/28 or later), and the £1,000 allowance itself is not rising (secondary).
- **Why people miss it:** Side hustlers either over-report with no deduction or under-report out of fear.
- **What we may say:** Information and calculation only.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None (worldwide income counts towards the £1,000).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income (amounts, restrictions; page last updated 8 May 2019), Oct 31, 2026
  - £3,000 threshold status: secondary https://www.pie.tax/tax-pible/tax-free-trading-allowance-uk, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (allowance); secondary only (£3,000 threshold timing)

### Self-assessment expenses freelancers miss, and Making Tax Digital

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed sole traders filing Self Assessment.
- **What it is:** Every allowable business cost cuts both income tax (20%) and Class 4 NI (6%), so it is worth 26p per £1 at £40k.
  - Often missed: the home-working flat rate (£10/£18/£26 per month for 25–50/51–100/101+ hours), a business share of phone and broadband, software, equipment (usually 100% in year one via the Annual Investment Allowance; not verified this session), professional subscriptions, business mileage, and **PayPal and currency-conversion fees**.
  - MTD from 6 Apr 2027: with gross income over £30k, quarterly digital updates through compatible software are mandatory.
- **Value:** saving = expenses × (income tax rate + Class 4 rate). Example: £312 home-working flat rate + £1,200 PayPal fees + £600 software/phone share = £2,112 × 26% ≈ £549 a year.
- **Time to money:** months (lower balancing payment on 31 Jan)
- **Risk:** low (keep receipts. Over-claiming personal costs risks penalties)
- **Effort:** an evening, then ongoing bookkeeping
- **Deadline or season:** 31 Jan filing and payment. MTD quarterly updates from 2027/28 (first due 7 Aug 2027 per secondary sources).
- **Traps and caveats:** Payments on account: in your second year the January bill can be about 150% of a year's tax. Student loan repayments are not included in payments on account (not verified this session), so a big January surprise follows. The simplified home-working flat rate replaces actual-cost claims for the same costs.
- **Why people miss it:** Fear of HMRC, no bookkeeping habit, not knowing PayPal fees are deductible.
- **What we may say:** Categorise transactions and estimate tax with the user's data. Preparing returns for a fee is not FCA-regulated but is a professional service. Present estimates, not tax advice certificates.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Income is taxed in GBP. Convert each receipt at the rate actually received, or another reasonable, consistently used rate (HMRC practice; not verified this session). PayPal's receiving and conversion fees are deductible.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/working-from-home (flat rates), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sole-traders-and-landlords-earning-more-than-30000-urged-to-act-now (MTD over £30k from 6 Apr 2027; over £50k since 6 Apr 2026), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates (Class 4), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (flat rates, MTD dates, NI); not verified this session (mileage 45p/25p, AIA, student-loan exclusion from payments on account, FX rule)

### VAT threshold and exporting services

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** UK sole traders approaching £90,000 of taxable turnover, or selling services to clients abroad.
- **What it is:** You must register for VAT once taxable turnover in any rolling 12 months passes £90,000, or is expected to within 30 days.
  - "Out of scope" supplies do not count. Most business-to-business services sold to an overseas business customer are supplied where the customer belongs, so they are outside UK VAT and do not count toward £90k.
  - Selling to overseas consumers (B2C) is generally a UK supply and does count. Services you buy from overseas businesses under the reverse charge do count toward taxable turnover.
- **Value:** VAT avoided = 20% of fees you would otherwise have to add or absorb. Example: £95k turnover of which £60k is B2B to US companies → taxable turnover £35k → no registration, avoiding 20% on £35k of UK consumer sales (£7,000) where clients cannot reclaim VAT.
- **Time to money:** immediate (pricing)
- **Risk:** low (wrong classification creates back-VAT and penalties)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** monitor the rolling 12-month figure monthly
- **Traps and caveats:** Keep evidence the customer is a business abroad: a VAT number, or registration or commercial documents. Digital services to consumers have special rules. Voluntary registration can pay off if you have big UK-VAT costs and only business clients.
- **Why people miss it:** They count all PayPal receipts toward £90k, or ignore reverse-charge purchases (Adobe and similar subscriptions billed from abroad).
- **What we may say:** Track turnover against the threshold and explain categories. Classification of a specific supply is tax advice; show it as "likely, check".
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This is the key row. Foreign B2B clients paid via PayPal are usually out of scope, so they do not count.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register (£90,000; taxable turnover excludes exempt and out-of-scope; includes reverse-charge services received), Oct 31, 2026
  - https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-place-of-supply-of-services-notice-741a (B2B general rule, evidence of business status, B2C rule), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### Bank switching bonuses (Current Account Switch Service)

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** UK adults with an existing current account who can move direct debits and pay in a set amount.
- **What it is:** Banks pay cash to switch your current account to them via the free Current Account Switch Service (50+ banks). The service moves payments and closes the old account, and the Switch Guarantee covers mistakes.
- **Value:** bonus − any lost perks. Example: Nationwide £175. It needs a full CASS switch completed within 28 days, at least 2 direct debits on the old account, £1,000 paid in within 31 days and 1 debit card payment. Pay-in rules usually let you recycle your own money, but Nationwide excludes money moved from existing accounts.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** low (behavioural: an overdraft or credit check at the new bank)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** offers come and go monthly. Most 2026 offers had closed by 31 Oct 2026 (Barclays 27 Aug, first direct 15 Jul, Santander 7 Oct).
- **Traps and caveats:** It is a credit search (soft or hard varies). The old account closes, so a freelancer's PayPal withdrawals and HMRC direct debit must be repointed. Offers usually exclude people who had an account with the bank recently. Bonuses are not taxable for individuals (not verified this session).
- **Why people miss it:** People think switching is a hassle. With CASS it is mostly automatic.
- **What we may say:** Listing live offers is information. If we earn affiliate commission, disclose it. Deposits are not investments, so no advice regime applies, but overdraft or credit features would bring in consumer credit rules.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Check that the new account accepts PayPal transfers and international payments. Some app banks limit incoming SWIFT.
- **Sources:**

### Student loan repayment facts people get wrong

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Graduates with English or Welsh Plan 1, 2, 4 or 5 or Postgraduate loans, especially self-employed graduates.
- **What it is:** You repay 9% of income above your plan's threshold (6% for postgrad), not of the balance. For the self-employed it is collected through Self Assessment on whole-year income, and arrives as a lump sum with the January tax bill. Unpaid balances are written off after a fixed term (Plan 2: 30 years; Plan 5: 40 years; not verified this session). So many Plan 2 borrowers never repay in full, and overpaying can be money thrown away.
- **Value:** annual repayment = 9% × (income − threshold).
  - Thresholds for 2026/27: Plan 1 £26,900; Plan 2 £29,385; Plan 4 £33,795; Plan 5 £25,000; Postgraduate £21,000.
  - At £40k profit: Plan 2 pays £955/yr; Plan 5 pays £1,350/yr; Plan 1 pays £1,179/yr.
  - Plan 2 interest is up to 4.1% at or below £29,385, rising to 6% at £52,885+.
- **Time to money:** immediate (stop overpaying; budget for January)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** 31 Jan (paid with Self Assessment)
- **Traps and caveats:** Payments on account do not include student loan (not verified this session), so January is bigger than expected. Voluntary overpayments are not refundable. Reported plan to freeze the Plan 2 threshold from April 2027: not verified, check.
- **Why people miss it:** They treat it like a normal debt and overpay, or are blindsided by the self-assessed lump sum.
- **What we may say:** Repayment and write-off projections are information. Not regulated.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** If you move abroad you must tell the Student Loans Company and pay overseas thresholds. Foreign-client income in the UK counts normally.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/repaying-your-student-loan/what-you-pay (thresholds, rates, interest, self-employed via SA), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (thresholds, rates); not verified this session (write-off terms, payments-on-account exclusion, 2027 freeze)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone who used hire purchase or PCP to buy a car, motorbike, van or campervan between 6 Apr 2007 and 1 Nov 2024, where commission was not disclosed. Leasing (PCH) is excluded. The FCA estimates 37% of agreements (about 12.1m) qualify.
- **What it is:**
  - The FCA's industry-wide scheme requires lenders to contact eligible customers and pay compensation. The average is about £830 per agreement.
  - Parts of the scheme are suspended pending a court hearing set for 16 Feb – 3 Mar 2027. Payments are expected from 2027 if it is upheld.

### Overdrafts and credit card minimum payments

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone in an arranged overdraft or carrying a credit card balance.
- **What it is:** Since 2020 banks must charge one simple interest rate on personal overdrafts. Typical high-street rates are about 35–40% EAR, far above credit cards. Paying only the credit card minimum (often interest plus 1% of the balance) stretches a debt over many years.
- **Value:** interest saved = balance × (overdraft rate − cheaper rate). Example: £1,500 overdraft all year at 39.9% EAR ≈ £600. Moved to a 0% balance-transfer card (fee about 3%, not verified) it costs about £45. Saving ≈ £555.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural (refilling the overdraft)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Balance-transfer cards need a credit check, and the 0% period ends. Under FCA persistent-debt rules (not verified this session), lenders must intervene after 18 months of mostly-interest payments.
  - Freelancers using an overdraft to smooth irregular PayPal payouts pay the most. An income buffer in savings is cheaper.
- **Why people miss it:** The overdraft is shown as "available balance" in banking apps.
- **What we may say:** Showing interest costs is fine. Recommending a specific credit product or debt solution can be credit broking or debt counselling, which are FCA-regulated activities. Keep it generic and link to free debt advice (MoneyHelper, StepChange).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Delays in PayPal withdrawals and currency transfers are a common cause of short overdraft use.
- **Sources:**

  - 35–40% EAR range: secondary, low authority (https://www.money.co.uk/guides/coronavirus-overdraft-changes and bank calculators that show placeholders only), Oct 31, 2026

### Cut PayPal cross-border and conversion fees

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** UK freelancers receiving business payments through PayPal, especially from outside the UK/EEA or in foreign currency.
- **What it is:**
  - PayPal's UK business fee for "all other commercial transactions" is 2.9% + £0.30.
  - Payments from senders outside the UK/EEA add 1.99%.
  - Converting a foreign-currency balance costs 3% above the base exchange rate.
  - These fees stack, so a USD client payment can lose about 8% before it reaches your bank.
- **Value:** fees = amount × (2.9% + cross-border % + 3% if converted) + £0.30. Example: a US client pays the equivalent of £2,000 in USD → 2.9% + 1.99% = £97.80 + £0.30, plus 3% conversion (£60) ≈ £158 (7.9%). On £20k a year of such income that is about £1,580. Options:
  - Invoice by bank transfer to a GBP or multi-currency account.
  - Hold USD and convert elsewhere.
  - Price the fee into your rate.
- **Time to money:** immediate (next invoice)
- **Risk:** low (clients may prefer PayPal; chargeback protection differs)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Never ask clients to send business payments as "friends and family". It breaches PayPal terms and removes protections. Fees on refunds may not be returned. The fees are deductible business expenses either way.
- **Why people miss it:** The fee is netted off silently, and the conversion spread does not show up as a line item.
- **What we may say:** Showing the user's actual fee totals and the mechanics of alternatives is information. If we recommend a specific payment provider, that is not FCA investment advice, but payment-services promotions and any commission must be disclosed.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This is the core move for this user.

### UK regulatory line: guidance vs regulated advice (FCA)

Not a move. These are the rules for the app's wording.

- **Regulated "advice on investments"** (RAO art. 53; FCA PERG 8.28) is a recommendation to a person, in their capacity as an investor, about buying, selling or holding a *particular* investment (a fund, share, personal pension, LISA or S&S ISA product). Facts, rules, generic comparisons and calculators on the user's own numbers are "guidance/information". Saying "you should put £4,000 into Vanguard's LISA" crosses the line. PERG text could not be fetched (page rendered empty); this summary is not verified this session.
- **Targeted support** (FCA PS25/22) has been live since 6 Apr 2026. Authorised firms with the permission may give ready-made suggestions to groups of customers with shared circumstances (pensions and investments only) without a full advice process. An unauthorised app cannot use it. The FCA's consolidated advice/guidance boundary rewrite was not confirmed as published (secondary: Freshfields, Freeths).
- **Other regulated lines to avoid:** credit broking (introducing users to lenders for a fee), debt counselling (advising a specific person how to deal with specific debts), and claims management (referring to car-finance claims firms).
- **MoneyHelper** (free, government-backed) and Pension Wise are safe referral points.

## Germany

### Summary: four highest-value moves for a ~€45k freelancer

1. Context: at €45,000 profit (Gewinn), after health and care insurance deductions, the marginal income tax rate is roughly 30% (estimate; check with the BMF calculator at bmf-steuerrechner.de). Each €1 of deduction is worth about 30 cents, more with Kirchensteuer.
2. **Move 1: Stop the PayPal cross-border leak.** For a business payment from a non-EEA client in foreign currency, PayPal Germany charges 2.99% + €0.39 + 1.99% (international) + 3% (conversion): about 8%. UK clients add 1.29% instead of 1.99%.
3. → On €20k a year of such income that is about €1,600. Invoice by SEPA/IBAN or a multi-currency account where possible, and book every fee as a Betriebsausgabe.
4. **Move 2: Künstlersozialkasse (KSK), if your work is creative or journalistic** (design, illustration, writing, photography, music; not pure programming). KSK members pay about half of statutory health, care and pension contributions, and the KSK covers the rest.
5. → On €45k that is worth roughly €4,000–5,000 a year versus self-paying health insurance (estimate; verify current rates).
6. **Move 3: Retirement deductions.** Rürup (Basisrente) contributions are 100% deductible up to €30,826 in 2026 (secondary): €3,000 saves about €900 tax. From 1 Jan 2027, the new Altersvorsorgedepot is open to self-employed people for the first time: €1,800 a year earns up to €540 Zulage (30%), plus any further tax benefit.
7. **Move 4: Tax-free investing basics.** File a Freistellungsauftrag (€1,000 Sparer-Pauschbetrag, worth up to €264 a year) and run an ETF-Sparplan, where the 2026 Basiszins of 3.20% means a small Vorabpauschale in January 2027 that the allowance usually covers.
8. → Also claim the Homeoffice deductions (€6/day up to €1,260, or the €1,260 Jahrespauschale if home is your main workplace).
9. Check if your clients are foreign businesses: B2B services to them are usually not taxable in Germany, so they should not count toward the €25,000/€100,000 Kleinunternehmer limits. But as a Kleinunternehmer you still owe reverse-charge VAT on foreign software and ads you buy, with no refund.
10. Leaks: average Dispo is 10.99% (up to 15.39%, Stiftung Warentest, May 2026). Watch Restschuldversicherung on loans, old Kapitallebensversicherungen, and Bausparverträge used as savings accounts. Regulatory line: the app may inform and calculate. Personal recommendations of specific financial instruments are Anlageberatung (§ 2 Abs. 8 Nr. 10 WpHG), and individual tax advice is reserved for Steuerberater.

### Freistellungsauftrag (Sparer-Pauschbetrag €1,000 / €2,000)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Everyone in Germany with interest, dividends or fund income, including children and students.
- **What it is:** The first €1,000 a year of investment income (€2,000 for jointly assessed couples) is tax-free. To use it automatically you file a Freistellungsauftrag with each bank or broker and split the €1,000 across them. Without one, the bank withholds 25% Abgeltungsteuer + 5.5% Soli (+ Kirchensteuer) and you have to reclaim it through Anlage KAP.
  - Students with very low total income can request a Nichtveranlagungs-Bescheinigung (NV) so no tax is withheld at all (not verified this session).
- **Value:** tax saved = min(€1,000, capital income) × 26.375% (more with Kirchensteuer). Example: €12,000 in Tagesgeld at 2.5% = €300 interest. Without a Freistellungsauftrag, €79.13 is withheld. With one, €0.
- **Time to money:** immediate (next interest payment)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** any time. Allocate before year-end interest and dividend dates.
- **Traps and caveats:** Allocations above €1,000 across all banks are illegal (the BZSt sees them). Unused allocations at one bank do not transfer automatically. Reallocate when you open new accounts (bonus offers!). If your personal rate is below 25%, claim the Günstigerprüfung in your return.
- **Why people miss it:** They open neobank accounts and never set it. New accounts default to €0.
- **What we may say:** Showing the allocation and the maths is information. No regulated activity.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None (foreign-broker income must be declared in Anlage KAP; no automatic withholding).
- **Sources:**
  - § 20 Abs. 9 EStG https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__20.html (€1,000 / €2,000), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (amounts); not verified this session (26.375% combined rate, NV details)

### ETF-Sparplan and the Vorabpauschale (Basiszins 2026: 3.20%)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Residents who can invest monthly (from €1–25 at most brokers) for 10+ years.
- **What it is:** A Sparplan buys an index ETF automatically each month. For accumulating funds, Germany taxes a notional "Vorabpauschale" each January: start-of-year value × Basiszins × 0.7, capped at the actual gain. Equity ETFs are 30% tax-exempt (Teilfreistellung). The tax is debited from your clearing account in early January.
- **Value:** Vorabpauschale for 2026 = value on 1 Jan 2026 × 3.20% × 0.7 = 2.24% of value (if the fund rose at least that much).
  - Example: €10,000 on 1 Jan 2026 → €224 Vorabpauschale → €156.80 taxable after the 30% Teilfreistellung → €0 tax if covered by the Freistellungsauftrag, otherwise about €41.
  - Long-term wealth comes from market returns, not the tax rule.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** an evening, then ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** the Vorabpauschale for 2026 is charged early January 2027. Keep cash in the Verrechnungskonto or the broker may sell shares.
- **Traps and caveats:** High-fee active funds and bank "Fondssparpläne" with front-end loads. Taxes paid via the Vorabpauschale are credited at sale, so they are not lost. Kirchensteuer applies on top if you are a church member.
- **Why people miss it:** Fear of investing, and confusion and fear about the January debit.
- **What we may say:** Explain mechanics and compute the Vorabpauschale on the user's holdings. Recommending a particular ETF to this user is Anlageberatung (licensable) under § 2 Abs. 8 Nr. 10 WpHG.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None (German broker). Foreign brokers do not withhold tax, so the user must declare it.
- **Sources:**
  - BMF letter of 13 Jan 2026 (Basiszins zum 2. ### Rürup pension (Basisrente) for the self-employed

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Especially self-employed people with no statutory pension insurance and a high marginal rate who want a locked-in lifelong annuity.
- **What it is:** Contributions to a certified Basisrente are 100% deductible as Sonderausgaben (since 2023), up to a shared ceiling of €30,826 for 2026 (€61,652 for couples). Employees must subtract their statutory pension contributions from this ceiling. Self-employed people without statutory contributions can use nearly all of it. In return the money is locked until at least 62, paid only as a lifelong annuity, cannot be sold or inherited freely, and the annuity is taxed later.
- **Value:** tax saved now = contribution × marginal rate (+Soli/KiSt where applicable). Example: €3,000 at about 30% → about €900 refund. Later the annuity is taxable (the taxable share rises toward 100% for future retirees; not verified this session).
- **Time to money:** months (tax refund); decades (annuity)
- **Risk:** market and behavioural (locked; costs; no lump sum)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** 31 Dec for that tax year
- **Traps and caveats:** Insurance-wrapped Rürup contracts often have high acquisition and running costs. Fund- or ETF-based low-cost versions exist (not verified this session). You cannot stop and access the money. Compare with the 2027 Altersvorsorgedepot and a flexible ETF depot.
- **Why people miss it:** It is sold badly or not at all, and young freelancers don't know they have the full deduction room.
- **What we may say:** Explain and compute deduction value. Recommending a specific Rürup product is insurance or investment intermediation (§ 34d/§ 34f GewO) or Anlageberatung. Do not.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None while German tax-resident.
- **Sources:**
  - €30,826 / €61,652 for 2026: secondary (insurer and tax pages) https://www.ruv.de/altersvorsorge/basisrente-sonderausgabenabzug, https://pandotax.de/gruender/rurup-rente-selbststaendige-2026/, Oct 31, 2026. Statute is § 10 Abs. 3 EStG (not opened).
- **Confidence:** secondary only (2026 ceiling; some pages still show 29,344 for 2025)

### Riester today vs the new Altersvorsorgedepot (from 1 Jan 2027)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:**
  - Today (2026): Riester is mainly for people paying into the statutory pension: employees, civil servants, and KSK-insured artists (not verified this session). It is not open to most freelancers except indirectly through an eligible spouse.
  - From 1 Jan 2027, the new subsidised Altersvorsorgedepot is also open to self-employed people and Versorgungswerk members (Altersvorsorgereformgesetz: Bundestag 27 Mar 2026, Bundesrat 8 May 2026).
- **What it is:** A subsidised retirement depot that can be invested in ETFs without a capital guarantee. Standard products are capped at 1% cost a year.
  - State Zulage: 50 cents per € on the first €360, plus 25 cents per € up to €1,800 a year, so a maximum €540.
  - Kinderzulage: €1 per € up to €300 per child.
  - Starter bonus: €200 if you start before 25.
  - Minimum contribution €120 a year. Payout from 65–70 as a withdrawal plan or annuity. Existing Riester contracts keep their protection, and switching costs at most €150 in the first five years.
- **Value:** Zulage = 0.5 × min(contribution, 360) + 0.25 × (min(contribution, 1,800) − 360). Example: €1,800 a year → €540 Zulage (30% instant return). €360 a year → €180 (50%). Additional tax deduction via Günstigerprüfung: details unknown, verify.
- **Time to money:** years (Zulage credited after the year; payout at retirement)
- **Risk:** market (no guarantee); behavioural (subsidy clawback if withdrawn early; not verified)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** from 1 Jan 2027. Self-employed people reportedly must have filed a tax return to qualify (secondary).
- **Traps and caveats:** It is not live yet. Product launch timing and providers are unknown. Some sources say not every self-employed type qualifies (income under § 15 or § 18 Abs. 1 Nr. 1–3 EStG). Old Riester insurance contracts are often high-cost, but cancelling one triggers clawback of past subsidies.
- **Why people miss it:** Freelancers were told "Riester isn't for you" and won't notice the change.
- **What we may say:** Explain the rule and compute the Zulage. Recommending a specific provider or the fund inside is Anlageberatung or intermediation.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None while German tax-resident.
- **Sources:**
  - https://finanztip.de/riester-reform/ (Zulage formula, €540, €300 child, €200 under-25, €120 minimum, 1% cost cap, €150 switch, eligible groups incl. self-employed, start 1 Jan 2027), Oct 31, 2026
  - Legislative dates: secondary https://www.fonds-super-markt.de/neuigkeiten/artikel/altersvorsorgedepot-kommt-ab-2027-bundesrat-stimmt-am-08-05-2026-zu/, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** secondary only (Finanztip, well-sourced; statute in BGBl not opened)

### Vermögenswirksame Leistungen (VL) and Arbeitnehmersparzulage

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** Employees, trainees (Azubis) and civil servants, including a freelancer with a part-time employed job. Pure freelancers get nothing.
- **What it is:** Many employers (often by collective agreement) pay up to €40 a month extra into a VL contract in your name. If your taxable income is low enough, the state adds Arbeitnehmersparzulage: 20% on up to €400 a year in an equity fund, or 9% on up to €470 in a Bausparvertrag (amounts not verified this session).
- **Value:** employer VL (up to €480/yr) + 20% × up to €400 (= €80/yr) if taxable income ≤ €40,000 single / €80,000 joint for the fund route (secondary).
- **Time to money:** years (7-year lock for fund VL; not verified)
- **Risk:** market (fund VL)
- **Effort:** minutes (form to employer)
- **Deadline or season:** apply for the Zulage in your tax return (Anlage VL)
- **Traps and caveats:** VL linked to expensive insurance or bank funds. Since 2024 VL no longer counts for Wohnungsbauprämie (secondary).
- **Why people miss it:** Young employees never ask HR. The Zulage needs a tick in the return.
- **What we may say:** Information. Product choice is Anlageberatung.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Not applicable to freelance income.
- **Sources:**
  - Income limits: secondary https://www.vergleich.de/wohnungsbaupraemie.html and https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/vermoegenswirksame-leistungen-6-hoehe-der-arbeitnehmersparzulage-und-einkommensgrenzen-HI13063512.html (search summary), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** secondary only

### Wohnungsbauprämie (and Bausparen misuse)

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** Residents 16+ with taxable income up to €35,000 single / €70,000 joint, saving in a Bausparvertrag. Self-employed people can use it too.
- **What it is:** The state adds 10% on up to €700 a year (€1,400 for couples) saved into a Bausparvertrag, so at most €70 or €140 a year. It is generally paid out only if the money is used for housing (rules not verified this session).
- **Value:** 10% × min(savings, €700). Example: €700 a year for 7 years → €490 premium.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** behavioural (fees can exceed the premium)
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** apply annually by the deadline on the Bausparkasse's form (not verified)
- **Traps and caveats:** Bausparverträge usually charge an Abschlussgebühr (commonly 1–1.6% of the contract sum; anecdotal) and pay low savings interest. Using one as a "savings account" or signing a contract sum far above need is a classic leak. Do not combine with VL on the same savings.
- **Why people miss it:** Too small to market. Conversely, people are sold oversized contracts by bank advisers.
- **What we may say:** Show the premium maths against fees. Recommending a specific Bauspar tariff is credit or insurance intermediation; avoid.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.vergleich.de/wohnungsbaupraemie.html (10%, €700/€1,400, €35,000/€70,000 zvE), secondary, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** secondary only (statute WoPG not opened; fee range anecdotal)

### Kleinunternehmerregelung (2026 limits) and reverse charge with foreign clients

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** German-resident freelancers with small German-taxable turnover, and anyone invoicing foreign businesses.
- **What it is:**
  - Under § 19 UStG you charge no VAT if your Gesamtumsatz was ≤ €25,000 net last year and stays ≤ €100,000 this year. Gesamtumsatz is turnover *steuerbar* in Germany.
  - B2B services to businesses abroad are usually taxable where the customer is (EU: reverse charge, "Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers"; non-EU: not taxable in Germany). So they likely do not count toward these limits (statute reading; confirm with a Steuerberater).
  - Trap: even as a Kleinunternehmer you are liable under § 13b Abs. 5 UStG for German VAT on services you *buy* from foreign businesses (Google/Meta ads, Adobe, Figma, AWS, OpenAI), and you cannot deduct it.
- **Value:**
  - Kleinunternehmer status saves 19% for German consumer clients: €20,000 of B2C sales means no €3,800 VAT to add or absorb.
  - The reverse-charge leak is 19% of foreign purchases: €100 a month of Adobe + ads → €228 a year owed with no refund. Opting into normal VAT may be cheaper if foreign-business purchases are large and clients are businesses.
- **Time to money:** immediate (pricing)
- **Risk:** low (misclassification brings back-tax and interest)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** watch the €100,000 running total. The exemption ends from the transaction that crosses it (secondary).
  - Waiving § 19 binds you for 5 calendar years. The waiver is due by end of February of the second following year.
- **Traps and caveats:** EU B2B services need your USt-IdNr on the invoice and possibly a Zusammenfassende Meldung. Whether a Kleinunternehmer must file a ZM after the 2025 reform is not verified. Kleinunternehmer still file an annual VAT return when they owe § 13b tax (secondary). PayPal's own fees are usually VAT-exempt financial services, so no reverse charge (not verified this session).
- **Why people miss it:** They count all PayPal receipts toward €25k and lose the exemption. Or they never self-assess VAT on foreign SaaS bills.
- **What we may say:** Track turnover and classify likely categories. Individual tax advice is reserved for Steuerberater (StBerG; not verified this session), so present results as "estimate, have it checked".
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Core row: foreign B2B clients are probably outside the € limits; foreign consumers (B2C) may not be.
- **Sources:**
  - § 19 UStG https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__19.html (€25,000 / €100,000; Gesamtumsatz = steuerbare Umsätze; 5-year waiver), Oct 31, 2026
  - § 13b UStG https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__13b.html (Abs. 1, 2 Nr. 1, 5: recipient owes tax if Unternehmer), Oct 31, 2026
  - Kleinunternehmer and § 13b practice: secondary https://norman.finance/de/blog/reverse-charge, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (thresholds, § 13b liability); secondary only (filing practice, € 100k timing)

### Betriebsausgaben and Werbungskosten freelancers miss

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed (Betriebsausgaben in the EÜR) and employees (Werbungskosten above the €1,230 Pauschbetrag).
- **What it is:** Commonly missed deductions:
  - **Homeoffice-Tagespauschale:** €6 per day worked mainly at home, max €1,260 a year.
  - **Arbeitszimmer:** if home is the centre of your work, a €1,260 Jahrespauschale or actual costs (§ 4 Abs. 5 Nr. 6b/6c EStG).
  - **Equipment:** computers and software can be written off in one year (BMF practice; not verified); small assets up to €800 net are written off immediately (not verified).
  - **Running costs:** phone and internet share, software subscriptions, PayPal and conversion fees, KSK and professional memberships, bank fees.
  - **Commuting** (employees): Entfernungspauschale €0.38 from the first km since 2026 (secondary).
- **Value:** tax saved = expenses × marginal rate (plus Gewerbesteuer effect for Gewerbetreibende only). Example: €1,260 home office + €1,200 PayPal fees + €900 software/phone share = €3,360 × about 30% ≈ €1,000.
- **Time to money:** months (refund or lower prepayments)
- **Risk:** low (documentation)
- **Effort:** an evening, then ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** tax return deadline. For 2025 returns without an adviser: 31 Jul 2026 (passed; not verified this session).
- **Traps and caveats:** The Tagespauschale and Jahrespauschale cannot be stacked for the same costs. Private use shares must be split.
- **Why people miss it:** No bookkeeping, PayPal fees netted invisibly, and fear that a home office "triggers audits".
- **What we may say:** Categorise and estimate. No individual tax advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Book foreign-currency income at the EUR amount received (Zuflussprinzip; not verified). PayPal fees are deductible.
- **Sources:**
  - § 4 Abs. 5 Satz 1 Nr. 6b, 6c EStG https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__4.html (€1,260; €6/day, max €1,260), Oct 31, 2026
  - 2026 figures (€1,230; €0.38 from km 1; Grundfreibetrag €12,348): secondary https://www.vlh.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/fuenf-aenderungen-fuer-2026-mit-blick-auf-die-einkommensteuer.html (BMF Monatsbericht Feb 2026 returned HTTP 503), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (home office); secondary only (2026 commuting, Pauschbetrag); not verified this session (GWG €800, 1-year IT depreciation)

### Kirchensteuer

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Members of a church that levies tax (Catholic, Protestant, some others).
- **What it is:** Church tax is 8% of your income tax in Bayern and Baden-Württemberg and 9% elsewhere. It applies to capital gains tax too, and banks deduct it automatically. Leaving at the Standesamt (or court) costs €25–100 depending on the municipality and ends the liability from the following month or the one after. Kappung (a cap at 2.75–4% of taxable income) mainly helps high earners and in some states needs an application.
- **Value:** saving = income tax × 8% or 9% + the same on Abgeltungsteuer. Example: if your income tax is €7,000 (illustrative), 9% = €630 a year.
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** none (financial)
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** any time
- **Traps and caveats:** This is a personal and religious decision. Present it neutrally. Some church employers require membership. Church tax paid is deductible as Sonderausgaben, so the net saving is a little lower.
- **Why people miss it:** Young people baptised as infants often don't realise they pay it until their first payslip or tax bill.
- **What we may say:** Show the amount. Never recommend leaving a church. Present facts only.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** None
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.finanztip.de/kirchensteuer/ (8%/9%, Kapitalerträge, €25–100 fee, Kappung 2.75–4%; Stand 08 Jul 2026), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** secondary only (Finanztip; well-established)

### Girokonto and Depot bonuses

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** Residents 18+ willing to open a new account and meet salary or usage conditions.
- **What it is:** Banks pay new customers €100–300 for opening a current account (sometimes with a depot) and meeting conditions such as monthly incoming payments or card use.
- **Value:** bonus − any account fees during the minimum period. Examples:

  - ING up to €300 (giro + depot; Aug 2026, current status unclear).

- **Deadline or season:** offers change monthly
- **Traps and caveats:** Salary-receipt conditions are hard for freelancers paid irregularly via PayPal. Check whether "Gehaltseingang" requires a minimum amount. Opening many accounts adds SCHUFA entries (not usually score-damaging; not verified). Fees after a free period.

- **What we may say:** List offers with an affiliate disclosure. Accounts are not Finanzinstrumente, but depot openings bundled with fund promos approach Anlagevermittlung if we earn commission (verify).

### Tagesgeld instead of Sparbuch or Girokonto

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone holding emergency savings or tax reserves, which matters for freelancers who must hold back money for Vorauszahlungen.
- **What it is:** Tagesgeld (instant-access savings) pays far more than most Sparbücher or current accounts. In early October 2026 the best unrestricted Tagesgeld paid 2.50%, with new-customer promotions up to about 4% for 3–4 months. The average across banks (Biallo index, €10k) is about 1.83%. ECB deposit rate: 2.50% since 16 Sep 2026; the 29 Oct 2026 decision was not verified. Deposits are protected to €100,000 per person per bank.
- **Value:** extra interest = balance × (Tagesgeld rate − current rate). Example: €10,000 tax reserve at 2.50% vs 0% on Girokonto → €250 a year (within the Sparer-Pauschbetrag if a Freistellungsauftrag is set). Sparbuch rate: unknown, verify at your bank.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** none (within €100k; check the country of the deposit guarantee)
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** promos end. Rates follow the ECB.
- **Traps and caveats:** Teaser rates drop to low regular rates after 3–4 months. Neobroker "interest" is sometimes a money-market fund, which is not deposit-protected. Watch for fake emails impersonating Raisin (BaFin warning per test.de).
- **Why people miss it:** Inertia. The Sparbuch is a family habit.
- **What we may say:** Rate comparison is information. Deposits are not Finanzinstrumente, so this is not Anlageberatung, but money-market ETFs are.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Keep tax reserves in EUR. FX swings on USD balances are a hidden risk.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.test.de/Zinsen-Neues-am-Zinsmarkt-6217294-0/ (30 Sep 2026: best 2.50%, promos to ~4%, €100k protection), Oct 31, 2026
  - Biallo index 1.831% and ECB 2.50% from 16 Sep 2026: secondary https://www.biallo.de/tagesgeld/news/tagesgeld-vor-der-ezb-sitzung-bis-4-50-prozent/, Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (test.de is Stiftung Warentest); secondary only (ECB rate, average); Sparbuch: unknown

### Künstlersozialkasse (KSK) for creative freelancers

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed artists and publicists (e.g. designers, illustrators, writers, journalists, photographers, musicians) with at least €3,900 a year of income from that work. Career starters are exempt from the minimum for their first 3 years (not verified this session). Software developers generally do not qualify (not verified).
- **What it is:** KSK members are compulsorily insured in statutory pension, health and care insurance, but pay only about half the contributions. The other half comes from a levy on companies that buy creative work (Künstlersozialabgabe: 4.9% in 2026, 5.0% in 2027) and a federal subsidy.
- **Value:** saving ≈ 50% of statutory health + care + pension contributions on your Arbeitseinkommen, compared with paying them fully yourself. Rough example on €45,000 with self-paid GKV and PV at about 20% (estimate): about €9,000 a year self-paid, versus about half via the KSK, plus pension rights you would not otherwise have. Exact rates are not verified this session.
- **Time to money:** months (application processing)
- **Risk:** low
- **Effort:** a weekend (application with portfolio and evidence)
- **Deadline or season:** apply when you start. Membership is compulsory if you qualify.
- **Traps and caveats:** You must estimate income each year, and underestimates are corrected. Mixed work (design + coding) may be rejected. Private health insurance members have special rules.
- **Why people miss it:** They think "artist" means painters only. Many designers and content writers qualify.
- **What we may say:** An eligibility checklist is information. Social-law advice in individual cases is regulated (RDG; not verified this session), so frame it as "check with KSK".
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign clients don't pay the levy, but the member still gets the subsidised rate (not verified this session).
- **Sources:**
  - 2026 levy 4.9%: secondary https://www.tk.de/firmenkunden/service/fachthemen/fachthema-beitraege/kuenstlersozialabgabe-2026-2201218 and DRV press note https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Ueber-uns-und-Presse/Presse/Meldungen/2025/250724-kuenstlersozialabgabe-sinkt-ab-2026 (search only), Oct 31, 2026

- **Confidence:** secondary only (levy rates); not verified this session (€3,900, half-contribution share, starter exemption)

### Rentenkonto gaps (Kontenklärung) and voluntary pension contributions

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Everyone with a German pension record, especially people who studied, trained, spent time abroad or raised children. Self-employed people not compulsorily insured can pay voluntary contributions.
- **What it is:** Your Deutsche Rentenversicherung (DRV) record often lacks periods that count toward your pension: school or university from age 17, training, child-rearing and periods abroad. A Kontenklärung (form V0100 or online eAntrag) fixes them, typically in 3–4 months. Self-employed people can pay voluntary contributions to avoid gaps; 2026 range €112.16–1,571.70 a month (secondary).
- **Value:** extra pension from corrected periods (varies, unknown per user). Context: missing child-rearing years are often worth over €100 a month of pension per child (not verified this session).
- **Time to money:** months (correction); years (pension)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** reply within 6 months to a DRV Kontenklärung letter. Proofs get harder to find with age.
- **Traps and caveats:** Some gaps cannot be closed (e.g. study periods beyond limits). Voluntary contributions are only worth it in specific cases (e.g. reaching the 5-year minimum or the 35-year threshold).
- **Why people miss it:** Young people ignore the Renteninformation letter.
- **What we may say:** Checklist and links. Individual pension calculations are Rentenberatung (regulated under RDG; not verified), so refer users to DRV advice (free).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Years working abroad may count under social-security agreements (EU/UK and others). Flag for DRV.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Ueber-uns-und-Presse/Presse/Meldungen/2026/260706-rentenkonto-online-klaeren.html (6 Jul 2026: V0100, eAntrag, school/study from 17, abroad, child-rearing, 3–4 months), Oct 31, 2026
  - Voluntary contribution range: secondary (search summary), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (process); secondary only (contribution range)

### Family and housing benefits: Kindergeld, Kinderzuschlag, Wohngeld

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:**
  - Kindergeld: parents. A young adult up to 25 in training or study can have it paid directly (Abzweigung; not verified this session).
  - Kinderzuschlag: low-income working parents.
  - Wohngeld: low-income renters and owners not on Bürgergeld, including freelancers with low or uneven income.
- **What it is:**
  - Kindergeld is €259 per child per month in 2026.
  - Kinderzuschlag is up to €297 per child per month (unchanged from 2025).
  - Wohngeld is a rent subsidy from your municipality. Non-take-up is believed to be widespread. One 2026 Bremen parliamentary document cites 12 studies estimating 40–88% non-take-up across benefit types. No reliable Wohngeld-specific rate was found.
- **Value:** Kindergeld €3,108 a year per child. Kinderzuschlag up to €3,564 a year per child. Wohngeld depends on rent, income and household: use the official Wohngeldrechner (unknown, verify).
- **Time to money:** weeks to months (Kinderzuschlag can be backdated up to 6 months; not verified)
- **Risk:** none (report income changes)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** Kindergeld is backdated only 6 months (not verified this session)
- **Traps and caveats:** Freelancers must estimate income. Wohngeld is recalculated, so expect paybacks if you underestimate. Wohngeld and Bürgergeld are mutually exclusive.
- **Why people miss it:** Stigma, complexity, and thinking self-employed people are excluded.
- **What we may say:** An eligibility screener with the user's numbers. Individual claims advice is legal or social advice; refer to the Verbraucherzentrale or Sozialberatung.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign-client income counts as income in EUR.
- **Sources:**

### BAföG

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Students and some pupils, depending on parental and own income and assets. Many who assume their parents earn "too much" actually qualify.
- **What it is:** State study support: half grant, half interest-free loan (repayment capped; not verified this session). The current maximum is €992 a month, with €380 of that for housing. A planned increase of the housing allowance to €440 was postponed from WS 2026/27 to SoSe 2027 (around 1 Apr 2027), and the law was still pending in parliament in autumn 2026.
- **Value:** up to €992 × 12 = €11,904 a year at the maximum. Individual amount: use the official BAföG calculator.
- **Time to money:** months (apply early; can be backdated to the month of application only; not verified)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** a weekend (forms)
- **Deadline or season:** apply before or at the start of semester
- **Traps and caveats:** Own freelance income above the allowance reduces BAföG (Minijob-level allowances; not verified). Change of subject rules.
- **Why people miss it:** "My parents earn too much" without checking.
- **What we may say:** Screener plus links.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Freelance income counts. Report it.
- **Sources:**
  - Secondary: https://www.studierenplus.de/bafoeg/bafoeg-2026 and GEW press releases (search summary), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** secondary only

### Dispo (overdraft) interest

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone using their Dispositionskredit.
- **What it is:** In May 2026, German banks charged on average 10.99% interest on overdrafts, with the most expensive at 15.39% (Stiftung Warentest, over 1,000 banks).
- **Value:** interest = average overdrawn balance × rate. Example: €2,000 overdrawn all year at 10.99% = €220. At 15.39% = €308. A Rahmenkredit or Ratenkredit is usually cheaper (rates not verified).
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Freelancers waiting on PayPal payouts or client payments often slide into Dispo. A buffer in Tagesgeld is cheaper. Banks must offer advice after 6 months of heavy Dispo use (§ 504a BGB; not verified this session).
- **Why people miss it:** The Dispo looks like "my money" in the banking app.
- **What we may say:** Show the cost. Recommending a specific loan is Darlehensvermittlung (§ 34c GewO), so avoid it.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** PayPal withdrawal delays and FX timing cause short Dispo use.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.test.de/Dispozinsen-6095517-0/ (Stand 18 May 2026: average 10.99%, max 15.39%, 1,000+ banks), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### Cut PayPal cross-border and conversion fees (Germany)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** German freelancers paid through PayPal by clients outside the EEA or in foreign currency.
- **What it is:**
  - Standard business fee: 2.99% + €0.39.
  - International surcharge: EEA +0%, UK +1.29%, other markets +1.99%.
  - Currency conversion: 3% above the base rate.
  - A USD payment from a US client can lose about 8%.
- **Value:** fees = amount × (2.99% + surcharge + 3% if converted) + €0.39. Example: €2,000-equivalent USD payment → 4.98% (€99.60) + €0.39 + 3% conversion (€60) ≈ €160 (8%). On €20k a year of foreign income ≈ €1,600. Options: SEPA invoice for EEA clients (no surcharge), a multi-currency account for USD/GBP, or pricing the fee in.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** low (client convenience, different buyer/seller protection)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Don't use "Freunde und Familie" for business payments (it breaks terms and has no protection). Fees remain deductible Betriebsausgaben.
- **Why people miss it:** Fees are netted off. Conversion isn't itemised as a fee.
- **What we may say:** Show the user's own fee history and alternatives. Not Anlageberatung. Disclose any provider commission.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Core row.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.paypal.com/de/webapps/mpp/merchant-fees (2.99% + €0.39; +0%/1.29%/1.99%; 3% conversion), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### German regulatory line: information vs Anlageberatung (and tax advice)

Not a move. These are the rules for the app's wording.

- **Anlageberatung** (§ 2 Abs. 8 Satz 1 Nr. 10 WpHG; same wording in KWG § 1 Abs. 1a Satz 2 Nr. 1a and WpIG) is a *personal recommendation* (per Art. 9 Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/565) about transactions in *specific financial instruments*. It counts as personal when it is based on the investor's personal circumstances or presented as suitable for them.
  - Recommendations given only through information channels or to the public are excluded.
  - BaFin's guidance (Merkblatt, Feb 2025) says a personal recommendation generally requires direct exchange with the client, and it does not matter who initiated it.
  - Investment advice needs a licence (KWG/WpIG), or a § 34f/§ 34h GewO registration for fund-only intermediaries.
- **Not financial instruments:** Tagesgeld, Girokonto, Bausparen. Insurance (Rürup, Lebensversicherung) falls under § 34d/§ 34e GewO, and loans under § 34c/§ 34i GewO.
- **Tax:** individual tax advice in specific cases is reserved for Steuerberater and Lohnsteuerhilfevereine (StBerG §§ 2–5; not verified this session). Present tax figures as general information or estimates.
- **Safe referral points:** Verbraucherzentrale (fee-based independent advice), DRV free pension advice.
- **Sources:**
  - § 2 WpHG https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/wphg/__2.html, Oct 31, 2026
  - BaFin Merkblatt "Hinweise zum Tatbestand der Anlageberatung" (Feb 2025) via https://www.bafin.de/ref/19619304 and the Bundesbank copy https://bundesbank.de/resource/blob/598312/f39e5ee8c04f323690cc8ca1b5fdfa26/mL/informationsblatt-zum-tatbestand-der-anlageberatung-data.pdf (search summary), Oct 31, 2026
- **Confidence:** verified primary (WpHG definition); secondary only (BaFin Merkblatt wording); not verified this session (StBerG reservation, GewO categories)

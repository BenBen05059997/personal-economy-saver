## India: summary (freelancer earning about ₹12 lakh a year, paid via PayPal)

1. **Declare under presumptive taxation and stay on the new regime.** Under 44ADA (section 58 of the Income-tax Act 2025 from FY 2026-27), only 50% of receipts counts as profit, so ₹12L of receipts becomes ₹6L of income. That is ₹0 tax after the 87A rebate.
2. You can earn up to about **₹24L in gross receipts and still owe ₹0 income tax**, because a deemed profit of ₹12L or less is fully rebated. You keep no books and need no audit. At ₹18L of receipts, that saves about ₹1.25L compared with reporting a ₹16L actual profit.
3. **Find out what PayPal costs you.** The fee is 4.4% + $0.30 per payment, plus a 3.0% conversion spread, which comes to roughly 7.3% of every USD invoice. On ₹12L received, that is about ₹85–95k a year. Comparing collection channels is the largest "get paid better" lever.
4. **Download the free weekly digital FIRA** (available from Feb 2026) and make sure your PayPal purpose code is correct. Below ₹20L you need no GST registration. Above ₹20L, register and file an LUT so exports stay at 0% GST.
5. **Never revolve a credit card balance.** Indian cards charge up to 3.75% a month (45% a year). Check every lending app against the RBI's digital-lending-app directory.
6. Put long-term savings in **PPF**: 7.1% (Q3 FY27, 2026), tax-free in both regimes, sovereign-backed, ₹1.5L a year cap. ₹1.5L a year for 15 years grows to about ₹40.7L.
7. At this income, **old-regime deductions (80C/ELSS, 80CCD(1B) NPS, 80D, 80GG) save ₹0**, because tax is already zero. Do not buy ULIPs or endowment plans "for tax saving".
8. If your tax due is ₹10,000 or more, pay advance tax in **one instalment by Mar 15**. Missing it costs about 1% a month in interest under 234B/234C.
9. Spend 15 minutes on **unclaimed money**: RBI UDGAM for bank deposits (₹72,454 cr in the DEA Fund as of Jan 2026), IEPF for old dividends and shares, and EPFO for PF left at a past job.
10. **Legal line:** showing a user's own numbers and rules is information. Recommending specific securities for a fee, or tying up with unregistered "finfluencers", falls under SEBI's Investment Adviser regime.

## India: moves

### Presumptive taxation for professionals (Section 44ADA; Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Indian residents (individuals, or partnership firms that are not LLPs) earning from a "specified profession". That covers technical consultancy, IT and software, design, film and arts, and similar work; check your activity against the list. Gross receipts must be ₹50L or less, or **₹75L or less if cash receipts are 5% of receipts or less**, which a PayPal-only freelancer meets automatically.
- **What it is:** You don't calculate your real profit. The law treats 50% of everything you received as your profit and taxes only that. You may declare more than 50%, but not less unless you keep books and get an audit. This also removes the obligation to maintain detailed books of accounts.
- **Value:** Taxable profit is gross receipts × 50%, and tax is computed on that. Inputs: gross receipts, other income, regime. *Example (FY 2026-27, new regime):* ₹12,00,000 received gives ₹6,00,000 of income. Slab tax is ₹10,000, which the 87A rebate cancels, so **tax is ₹0**. At ₹24,00,000 received, income is ₹12,00,000 and slab tax of ₹60,000 is fully rebated, so tax is still ₹0. At ₹18,00,000 received *without* 44ADA (say ₹2L of actual expenses, ₹16L profit), new-regime tax is ₹1,20,000 + 4% cess = **₹1,24,800**. With 44ADA it is ₹0.
- **Time to money:** months (the saving shows when you file the ITR, usually by July 31)
- **Risk:** none (it is a statutory option). The risk is behavioural: misreporting receipts.
- **Effort:** an evening (file ITR-4 Sugam instead of ITR-3)
- **Deadline or season:** chosen each year in the return. The ITR due date for non-audit cases is usually Jul 31. Advance tax is due in one instalment by Mar 15 (see the advance tax row).
- **Traps and caveats:** If you opt out and declare less than 50%, you may need books and an audit, and the old rule bars you from re-entering for 5 years. Check how section 58 restates this. The ₹75L limit depends on cash receipts staying at or below 5%. The 87A rebate does not cover special-rate income such as STCG on equity, so a trading side-income can create tax. Sales of goods (dropshipping, print-on-demand) fall under 44AD (6% or 8% deemed profit), not 44ADA. "Consultants" who sell a course or a template business fall into a grey area. Section numbers changed on Apr 1, 2026, and blog guides still quote the old ones.
- **Why people miss it:** People assume presumptive tax is for shopkeepers, or a CA files them under ITR-3 with an expense list. Few people realise how the 50% deeming interacts with the ₹12L rebate.
- **What we may say:** We may show the user's receipts, the deemed profit and the resulting slab tax as arithmetic. Choosing the ITR form or confirming that a given activity is a "specified profession" is tax advice for a CA. Say "check with a CA".
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Receipts are counted in INR at the rate actually credited (the FIRA and bank credit show it). PayPal credits arrive by bank transfer, so they count as non-cash for the 5% test. Gross receipts are what the client paid, before PayPal's fee. Whether fees reduce "gross receipts" is unclear, so use the gross amount to be safe.
- **Sources:**

  - Presumptive FAQs: https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Pages/faqs.aspx?k=FAQs+on+Tax+on+Presumptive+Taxation+Scheme (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Mapping to section 58 of the 2025 Act (secondary): https://taxbymanish.blogspot.com/2026/02/section-58-of-income-tax-act-2025.html and the ICAI comparison via https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/icai-releases-updated-income-tax-act-2025-with-comparison-of-sections-with-old-act-1433538 (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Budget 2026 left slabs unchanged; the new Act applies from Apr 1, 2026: https://scroll.in/latest/1090410/scroll_in (Oct 31, 2026)
  - 87A to section 156 mapping: https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025-section-numbers-old-vs-new (Oct 31, 2026)

### New vs old tax regime for a freelancer

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** every resident individual filer. Business and professional income earners can switch regimes only in limited cases (under the 1961 Act, they could move back to the new regime once). Check how the 2025 Act restates this.
- **What it is:** India has two tax systems. The **new regime** (the default) has lower slabs and a rebate that makes income up to ₹12L tax-free, but almost no deductions. The **old regime** has higher slabs but allows deductions such as 80C (PPF/ELSS), 80CCD(1B) (NPS), 80D (health insurance), 80GG (rent) and home-loan interest.
- **Value:** Compute tax both ways and pick the lower. **(2026)** New regime slabs for FY 2026-27 are unchanged from FY 2025-26: 0–4L nil, 4–8L 5%, 8–12L 10%, 12–16L 15%, 16–20L 20%, 20–24L 25%, over 24L 30%. The rebate is up to ₹60,000 when income is ₹12L or less. *Example:* ₹12L receipts under 44ADA gives ₹6L of income. New regime: ₹0. Old regime: ₹6L minus ₹2.25L of deductions = ₹3.75L, which is ₹0 after the old-regime 87A rebate. A tie at ₹0, so the simpler new regime wins. At ₹30L receipts (₹15L income), the new regime gives about ₹1,09,200 including cess. The old regime with ₹2.25L of deductions gives about ₹2,02,800 including cess. The new regime wins by about ₹93k.
- **Time to money:** months (at filing)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** at ITR filing
- **Traps and caveats:** Salaried people get a ₹75,000 standard deduction in the new regime; freelancers don't. Agents sell "tax-saving" products (ULIPs, endowment plans, ELSS) to people whose tax is already ₹0. Switching rules for business income are stricter than for salaried people.
- **Why people miss it:** Tax advice online is written for salaried employees. Freelancers rarely run the 44ADA plus new-regime combination.
- **What we may say:** We may show both computations with the user's own numbers. Choosing on their behalf is tax advice; leave the final choice with them or their CA.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign income is taxed in India like domestic income for a resident. If a foreign client withheld tax, a foreign tax credit (Form 67) may apply, which is rare for PayPal payments.
- **Sources:**
  - Slabs and rebate (secondary, multiple): https://www.canarahsbclife.com/blog/budget-news/budget-2026-income-tax-slabs-changes-salaried-self-employed and https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/no-change-income-tax-slabs-union-budget-2026-243.htm (Oct 31, 2026)
  - CBDT AY 2026-27 validation rule (80CCD(1B) must be 0 under the new regime, which shows Chapter VI-A deductions are unavailable): https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/sites/default/files/2026-05/CBDT_e-Filing_ITR%201_Validation%20Rules_AY%202026-27.pdf (Oct 31, 2026)
- **Confidence:** secondary only for the FY 2026-27 slab table (consistent news reports). Verified primary that deductions are blocked in the new regime (CBDT validation rules). The examples are our arithmetic.

### Advance tax in one instalment (Section 211/234B/234C; s.424-425 of the 2025 Act, numbering unverified)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** any freelancer whose estimated tax for the year is **₹10,000 or more**. With income from 44ADA, the whole amount is due in one instalment.
- **What it is:** Instead of paying all your tax when you file, you pay it during the year. A 44ADA filer pays 100% by **Mar 15**. If you miss it, you pay simple interest of about 1% a month on the shortfall.
- **Value:** Avoided interest is roughly the shortfall × 1% × the months late (234B), plus 234C for the missed instalment. *Example:* ₹1,09,200 of tax (₹30L receipts) paid at filing in July instead of Mar 15 costs about ₹1,09,200 × 1% × 4 months = **₹4,368** in 234B interest, plus 234C of about ₹1,092. At ₹12L receipts the tax is ₹0, so nothing is due.
- **Time to money:** days (it avoids a charge)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (Challan 280 online)
- **Deadline or season:** Mar 15. Amounts paid by Mar 31 still count as advance tax.
- **Traps and caveats:** One large invoice in February can push you over ₹10,000 unexpectedly. Interest is computed per month or part of a month, so one day late counts as a full month.
- **Why people miss it:** Freelancers think tax is paid only when filing, as it is for a salaried person whose employer deducts TDS.
- **What we may say:** We may show the estimated liability and the date. Saying "you must pay ₹X" is fine as arithmetic, with a "verify with a CA" note.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** There is no TDS on foreign receipts, so a foreign-paid freelancer usually owes the full amount as advance tax. Domestic clients would have deducted TDS.
- **Sources:**
  - Income Tax Dept (44AD/44ADA filers pay 100% by Mar 15; payments by Mar 31 count): https://wm.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/tax-on-presumptive-basis-in-case-of-certain-businesses (Oct 31, 2026)
  - ₹10,000 threshold: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/who-is-not-required-to-pay-advance-tax- (Oct 31, 2026)
  - 234B mapped to s.424 (conflicting; one table says s.447): https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025-section-numbers-old-vs-new (Oct 31, 2026)
- **Confidence:** verified primary for the single instalment and the ₹10k threshold. The 1%/month rate is long-standing statute but was **not re-verified** on a 2026 primary page. The new section numbers are secondary and conflicting.

### GST: zero-rated export of services, the LUT and the ₹20 lakh threshold

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** freelancers whose clients are outside India. Registration is not compulsory while aggregate turnover stays under **₹20L** (₹10L in special-category states). Exports count towards that turnover.
- **What it is:** Services exported abroad are "zero-rated", so no GST is charged to the foreign client. Below ₹20L you don't need to register at all. Once you register (compulsory above ₹20L), you file a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT, form RFD-11)** each financial year so you can export without paying IGST upfront and claiming it back.
- **Value:** It avoids 18% IGST on export invoices (cash-flow and refund pain) and lets you claim back GST paid on inputs (laptop, software, and possibly PayPal's fees if GST is charged on them). *Example:* at ₹25L of exports a year without an LUT, you would have to pay ₹4.5L of IGST and wait for a refund. With an LUT you pay ₹0.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** low (compliance)
- **Effort:** a weekend for registration, then ongoing monthly or quarterly returns
- **Deadline or season:** the LUT is filed for each FY, ideally before Apr 1. It is valid for that FY only.
- **Traps and caveats:** It only counts as "export of services" if the client is outside India, the place of supply is outside India, payment comes in **convertible foreign exchange**, and the client isn't your own establishment. The FIRA proves the foreign-exchange condition. Selling to Indian clients too may require GST on those invoices. Registering voluntarily brings monthly or quarterly return obligations, with late fees even for nil returns.
- **Why people miss it:** Freelancers assume foreign income means "18% GST", or register too early and then miss nil returns.
- **What we may say:** We may say what the threshold is and the user's running turnover against it. Whether a specific service qualifies as an export is a question for a CA.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This is central for them. Keep invoices in USD, the FIRA for each receipt, and the PayPal transaction IDs.
- **Sources:**
  - Secondary: https://www.incorpx.io/blog/gst-export-of-services-freelancers-india and https://www.taxaj.com/learn/gst-applicability-tax-rates-freelancers-india-2026/ (says the threshold is still ₹20L for FY 2026-27) (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Primary to verify: CBIC Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax (exempts inter-state service suppliers below the threshold from compulsory registration) at https://cbic-gst.gov.in. Not opened in this session.
- **Confidence:** secondary only (multiple consistent practitioner guides). The CBIC primary text was not opened. Verify that the threshold is unchanged in 2026.

### Get the free weekly FIRA from PayPal and set the right purpose code

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** anyone in India receiving international payments into a PayPal India account
- **What it is:** A **FIRA (Foreign Inward Remittance Advice, formerly FIRC)** is the bank-grade proof that foreign money came in. You need it for GST export proof, for income-tax queries, and sometimes for loans or visas. **(2026)** From Feb 2026 PayPal generates a **free weekly digital FIRA**. Before you can withdraw, PayPal India also requires a **purpose code**, a 5-character RBI code describing why the money came in.
- **Value:** It avoids PayPal's *custom* FIRA fee of **₹100 per transaction + 18% GST** (₹2,000 + GST for bulk requests over 20 transactions). *Example:* 50 client payments a year requested later as custom FIRAs cost ₹2,000 + GST = ₹2,360. Downloading the weekly ones costs ₹0. It also prevents a GST refund or export claim from failing for lack of proof.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (Reports > Tax > FIRA > Download)
- **Deadline or season:** PayPal's report history covers only the **last 12 months**, so download monthly.
- **Traps and caveats:** A wrong purpose code (for example P0104 vs P0802 for software services) can make your bank or the GST officer question whether this was an export. Users report it is hard to change codes on past FIRAs. One FIRA covers one UTR (bank credit reference), which can bundle several PayPal payments.
- **Why people miss it:** The documents sit in a reports menu, and most people only learn about them when a CA or GST officer asks.
- **What we may say:** We may explain the document and where to download it. The right purpose code for a given activity is the user's call, made with their bank or CA.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies only to people paid from abroad.
- **Sources:**
  - PayPal India FIRA page (weekly from Feb 2026, free; custom ₹100/txn + 18% GST; 12-month history): https://www.paypal.com/in/webapps/mpp/firc-certificate (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Purpose code needed before withdrawal: https://www.paypal.com/in/cshelp/article/what-is-the-purpose-code-help978 (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Purpose-code mismatch anecdotes: https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Getting-started-with-PayPal/Purpose-Code-for-Export-of-Goods-in-India/td-p/1347043 (Oct 31, 2026)
- **Confidence:** verified primary (PayPal). The purpose-code problems are anecdotal.

### Know the real cost of PayPal in India (and compare channels)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** Indian freelancers paid via PayPal by foreign clients
- **What it is:** For an international commercial payment, PayPal India charges **4.4% + a fixed fee** ($0.30 for USD, €0.35, £0.20). When it converts the money to INR it adds a **3.0% spread** above its base rate. Balances in India are swept to your linked bank account automatically. You can't hold USD, so every receipt gets converted.
- **Value:** The cost is the invoice × 4.4% + the fixed fee, plus the remainder × 3%. *Example ($1,000 invoice, ₹88/USD assumed):* the fee is $44.30, leaving $955.70. At the base rate that would be ₹84,102, but with the 3% spread you receive about ₹81,579. Compared with the mid-market ₹88,000, you lose **about ₹6,421 (7.3%)**. On ₹12L of annual receipts, the same rate means about **₹85,000–95,000 a year**. Some other licensed cross-border collection routes (direct SWIFT to an Indian bank, or RBI-authorised payment aggregators for cross-border, PA-CB) advertise much lower all-in costs. Compare the landed INR for the same invoice.
- **Time to money:** weeks (switching a client's payment route)
- **Risk:** low (the alternatives carry counterparty and dispute-protection trade-offs)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Secondary sources say 18% GST is charged on PayPal's fee as well; that is not shown on PayPal's page, so verify. Buyer-protection chargebacks can claw money back. A failed withdrawal costs 250 in bank return fees. Do not ask clients to send "friends and family" payments to dodge fees: that breaks PayPal's terms and gives you no FIRA or export proof. Comparison sites for alternatives are often run by those competitors.
- **Why people miss it:** The 3% spread is built into the exchange rate rather than shown as a fee line.
- **What we may say:** We may show the user's own effective cost from their transactions and the published fee schedules. We should present alternatives neutrally, not as an endorsement or affiliate push.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies only to people paid from abroad. US clients may also ask for **IRS Form W-8BEN**, which establishes that you are not a US person. Filing it avoids the platform or client wrongly applying US backup withholding. Whether services performed in India count as US-source income is a separate question; generally they are not, but verify.
- **Sources:**
  - PayPal India merchant fees (4.40% + fixed fee; 3.0% conversion on received payments; no withdrawal fee without conversion; page "last updated 28 March 2024"): https://www.paypal.com/in/webapps/mpp/merchant-fees (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Daily auto-sweep to the bank (secondary): https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/withdraw-money-from-paypal-india (Oct 31, 2026)

### Public Provident Fund (PPF)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** resident Indians, one account per person (at a post office or bank). It is open to freelancers, who cannot join EPF.
- **What it is:** A 15-year government savings account. You can deposit ₹500 to ₹1.5L a year. The interest is set by the government each quarter and is **tax-free in both regimes**. Contributions qualify for 80C only in the old regime. Partial withdrawals and loans are allowed after some years.
- **Value:** Future value of annual deposits = deposit × [((1+r)^n − 1)/r] × (1+r). **(2026)** r = 7.1% for Oct–Dec 2026. *Example:* ₹1,50,000 a year for 15 years at a constant 7.1% grows to about **₹40.7L**, of which ₹22.5L is your deposits, all tax-free. Compare a bank FD at 7% taxed at 10–30%.
- **Time to money:** years (15-year lock, with partial access from year 7)
- **Risk:** low (sovereign). The rate can be cut quarterly.
- **Effort:** minutes (online via net-banking)
- **Deadline or season:** deposit before the **5th of the month** (interest is computed on the lowest balance between the 5th and month-end) and before Mar 31 to count for the year
- **Traps and caveats:** Skipping the ₹500 minimum makes the account "discontinued", with a penalty to revive it. Locking up money you need for an emergency is a mistake, so keep 3–6 months of expenses liquid first. The rate has been frozen for about 9–10 quarters, and it is reset quarterly.
- **Why people miss it:** It looks old-fashioned. People who don't have 80C to claim (new regime) wrongly think it is useless, but the tax-free interest still applies.
- **What we may say:** We may show the rate, the rules and the user's projected balance. Saying "you should put ₹X in PPF" crosses into personal advice. PPF is not a SEBI security, but keep it neutral anyway.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing changes while you are resident. Non-residents cannot open a new PPF account.
- **Sources:**
  - Rate 7.1% for Oct–Dec 2026 (Finance Ministry notification as reported Sep 30, 2026): https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/small-savings-scheme-interest-rates-unchanged-for-q3-fy27-check-ppf-scss-ssy-rates-558892-2026-09-30 (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Primary to verify: Ministry of Finance (DEA) small savings notification at https://dea.gov.in. Not opened.
- **Confidence:** secondary only for the Q3 FY27 rate (multiple consistent outlets). The rules are long-standing. The example is our arithmetic.

### National Pension System (NPS) extra deduction (Section 80CCD(1B); s.124 of the 2025 Act, sub-clause unverified)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Indian citizens aged 18–70. The tax benefit applies **only in the old regime**.
- **What it is:** A low-cost, market-linked retirement account regulated by PFRDA. Self-employed people can deduct contributions up to 20% of gross income within the ₹1.5L 80CCE cap, **plus an extra ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B)** that nothing else unlocks. Most of the money is locked until 60.
- **Value:** Tax saved = min(contribution, ₹50,000) × your marginal old-regime rate × 1.04. *Example:* a freelancer on the old regime in the 30% bracket saves ₹50,000 × 31.2% = **₹15,600 a year**. *At ₹12L receipts under 44ADA the tax is already ₹0, so the saving is ₹0.*
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market (equity, corporate bond and government bond mix)
- **Effort:** an evening (eNPS online)
- **Deadline or season:** contribute by Mar 31 to count for the year
- **Traps and caveats:** It does nothing in the new regime, which is most freelancers. Exit rules have historically required part of the corpus to buy an annuity, and PFRDA has been changing exit rules for non-government subscribers. **Current 2026 exit percentages: unknown, verify.** Sales pitches stress the tax deduction and leave out the lock-in.
- **Why people miss it:** The ₹50k figure is famous, but people don't check whether their regime allows it.
- **What we may say:** We may show the rule and whether it applies to the user's chosen regime. Recommending an NPS fund manager or allocation is advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing changes. NRIs can also hold NPS.
- **Sources:**
  - CBDT AY 2026-27 ITR validation rules (80CCD(1B) must be 0 under the new regime): https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/sites/default/files/2026-05/CBDT_e-Filing_ITR%201_Validation%20Rules_AY%202026-27.pdf (Oct 31, 2026)
  - PFRDA tax guide (20% of gross income for self-employed; extra ₹50k): https://www.pfrda.org.in/documents/33652/217954/A+Guide+to+Tax+Savings+Through+NPS.pdf (Oct 31, 2026)
- **Confidence:** verified primary (CBDT, PFRDA). The 2026 exit and annuity rules were not verified.

### Equity-Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) mutual funds

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** any investor. The 80C deduction applies only in the old regime.
- **What it is:** An equity mutual fund with a 3-year lock-in that counts towards 80C's ₹1.5L. Outside the tax angle it is simply a diversified equity fund.
- **Value:** In the old regime, tax saved = contribution (within the 80C room left after PPF/EPF/insurance) × marginal rate. *Example:* ₹1.5L at 31.2% saves ₹46,800. Under 44ADA at ₹12L in the new regime, the saving is ₹0. For new-regime users, ELSS has no advantage over a regular index fund and has a lock-in.
- **Time to money:** years (3-year lock per SIP instalment)
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** Mar 31 (the January–March "tax-saving rush")
- **Traps and caveats:** Gains are taxed as LTCG on exit. Each monthly SIP instalment has its own 3-year lock. "Regular" plans sold through agents carry higher expense ratios than "direct" plans.
- **Why people miss it:** The reverse problem: people buy ELSS in the Q4 rush without checking that they get any deduction at all.
- **What we may say:** We may describe the category and the lock-in. Naming a specific ELSS fund as "good for you" is investment advice under SEBI's IA rules.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:**

### Old-regime-only deductions: health insurance (80D, s.126 of the 2025 Act) and rent without HRA (80GG)

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** filers on the **old regime only**. 80GG is for people who receive no HRA (that is, self-employed) and who, along with their spouse and minor child, own no house in the city where they live.

## Philippines: summary (freelancer earning about ₱900,000 a year, paid via PayPal)

1. **Choose the 8% flat income tax.** It is (₱900k − ₱250k) × 8% = **₱52,000**. Graduated rates with the 40% OSD plus 3% percentage tax come to about **₱77,500**, so you save about ₱25,500 a year. You elect it when you register or on your first quarterly return of the year, and it can't be changed mid-year.
2. **Actually register with BIR** (Form 1901 via ORUS). The ₱500 annual registration fee was abolished in Jan 2024. File 1701Q quarterly (the **next deadline is Nov 15, 2026** for Q3) and 1701A by Apr 15. Foreign clients withhold nothing, so you self-assess.
3. **Know the PayPal cost:** 4.4% + $0.30 per payment, plus a 3% conversion spread, which is about 7.3% of every USD invoice (about ₱65k a year on ₱900k). Always withdraw **more than ₱7,000** to avoid the ₱50 fee, and compare other channels by the pesos that land.
4. **Pag-IBIG MP2:** the **2025 dividend was 7.12%**, declared Feb 2026. It is tax-free and government-guaranteed, with a 5-year term. A bank deposit at 4% nets about 3.2% after the 20% final tax. Saving ₱2,000 a month for 5 years earns about ₱13–14k more in MP2.
5. **SSS at the top bracket:** MSC ₱35,000 costs ₱5,250 a month at 15%. The portion above ₱20k MSC goes to the MySSS Pension Booster (formerly WISP). You need 120 monthly contributions for a lifetime pension; past jobs count, so top them up.
6. A Pag-IBIG membership (₱200 a month minimum) unlocks **housing loans at 3% / 4.5% / 5.75%**, with promo rates on applications until **Dec 31, 2026**. These are far below bank rates.
7. Keep the emergency fund in a **PDIC-insured** digital bank (cover is **₱1M per depositor per bank** since Mar 15, 2025). Remember interest is taxed at a flat 20% under CMEPA from Jul 2025. E-wallet balances are not deposits.
8. **Stop the leaks:** credit cards are capped at 3% a month (36% a year). Small online loans have been capped at **12% a month effective** since **Apr 1, 2026**, with total cost at most 100% of principal. 5-6 lending runs at about 20% a month.
9. **Unclaimed money:** dormant bank balances escheat to the Treasury after 10 years. Pag-IBIG savings become claimable at 20 years / 240 contributions. Check old SSS and Pag-IBIG records from past jobs.
10. **Legal line:** information on the user's own numbers is fine. Soliciting or recommending specific investments without an SEC licence exposes "influencers and promoters" to SRC liability. SEC advisories name them explicitly.

### 8% flat income tax option for self-employed / professionals (8% gross receipts tax option)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** purely self-employed individuals or professionals with gross sales or receipts plus other non-operating income of **₱3,000,000 or less**, who are not VAT-registered. Mixed-income earners (salary plus freelancing) may use 8% on the business part but **don't get the ₱250k deduction**.
- **What it is:** Instead of graduated income tax *and* 3% percentage tax, you pay 8% on gross receipts above ₱250,000, and that's it. No expense records are needed to compute the tax. You must elect it each year; otherwise you default back to graduated rates.
- **Value:** Under 8%, tax = (gross − ₱250,000) × 8%. Under graduated rates (TRAIN schedule from 2023): 0 up to ₱250k; 15% of ₱250k–400k; ₱22,500 + 20% of ₱400k–800k; ₱102,500 + 25% of ₱800k–2M; and so on. That is applied to net income (gross × 60% if using the Optional Standard Deduction), plus **3% percentage tax** on gross. *Example (₱900,000):* 8% option = **₱52,000**. Graduated with OSD: net ₱540,000 gives ₱22,500 + ₱28,000 = ₱50,500, plus percentage tax ₱27,000 = **₱77,500**. **8% saves about ₱25,500.** It wins unless real expenses are very high.
- **Time to money:** months (savings show at each quarterly filing)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (tick the box on Form 1901 or on the first 1701Q of the year)
- **Deadline or season:** elect at registration or on the **Q1 1701Q (May 15)**. The choice is irrevocable for that year.
- **Traps and caveats:** If you forget to elect, you're on graduated rates plus 3% percentage tax for the year. Crossing ₱3M mid-year forces VAT registration and drops 8%. The 3% percentage-tax rate (1% from Jul 2020 to Jun 2023 under CREATE) is now back at 3%. Whether foreign-client receipts of a non-VAT freelancer are subject to percentage tax is generally treated as yes in BIR forms, but **verify** the treatment of export receipts.
- **Why people miss it:** Unregistered freelancers never see the choice. Registered ones get defaulted by an accountant who prefers itemised deductions.
- **What we may say:** We may show both computations with the user's receipts. Choosing for them, or filing, is tax practice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Gross receipts are the peso value of what the client paid, before PayPal fees. Use the conversion on the date received and keep PayPal statements as support.
- **Sources:**
  - BIR RR 8-2018 digest (₱3M VAT threshold; 8% on excess over ₱250k; election rules; irrevocable for the year): https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/Digest%20RR%208-2018_copy.pdf (Oct 31, 2026)
  - RMO 23-2018 digest: https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RMO_No.%2023-2018_digest.pdf (Oct 31, 2026)

### BIR registration realities for online freelancers

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** every Filipino earning from freelance work, including payments through PayPal, Upwork or Fiverr
- **What it is:** You register as a self-employed or professional taxpayer (BIR Form 1901, online via ORUS), register books of accounts, issue invoices, and file 1701Q quarterly and 1701A annually. **(2024)** The EOPT Act (RA 11976) **abolished the ₱500 annual registration fee** (not collected from Jan 22, 2024). The Certificate of Registration (Form 2303) no longer needs annual renewal. EOPT also created "micro" taxpayers (gross sales under ₱3M) with lighter penalties.
- **Value:** It avoids penalties and unlocks proof of income (the ITR) for visas, loans and Pag-IBIG housing loans. *Example:* without an ITR, banks and Pag-IBIG often can't verify freelance income, so a housing loan may be impossible (anecdotal).
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** low
- **Effort:** a weekend (registration), then ongoing (4 filings a year)
- **Deadline or season:** 1701Q is due **May 15, Aug 15 and Nov 15**. 1701A is due **Apr 15**. Register within 30 days of starting business (rule as commonly cited; verify).
- **Traps and caveats:** Late-filing penalties (surcharge plus interest) are cut for micro and small taxpayers under EOPT. The exact current rates are **unknown, verify**. You must keep your registered address current (EOPT moves). Fixers sell "BIR registration packages". Registering doesn't by itself trigger VAT below ₱3M.
- **Why people miss it:** People think income from foreign clients isn't taxable in the Philippines. It is, for residents.
- **What we may say:** We may share the checklist, the deadlines and the user's quarterly numbers. Preparing or filing returns for them is tax practice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** No one withholds tax for you (no Form 2307 from foreign clients), so you pay the full liability via the quarterly returns.

### Pag-IBIG MP2 savings (Modified Pag-IBIG II)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** active Pag-IBIG members, including self-employed and voluntary members
- **What it is:** A voluntary 5-year savings program run by the government's housing fund. It pays an annual dividend that is **tax-free** and backed by a government guarantee. You can choose to receive dividends yearly or compounded at maturity. **(2026)** The 2025 dividend was **7.12%** (declared Feb 27, 2026; 2024 was 7.10%).
- **Value:** Annual compounding at the declared rate, with no tax. *Example:* ₱2,000 a month for 5 years (₱120,000) at a constant 7.12% grows to about **₱143,600**, of which about ₱23,600 is tax-free dividends. The same at a 4% digital bank nets 3.2% after the 20% final tax, about ₱129,900. **MP2 is about ₱13,700 better.** Rates are declared yearly and not guaranteed in advance.
- **Time to money:** years (5-year maturity)
- **Risk:** low (fund performance sets the dividend; the guarantee is on principal and dividends per the Pag-IBIG law)
- **Effort:** minutes (enrol online, pay via apps)
- **Deadline or season:** the dividend is credited after the yearly declaration (Feb–Mar)

Other early exits reportedly forfeit part of the dividends (**exact penalty: verify**). Scams use "MP2 agents" to collect payments, so pay only through official channels. Membership must be active, meaning regular ₱200/month savings are paid.

### SSS voluntary / self-employed contributions and the MySSS Pension Booster (formerly WISP)

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** self-employed freelancers (mandatory coverage) and anyone with past SSS contributions
- **What it is:** SSS gives you a lifetime pension (after 120 monthly contributions, at 60 or older), plus sickness, maternity, disability, death and unemployment benefits and salary loans. **(2025–2026)** The contribution rate has been **15%** since Jan 2025, on a Monthly Salary Credit (MSC) between ₱5,000 and **₱35,000** for self-employed members. The part of your contribution above ₱20,000 MSC goes into the **MySSS Pension Booster** (formerly WISP), an individual savings account. There is also a voluntary booster (formerly WISP Plus).
- **Value:** Contribution = MSC × 15%. *Example:* a ₱75,000/month freelancer pays at the ₱35,000 cap, which is **₱5,250 a month**. Of that, ₱2,250 (15% of the ₱15,000 above ₱20k) builds the Booster account. The voluntary fund reportedly returned **6.83% for 2025**, with the 1% fee waived 2025–2028 (secondary). A higher MSC raises the pension formula.
- **Time to money:** years (pension); weeks (loans and benefits)
- **Risk:** low (state-backed; the Booster returns vary)
- **Effort:** minutes (generate a PRN, pay via app)
- **Deadline or season:** self-employed and voluntary payments are due by month or quarter per SSS schedules; late payments can't be made retroactively for some months (**verify current rules**)
- **Traps and caveats:** With fewer than 120 contributions at 60, you get a lump sum instead of a pension. Self-employed people need SSS registration matching their actual earnings. Whether self-employed members pay the EC charge is disputed between sources (**verify**).
- **Why people miss it:** People stop paying when they leave employment and think the money is lost.
- **What we may say:** We may show contribution tables and the user's months-to-120 count. Benefit-amount projections should carry a "verify with SSS" note.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** You pay as self-employed (not as an OFW) if you live in the Philippines.

### Personal Equity and Retirement Account (PERA)

- **Group:** invest & cash

- **What it is:** **(2026)** Pag-IBIG offers housing loans at **3%** (socialized housing under Expanded 4PH, fixed for the first 5 years, extendible), **4.5%** (above the socialized ceiling up to ₱2.5M, fixed 3 years) and **5.75%** (above ₱2.5M up to ₱10M, fixed 3 years). These are promo rates for applications received **until Dec 31, 2026**. Pag-IBIG also offers a Multi-Purpose Loan (cash loan) against your savings.
- **Value:** Monthly payment = P × r / (1 − (1+r)^−n). *Example:* a ₱2M, 30-year loan at 4.5% costs about **₱10,134 a month**. At an assumed 7.5% bank rate it costs about ₱13,985, so the Pag-IBIG loan is about ₱3,850 a month cheaper while the fixed period lasts. It reprices after the fixed period.

- **What we may say:** We may show the rates and the user's payment arithmetic. Arranging or brokering loans is lending intermediation.

### Stay away from 5-6 lending

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** anyone short of cash who is approached by informal lenders
- **What it is:** "Borrow 5, pay 6": about **20% interest per month**, usually collected daily or weekly by informal lenders.
- **Value:** Avoided interest ≈ principal × 20% per cycle. *Example:* ₱10,000 borrowed means ₱12,000 repaid within the month, an annualised cost well above 200%. An SSS salary loan or Pag-IBIG MPL costs a small fraction of that.
- **Time to money:** days (cost avoided)
- **Risk:** behavioural, harassment
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Daily collection hides the true rate. Lenders may be unregistered. Legal status is disputed: courts can strike down "unconscionable" interest, but there is no general usury ceiling.
- **Why people miss it:** It is fast, needs no paperwork, and the lender is a neighbour.
- **What we may say:** We may show the user's true cost arithmetic and government loan alternatives as information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** PayPal holds and slow client payments drive people to it. Keep a buffer.
- **Sources:**
  - PIDS "5-6 under scrutiny": https://pids.gov.ph/details/5-6-under-scrutiny (Oct 31, 2026)
  - UC Irvine IMTFI research: https://imtfi.uci.edu/research/2015/dula_grego_2015.php (Oct 31, 2026)
- **Confidence:** secondary (a government think-tank plus academic research) for the 20%/month mechanics

### Online lending apps: know the 2026 caps

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** anyone using online lending apps (OLAs) or small cash loans from lending or financing companies
- **What it is:** **(2026)** Under **SEC Memorandum Circular No. 14, s. 2025, effective Apr 1, 2026**, unsecured loans of **₱10,000 or less** with terms of **up to 4 months** from lending and financing companies have these caps: a **6% per month nominal interest**, a **12% per month effective rate** (down from 15%, including fees), late penalties at most 5% a month, and **total cost no more than 100% of the principal**. Lenders face fines starting at ₱50,000.
- **Value:** Recover overcharges and refuse illegal charges. *Example:* ₱10,000 for one month can cost at most ₱1,200 in interest plus fees. An app deducting ₱2,500 "service fees" upfront exceeds the cap.
- **Time to money:** weeks (complaint)
- **Risk:** behavioural, harassment
- **Effort:** an evening (SEC complaint)
- **Deadline or season:** applies to loans entered, renewed or restructured from Apr 1, 2026
- **Traps and caveats:** Unregistered OLAs ignore the rules entirely, so check that the company and app are SEC-registered. Debt-shaming through your contacts is prohibited. Bigger or longer loans fall outside these caps. Oversight may shift towards the BSP (an SEC proposal, Apr 2026).
- **Why people miss it:** Borrowers don't know caps exist.
- **What we may say:** We may explain the caps and compute whether the user's loan complies. We may not rank lenders.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** same
- **Sources:**
  - GMA on SEC lowering caps: https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/969232/sec-lowers-interest-rate-caps-for-loans-up-to-p10-000/story/ (Oct 31, 2026)
  - Manila Bulletin (12%/month): https://mb.com.ph/2025/12/11/sec-caps-interest-rates-on-small-loans-at-12-monthly (Oct 31, 2026)
  - eLegal summary: https://elegal.ph/sec-recalibrates-ceilings-of-interest-rates-and-other-fees-charged-by-financing-and-lending-companies/ (Oct 31, 2026)

### Credit cards, BNPL and sangla (pawnshop loans)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** card, BNPL and pawnshop users
- **What it is:** BSP caps credit-card interest on unpaid balances at **3% a month (36% a year)** and cash-advance processing fees at ₱200 (kept since 2023; the industry asked for the caps to be scrapped in Jan 2026, with no change found). BNPL providers that are lending companies fall under SEC rules: late penalties are capped at 5% a month and "stacked" fees are disallowed for small loans. Pawnshop (sangla) loans are BSP-regulated, but their typical monthly rates were **not verified** in this session.
- **Value:** Interest ≈ balance × 3% × months. *Example:* a ₱50,000 card balance revolving costs about **₱1,500 a month** (₱18,000 a year). Paying it from savings earning 3.2% net is a large guaranteed gain.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes (autopay in full)
- **Deadline or season:** each due date
- **Traps and caveats:** Installment plans charge add-on rates that look small monthly but are high annually. Several BNPL lines at once add up quickly. Pawned gold that isn't redeemed by the deadline is auctioned.
- **Why people miss it:** "Minimum due" thinking, and BNPL at checkout feels like it isn't debt.
- **What we may say:** We may show the user's cost arithmetic and the caps.

Before that, dormant accounts may lose interest or incur dormancy fees.
- **Value:** The balance itself, plus fees avoided. *Example:* a forgotten ₱8,000 payroll account from a first job (illustrative).
- **Time to money:** days to weeks (reactivate at the bank)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening (visit the branch with ID)
- **Deadline or season:** before the 10-year mark and escheat proceedings
- **Traps and caveats:** Dormancy fee rules and the BSP dormancy definition were **not verified** (a commonly cited 2-year dormancy figure is unconfirmed). After escheat, recovery is much harder.
- **Why people miss it:** Job changes, and payroll accounts opened by employers.

### Philippines regulatory note: the SEC's line on investment advice

This is a reference row for the product, not a move.

- **The rule:** Selling or offering securities (including "investment contracts") to the public requires SEC registration (SRC Sec. 8). Acting as a broker, dealer or salesman requires a licence (SRC Sec. 28). Investment advisers of investment companies are licensed under the Investment Company Act. **Recent SEC advisories (2025–2026) state that promoters, recruiters and "influencers" who solicit for unregistered schemes may be held liable.** Press reports cite fines of up to ₱5M and up to 21 years' imprisonment. RA 11765 (Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act) adds conduct rules for financial service providers.
- **What we may say:** general financial education. We may show the user's own numbers, run arithmetic, and give published rates and rules with dates.
- **What we may not say without a licence:** recommending specific securities, funds or crypto products, soliciting investments, or taking referral fees for investment products. Insurance product recommendations fall to the Insurance Commission. Lending referrals touch SEC and BSP rules.

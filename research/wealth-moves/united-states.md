# Wealth moves: United States (US residents)

Researched Oct 31, 2026. All dollar figures are USD. "2026" marks a figure set for tax year or calendar year 2026. Where a primary page could not be fetched, the row says so and the confidence field shows it.

## Summary: five highest-value moves for a single $60k freelancer (net profit, no kids, paid via PayPal)

Baseline (federal only, rough): SE tax ≈ $8,480; half of it (≈ $4,240) is deductible, so AGI ≈ $55,760; after the $16,100 standard deduction and the QBI deduction, taxable income ≈ $31,700, which is in the **12% bracket**. Total federal tax ≈ $12,000.
1. **Marketplace Premium Tax Credit, kept under the 400% FPL cliff ($62,600 MAGI for 2026 coverage):** about **$1,600/yr at age 40 and $8,400/yr at age 60** in our illustrative example. Above the cliff the credit is **$0**, and from 2026 excess advance credit has to be paid back in full.
2. **Solo 401(k) or SEP, Roth vs pre-tax chosen deliberately:** $10k pre-tax saves about **$960/yr** in federal tax at this income. $500/mo at an assumed 7% for 35 years grows to about **$900k**. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.
3. **The freelancer deduction stack** (QBI ≈ $950, simplified home office ≈ $350, SE health insurance ≈ $480 on $5k of premiums, HSA ≈ $420): about **$1,500–2,200/yr**.
4. **Bank, brokerage and credit-card sign-up bonuses:** about **$1,000–3,500/yr** before tax. These are anecdotal blogger totals, taxable, and they need clean credit and spare cash.
5. **Cash in a ~4% HYSA or T-bills instead of 0.38% savings, and estimated tax paid on time:** about **$360/yr per $10k** of cash, plus about **$560/yr** of underpayment penalty avoided on a $12k tax bill.
- Stop-the-leak note: a $5,000 card balance at the Fed's 22.36% average costs **about $1,100/yr**. Paying it off beats every move above except #1.
- Biggest things we could not verify are listed at the end of this file. In short: live bonus offers, the 2027 PTC status, the Free File $89k cap, and the 1 Nov 2026 I-bond rate.

## What we may say: education vs regulated advice (US)

- **Federal:** the Investment Advisers Act defines an "investment adviser" as anyone who, for compensation, advises others "as to the value of securities or as to the advisability of investing in, purchasing, or selling securities" (15 U.S.C. §80b-2(a)(11)). The same section excludes publishers of a "bona fide ... financial publication of general and regular circulation". *Lowe v. SEC*, 472 U.S. 181 (1985) read that exclusion as covering impersonal, disinterested content that is not timed to specific clients' situations. Sources: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-472/pdf/USREPORTS-472-181.pdf (opinion, not re-read; summaries checked via https://firstamendment.mtsu.edu/article/lowe-v-securities-and-exchange-commission/); https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/80b-2 (statute, not fetched).
- **Where an app crosses the line:** an app that takes the user's own data and recommends *specific securities or funds* or an allocation for that person is giving personalized advice. Robo-advisers doing this register as investment advisers; see the SEC's 2017 robo-adviser guidance, https://www.sec.gov/investment/im-guidance-2017-02.pdf (not fetched, verify). Firms with under $100M AUM generally register with their **state** instead of the SEC, and states regulate people who "hold out" as financial planners. That is unknown per state; verify with NASAA.
- **Safe zone:** general education (how an HSA works), the user's own arithmetic (what a $10k Solo 401(k) contribution changes on *their* tax estimate), government rates and limits, and category-level cost facts ("index ETFs averaged 0.14%"). Show these as "information, not advice".
- **Avoid:** "buy X fund", "move your money to Bank Y" for a fee or affiliate payment without disclosure, performance promises, and handling client money or trades (broker-dealer / money-transmitter rules).
- **Tax:** general tax information is fine. Preparing returns for pay requires an IRS PTIN. Credit-card and bank-account referrals paid by issuers need clear affiliate disclosure under FTC endorsement rules.
- Confidence: statute and case verified via secondary summaries. State rules: unknown, verify.

## Quick money

### Bank and brokerage account sign-up bonuses

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** US residents with an SSN or ITIN, adults, who can route a deposit (often $500–$5,000 of "direct deposit") to a new account. People who already bank at that institution, or did recently, are usually excluded.
- **What it is:** Banks pay new customers $100–$900 to open a checking or savings account and meet conditions, usually a direct deposit or a balance held for 60–90 days. Brokerages pay a percentage "match" for moving an existing investment account to them. You can do this once per bank every 1–2 years, and the payments add up.
- **Value:** Bonus minus fees paid minus interest lost, then minus income tax at your marginal rate. Inputs: cash available, whether you can redirect a deposit, and your bracket. Example: Chase Total Checking listed $300 for a new account with direct deposit within 90 days on its offer page. The monthly fee is $15, waived with $500+ of electronic deposits per statement period. Net after 12% tax ≈ $264. A brokerage match of 1% on a $20,000 transfer = $200 (Robinhood ran 1–3% personalized matches in 2026, some with a 5-year hold).
- **Time to money:** weeks to about 4 months (most banks pay 2–8 weeks after the conditions are met)
- **Risk:** low (fees, missed conditions, clawback if you leave early)
- **Effort:** an evening per bonus, then ongoing tracking
- **Deadline or season:** each offer has its own expiry. Third-party sites reported the Chase $400 version expired Oct 14, 2026.
- **Traps and caveats:** Bonuses are taxable (1099-INT or 1099-MISC). Some banks pull a hard credit inquiry or a ChexSystems report. Monthly fees can eat the bonus if waivers are missed. Early-closure fees or clawbacks run 90–180 days. Some banks do not count PayPal or Venmo transfers as "direct deposit"; the data points are anecdotal, so verify. Brokerage matches often require a paid tier (e.g., Robinhood Gold) and multi-year holding, and moving out early forfeits the bonus. Scam version: "bonus" offers that ask for your login, or money-mule "deposit this check" schemes.
- **Why people miss it:** Offers are scattered, use coupon links and change monthly. People assume "free money" means a catch.
- **What we may say:** List the offer terms the user can verify and compute the net value with their tax rate. Do not steer users to one bank for undisclosed referral pay.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** PayPal payouts to a bank are usually coded as transfers, not payroll direct deposit, so they may not trigger the bonus (anecdotal). Keep a W-2 or ACH-payroll source in mind.
- **Sources:** Chase Total Checking page (fee and waiver rules), https://www.chase.com/personal/checking/total-checking (checked Oct 31, 2026). The $300 offer is on chase.com's offer page per search snippet; it could not be fetched. Robinhood transfer-bonus terms, https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/2026-Hood-Month-ACAT-Match/ and https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/2026-trust-account-match/ (snippets). Anecdotal annual totals: Travel with Grant, ≈$3,475 from 9 accounts in 2025, https://travelwithgrant.boardingarea.com/?p=30372; Financial Panther, ≈$1,000 from 5 banks in 2025, https://financialpanther.com/back-with-the-bank-bonuses-my-2025-bank-account-bonus-earnings/.
- **Confidence:** Fee and waiver rules verified primary. The bonus amount is secondary (bank page via snippet). Annual totals are anecdotal.

### Credit card sign-up bonuses (and portals / card-linked offers)

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** People with good credit (roughly FICO 670+), no card debt and normal spending that can hit the minimum spend without buying extra. **Not** for anyone carrying a balance.
- **What it is:** Card issuers pay 50,000–100,000 points (or $200–$1,000 cash) when a new cardholder spends a set amount in the first 3 months. Issuers limit how often you can get them. Chase usually declines people who opened 5+ cards from any bank in 24 months (the "5/24" rule, unpublished). Amex limits many bonuses to "once per lifetime" per card. Shopping portals and card-linked offers add a few percent back on purchases you were going to make anyway.
- **Value:** Bonus value minus annual fee minus any interest paid. Inputs: credit score, monthly spend, and whether the balance is paid in full. Example: Chase Sapphire Preferred was reported at 75,000 points after $5,000 spend in 3 months (Oct 2026). At 1¢/point cash value that is $750, minus the $95 annual fee = $655 in year one. Card rewards on personal spending are generally treated as rebates, not income; IRS practice, verify for bank-account bonuses vs card bonuses.
- **Time to money:** 1–4 months
- **Risk:** behavioural (overspending to hit the threshold; any interest at 22% wipes out the bonus)
- **Effort:** an evening, then monthly attention
- **Deadline or season:** offers change often and elevated offers come and go. Plan around big planned expenses (a laptop, taxes paid by card only if the fee is below the reward rate).
- **Traps and caveats:** A hard inquiry usually costs "less than five points" for most people (myFICO) and fades within about a year. New accounts lower your average account age, so avoid applying within ~6–12 months of a mortgage or car loan. Chase Sapphire bonus eligibility windows changed (24 vs 48 months, and a reported January 2026 change). Amex shows a pop-up if you are ineligible. Late fees: the CFPB's $8 cap was vacated in April 2025, so fees of about $30–41 remain. Portal cash-back rates are not regulated, and tracking fails often.
- **Why people miss it:** It looks like a game for travel hobbyists, and people fear credit-score damage, which is usually small.
- **What we may say:** Explain how bonuses, the 5/24 rule, inquiries and fees work, and show the net value given the user's own spend. Do not tell a specific user to apply for a specific card; that is credit marketing and needs disclosure. Never suggest it to users carrying a balance.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign-transaction fees (often 3%) apply on non-USD charges for some cards. Business cards are available to sole proprietors using their SSN.
- **Sources:** myFICO, inquiries, https://www.myfico.com/crediteducation/CreditChecks.aspx (via snippet). Late-fee rule vacated Apr 15, 2025: https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/04/16/federal-judge-voids-cfpb-credit-card-late-fee-rule/. Offer size: The Points Party (Oct 9, 2026), https://thepointsparty.com/articles/chase-sapphire-preferred-complete-guide. 5/24 and Sapphire windows: The Points Guy, https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/chase-sapphire-preferred-bonus-eligibility/. Amex lifetime language: NerdWallet, https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/your-guide-to-amexs-once-per-lifetime-rule.
- **Confidence:** The inquiry effect is primary (via snippet). Offer size and issuer rules are secondary only; issuers do not publish 5/24. Portal rates: unknown, anecdotal.

## Unclaimed money

### State unclaimed property (dormant accounts, uncashed checks, deposits)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Everyone, especially people who moved, changed jobs, closed accounts, had utility or rental deposits, or are heirs.
- **What it is:** When a company can't reach you, it must hand your money (old paychecks, refunds, bank balances, insurance payouts, stock) to the state. The state holds it **forever** and returns it free when you claim. You search by name on each state's site or on MissingMoney.com, which covers most states at once.
- **Value:** Sum of claims found. Inputs: your name variants and every state you have lived or worked in. Example: NAUPA estimates states hold about $70 billion, and about 1 in 7 Americans has some. Programs returned $4.49 billion in FY2024 and about $4.25 billion in FY2025. Typical individual claims are unknown; verify.
- **Time to money:** weeks to months (simple claims are often 30–90 days)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes to search, an evening to claim
- **Deadline or season:** none for the owner. States hold the money indefinitely.
- **Traps and caveats:** It is always free to search and claim. "Finders" charge 10–35%, and some states cap their fees. Scam version: texts or emails saying "you have unclaimed funds, pay a fee". Name searches miss maiden names and typos. Federal money is separate: savings bonds (TreasuryDirect "Treasury Hunt"), pensions (PBGC) and FHA mortgage-insurance refunds (HUD).
- **Why people miss it:** They have never heard of it, or assume it's a scam.
- **What we may say:** Everything. Prompt the user to search, and link the official state and MissingMoney sites.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** NAUPA, https://unclaimed.org/ (describes MissingMoney as free and NAUPA-managed; no totals on the page, checked Oct 31, 2026). Totals via news citing NAUPA: FOX, https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/theres-billion-dollars-unclaimed-money-us-check-see-youre-owed-money; ClickOrlando May 7, 2026 (FY2025 $4.25B), https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/05/07/dollars-sense-billions-of-dollars-go-unclaimed-millions-of-people-are-missing-out/. https://www.missingmoney.com/. TreasuryDirect Treasury Hunt, https://www.treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/treasury-hunt/ (not fetched).
- **Confidence:** The search mechanics are verified primary. The $70B and "1 in 7" figures are secondary (news citing NAUPA; NAUPA's 2020 report said 1 in 10).

### Class-action settlements and FTC/CFPB refunds

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone who bought a product or used a service later sued over (data breaches, subscription traps, fees).
- **What it is:** When companies settle, everyone in the "class" can file a short claim form, often with no receipt for small amounts. Most people never file, so filers sometimes get more. The FTC also mails refunds from its own cases, often automatically.
- **Value:** Sum of per-claim payouts. Inputs: products and services you used and your proof. Example: the FTC sent $337.3M in refunds in 2024. In claims-made class settlements the FTC's study found a median claims rate of about 8–9% and a class-weighted rate of 4%, so the other ~90% get nothing. Typical consumer payouts run $5–$100 per claim. That range is anecdotal; verify per case.
- **Time to money:** months to years (payout after final court approval)
- **Risk:** none (false claims are perjury)
- **Effort:** minutes per claim
- **Deadline or season:** each settlement has a hard claim deadline
- **Traps and caveats:** Only file claims you are honestly in. Phishing sites mimic settlement pages, so check that the administrator site is linked from the court or FTC. FTC refund agencies never ask for payment or bank login.
- **Why people miss it:** Notices look like spam, and amounts seem too small to bother with.
- **What we may say:** Inform and link to the official FTC refunds list and administrator sites. Do not file claims on users' behalf without explicit consent.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** FTC refunds list, https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds. FTC 2024 refunds report, https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-refunds-consumers-2024.pdf (headline $337.3M per snippet). FTC claims-rate study (2019) via Duke Judicature, https://judicature.duke.edu/articles/claims-made-class-action-settlements/ and Bloomberg Law, https://news.bloomberglaw.com/class-action/insight-surprising-results-from-ftc-study-of-class-settlement-claims-response-rates.
- **Confidence:** FTC totals verified primary via snippet. Claims rates are secondary (8% vs 9% median conflict). Per-claim payouts are anecdotal.

### Unfiled tax returns: refunds the IRS keeps after 3 years

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone who had tax withheld, or qualified for refundable credits (EITC, child tax credit), and did not file. This includes students, part-time and low-income workers.
- **What it is:** If you don't file, the IRS doesn't send your refund. You generally have **3 years** from the original due date to claim it, then the Treasury keeps it. Filing late when you're owed a refund carries no penalty.
- **Value:** Withholding plus refundable credits minus tax owed, per unfiled year. Inputs: W-2s, 1099s and credits. Example: for tax year 2022, the IRS estimated ~$1.2B unclaimed by 1.3M+ people, median **$686** before credits. That deadline passed Apr 15, 2026. **Tax year 2023 refunds expire Apr 15, 2027** (3-year rule; the IRS has not yet published a 2023 estimate, verify).
- **Time to money:** weeks (e-file) to months (paper)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening per year
- **Deadline or season:** Apr 15, 2027 for 2023 returns
- **Traps and caveats:** Refunds can be held if later years are also unfiled, and offset against federal or state debts or child support. Self-employed people owing SE tax may owe rather than be owed.
- **Why people miss it:** "I didn't earn enough to have to file" (true, but you still have to file to get money back).
- **What we may say:** All of it. Prompt users with past W-2 withholding to check.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign-client income still counts. A missed year with net SE earnings ≥ $400 means a return was required.
- **Sources:** IRS IR-2026-37 (Mar 20, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/time-is-running-out-to-claim-12-billion-in-refunds-for-tax-year-2022-taxpayers-face-april-15-deadline.
- **Confidence:** verified primary (via snippet of the IRS release)

## Tax

### Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction, Section 199A

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Sole proprietors, single-member LLCs, partners and S-corp owners with US business profit. For incomes like $60k there are no service-business restrictions; those start at much higher incomes (2026 threshold ≈ $200k single, verify).
- **What it is:** You can deduct up to 20% of your business profit from taxable income, on top of the standard deduction. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21, signed Jul 4, 2025) made it **permanent**. From 2026 it adds a **$400 minimum deduction** if you have at least $1,000 of QBI from an active business.
- **Value:** lesser of 20% × QBI (profit − half SE tax − SE health insurance − retirement contributions) or 20% × (taxable income before QBI), times your marginal rate. Example: $60k profit gives QBI ≈ $55,760 and taxable income before QBI ≈ $39,660. The deduction ≈ $7,930, which saves ≈ **$950** at 12%.
- **Time to money:** months (at filing, or via lower estimated payments)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (Form 8995 in tax software)
- **Deadline or season:** tax return
- **Traps and caveats:** Software does it automatically only if Schedule C is filed correctly. People who misreport freelance income as "other income" lose it. It lowers income tax only, not SE tax. It does not reduce AGI, so it does not help PTC or Saver's Credit eligibility.
- **Why people miss it:** They never heard of it, or mis-file gig income.
- **What we may say:** Explain and estimate it with the user's numbers. That is education.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** QBI is limited to income from a US trade or business. Work done while living in the US for foreign clients is generally US-source, so it qualifies. Work performed abroad may not; verify.
- **Sources:** OBBBA §70105 summaries: Porte Brown, https://blog.portebrown.com/qbi-deduction-how-the-obbba-improves-the-rules; Beancount, https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/24/obbba-400-minimum-qbi-deduction-small-business-guide. IRS Form 8995 instructions (not fetched).
- **Confidence:** The permanence and the $400 minimum are secondary only (multiple consistent tax-firm sources). The worked example is our arithmetic.

### Deduct half of self-employment tax (and know what SE tax is)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with net self-employment earnings ≥ $400.
- **What it is:** Freelancers pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare: 15.3% on 92.35% of profit, with the Social Security part up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. You get to deduct the "employer half" from income, without itemizing.
- **Value:** 50% × SE tax × marginal income-tax rate. Example: profit $60k → SE tax ≈ $8,480 → deduction ≈ $4,240 → saves ≈ $510 at 12% (less after the QBI interaction, ≈ $410).
- **Time to money:** at filing
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (Schedule SE / Schedule 1)
- **Deadline or season:** tax return
- **Traps and caveats:** The biggest trap is cash flow, not the deduction. New freelancers forget the 15.3% entirely and owe a surprise bill. A business expense lowers SE tax and income tax together, which is why real expenses are worth ~27% at this income.
- **Why people miss it:** Gig workers think "my income tax rate is 12%" and do not budget for SE tax.
- **What we may say:** Show the user's own estimated SE tax and set-aside amount.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** US citizens and residents owe SE tax on worldwide self-employment income. If working inside a "totalization agreement" country, verify.
- **Sources:** SSA 2026 wage base $184,500 (COLA fact sheet), https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html (page text not readable; figure via Journal of Accountancy, https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2025/oct/social-security-wage-base-and-cola-announced-for-2026/). IRS Topic 554, https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554 (not fetched).
- **Confidence:** The wage base is verified primary via secondary report. The rates are long-standing law; verify with Topic 554.

### Home office deduction

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed people (not W-2 employees) who use a specific area of their home **regularly and exclusively** for business, owners or renters.
- **What it is:** Either $5 per square foot up to 300 sq ft (max $1,500, the simplified method), or the business share of rent, utilities, insurance and so on (the regular method, Form 8829). It lowers both SE tax and income tax.
- **Value:** Simplified: min(sq ft, 300) × $5 × (≈14.1% SE + marginal income rate × 0.8 after QBI). Example: a 150 sq ft office = $750 deduction, saving ≈ $175 at $60k. At 300 sq ft = $1,500, saving ≈ $350. Regular method example: 10% of a $1,800/mo rent plus $150/mo utilities = $2,340, saving ≈ $540.
- **Time to money:** at filing
- **Risk:** low (audit question if the space is not exclusive)
- **Effort:** an evening (measure, keep a photo and floor plan)
- **Deadline or season:** tax return
- **Traps and caveats:** A guest room or kitchen table doesn't qualify. Owners using the regular method face depreciation recapture when selling. The deduction is limited to business profit.
- **Why people miss it:** Fear that it is an "audit flag", and confusion with the W-2 employee rule (employees can't take it).
- **What we may say:** Explain the eligibility test and compute both methods with the user's numbers.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** IRS simplified option ($5/sq ft, 300 sq ft), https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/simplified-option-for-home-office-deduction (not fetched; confirmed via secondary Kiplinger, https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/income-tax/603972/most-overlooked-tax-deductions-and-credits-self-employed).
- **Confidence:** secondary only (figures unchanged since 2013; verify on IRS page)

### Self-employed health insurance deduction

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed people with a net profit who pay their own health, dental or qualifying long-term-care premiums, and who were not eligible for an employer-subsidized plan (through their own or a spouse's job) that month.
- **What it is:** Premiums for you, your spouse and children under 27 come off your income "above the line", without itemizing. It reduces income tax (not SE tax) and lowers AGI, which can increase your marketplace subsidy.
- **Value:** Premiums paid (net of PTC) × marginal rate × ~0.8 (QBI interaction). Example: $5,000 of premiums → saves ≈ $480 federal.
- **Time to money:** at filing
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes, unless you also get PTC (then it is a circular calculation, IRS Form 7206 / Pub 974)
- **Deadline or season:** tax return
- **Traps and caveats:** It is capped at business profit. It can't be taken for months you could have joined a spouse's employer plan. People with PTC double-count by deducting the full premium instead of the net.
- **Why people miss it:** They think medical costs need itemizing (that's a different deduction).
- **What we may say:** Explain and compute it.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** IRS Form 7206 (not fetched; rules via secondary), https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-7206. Kiplinger, https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/income-tax/603972/most-overlooked-tax-deductions-and-credits-self-employed.
- **Confidence:** secondary only (rules well established; verify on IRS)

### Solo 401(k) vs SEP-IRA for the self-employed

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Self-employed people with no employees other than a spouse (Solo 401(k)), or any self-employed person (SEP).
- **What it is:** Retirement accounts you open yourself. **2026:** a Solo 401(k) lets you put in up to **$24,500** as "employee" plus about **20% of net SE earnings** as "employer", with a total cap of **$72,000** (+$8,000 catch-up at 50+, $11,250 at ages 60–63). A SEP only allows the ~20% employer part, with a 25% formula on employees and a $72,000 cap. Solo 401(k)s can be Roth.
- **Value:** Tax saved = pre-tax contribution × marginal rate (≈ 0.8 × 12% here), plus long-run tax-deferred or tax-free growth. Example ($60k profit): the max Solo 401(k) ≈ $24,500 + 20% × $55,760 ≈ $11,150 = **$35,650**, while the max SEP ≈ **$11,150**. A realistic $10,000 pre-tax contribution saves ≈ **$960** federal and lowers MAGI by $10k (see the PTC row). At 12%, a **Roth** contribution forgoes ≈ $960 now but makes all future growth tax-free. At this income, Roth is often the better trade (education, not advice).
- **Time to money:** immediate tax effect at filing; retirement money is locked until 59½ (with exceptions)
- **Risk:** market (if invested); behavioural
- **Effort:** an evening to open, then ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** A **SEP** can be opened and funded up to the extended return due date. A new **Solo 401(k)**: employer contributions are due by the extended due date. SECURE 2.0 §317 lets a sole proprietor's *first-year* employee deferrals be made up to the unextended return date; for later years, the deferral election must be made by Dec 31 (verify with Pub 560).
- **Traps and caveats:** File Form 5500-EZ when plan assets reach $250k+. The employee limit is shared with any W-2 401(k). The "20%" is computed on profit after half of SE tax. Contributions do not reduce SE tax. Watch for provider fees.
- **Why people miss it:** Freelancers think retirement plans come from employers, and the Solo 401(k) is barely marketed.
- **What we may say:** Show the user's own contribution room and tax effect under each, and explain Roth vs pre-tax. Do not recommend specific providers or funds without adviser status.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign-client income counts as net SE earnings. If excluding income under the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (living abroad), that income can't fund these plans; verify.
- **Sources:** IRS IR-2025-111 (Nov 13, 2025), https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500. IRS SEP limits ($72,000 for 2026, page reviewed Jun 28, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/node/5958. IRS one-participant 401(k) page (reviewed Apr 9, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/one-participant-401k-plans. 415(c) $72,000 per Notice 2025-67 via PSCA, https://www.psca.org/news/psca-news/2025/11/irs-announces-2026-401k-contribution-limits/. SECURE 2.0 §317 via Lord Abbett, https://www.lordabbett.com/en-us/financial-advisor/insights/retirement-planning/retroactive-first-year-solo-401-k--plan-deferrals-allowed-for-so.html.
- **Confidence:** Limits verified primary. The deadline rules are secondary.

### Estimated tax: safe harbor and the underpayment penalty

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Anyone expecting to owe $1,000+ when filing. That covers nearly every freelancer without withholding.
- **What it is:** The IRS wants tax paid during the year, in four quarterly payments. Paying late costs interest-like "penalty" (7% a year in Q4 2026). You avoid it entirely if you pay the **smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax** (110% if last year's AGI > $150k).
- **Value:** Underpayment × IRS rate × days late / 365. Example: $12,000 of total 2026 tax, paid in a lump on Apr 15, 2027 instead of $3,000 per quarter → ≈ $3,000 × 7% × (365+304+212+90)/365 ≈ **$560** penalty. Using last year's tax as a fixed target removes guesswork.
- **Time to money:** immediate (avoided cost)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes per quarter (IRS Direct Pay, EFTPS)
- **Deadline or season:** 2026 tax year: Apr 15, Jun 15 and Sep 15, 2026, and **Jan 15, 2027**
- **Traps and caveats:** The rate is reset quarterly (6% in Q2 2026, 7% in Q3 and Q4). The "annualized income" method helps lumpy earners (Form 2210 Schedule AI). Scam version: calls or texts demanding immediate payment by gift card.
- **Why people miss it:** New freelancers have never had to pay tax themselves.
- **What we may say:** Compute the user's safe-harbor amount and remind them of dates.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** The same rules apply. Convert foreign-currency income at the rate when received, or a consistent yearly average (IRS yearly average rates).
- **Sources:** IRS Estimated taxes (reviewed Sep 25, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes. IRS Q4 2026 interest (IR-2026-98, Aug 21, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/interest-rates-remain-the-same-for-the-fourth-quarter-of-2026. The 110% rule is from IRS Pub 505, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505 (not fetched).
- **Confidence:** verified primary (110% rule from Pub 505, not re-checked)

### Roth IRA in a low-income year, plus the Saver's Credit (Saver's Match from 2027)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with earned income (W-2 or net SE). **2026:** the Roth IRA phases out at $153k–$168k MAGI (single). The Saver's Credit requires age 18+, not a full-time student, not a dependent, and AGI ≤ **$40,250 single / $60,375 head of household / $80,500 joint**.
- **What it is:** A Roth IRA (up to **$7,500** in 2026, +$1,100 at 50+) is taxed now but grows and comes out tax-free. In a year when you're in the 10% or 12% bracket, that is cheap tax to pay. Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn any time. The Saver's Credit pays back 10%, 20% or 50% of up to $2,000 contributed, max $1,000. From **2027** it becomes the **Saver's Match**: Treasury deposits up to $1,000 directly into your account (50% of up to $2,000, phasing out at $35,500 single / $71,000 joint), with first deposits in 2028.
- **Value:** Roth: tax-free growth. $7,500 at an assumed 7% for 40 years ≈ $112,000 with no tax on withdrawal. Saver's Credit: rate × min(contribution, $2,000). Example: a student-freelancer with $22k AGI who puts in $2,000 gets a $1,000 or $400 credit depending on the band (credit is nonrefundable, so it needs tax liability).
- **Time to money:** years (growth); the credit comes at filing
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** contributions for 2026 can be made until **Apr 15, 2027**
- **Traps and caveats:** A Roth needs earned income, and SE losses can reduce it. Excess contributions carry a 6% yearly excise tax. The Saver's Credit is nonrefundable, so people with zero tax get nothing until the 2027 Match. A $60k freelancer is above the Saver's Credit limit.
- **Why people miss it:** Low earners think investing is for later. Fewer than half of eligible people know the Saver's Credit exists (anecdotal; verify).
- **What we may say:** Explain and compute. Don't pick funds inside the IRA.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Income excluded under FEIE doesn't count as compensation for IRA purposes.
- **Sources:** IRS IR-2025-111, https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500 (Roth phase-out, Saver's Credit AGI limits, IRA limit). Saver's Match (Notice 2026-48) via 401(k) Specialist, https://401kspecialistmag.com/treasury-irs-begin-rulemaking-for-2027-savers-match-advance-trumpira-gov/, and Pew, https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/fact-sheets/2024/04/federal-savers-match-could-benefit-millions-of-low-to-moderate-income-americans.
- **Confidence:** 2026 limits verified primary. The Saver's Match details are secondary.

### Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Low- to moderate-income workers, including the self-employed, with investment income under a cap. Bigger amounts go to people with children. Childless workers aged 25–64 get a small credit.
- **What it is:** A **refundable** credit, so you get it even if you owe no tax. About 1 in 5 eligible people don't claim it, often self-employed people, childless workers and people who don't usually file.
- **Value:** Credit from the IRS tables, based on earned income, AGI and number of children. Example: unknown for 2026 exact maxima (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 not fetched); verify on IRS. In TY2022, about 19.2% of eligible taxpayers didn't claim it. Participation was ~78% by count and ~81% by dollars in TY2021.
- **Time to money:** weeks after filing (EITC refunds can't be paid before mid-February by law)
- **Risk:** none (but errors trigger audits and 2–10-year bans)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** tax return; 3-year lookback to amend or claim
- **Traps and caveats:** Self-employed people who under-report expenses to *raise* earned income, or over-report, both get flagged. Paid preparers sometimes add costly refund-advance products. A $60k single freelancer is above the limit.
- **Why people miss it:** They don't file because income is low, or don't know SE income counts.

- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Requires a valid SSN and US residence for more than half the year. People claiming the FEIE can't take the EITC.

### 2025–2026 federal law changes that matter (OBBBA, "Working Families Tax Cuts")

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** All US filers. Each item has its own eligibility.
- **What it is:** OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, signed Jul 4, 2025) changed many rules. The ones a young freelancer can use:
  - **Standard deduction 2026:** $16,100 single / $24,150 head of household / $32,200 joint.
  - **QBI** is permanent with a $400 minimum (row above).
  - **1099 thresholds:** 1099-K back to >$20,000 *and* >200 transactions, and 1099-NEC/MISC raised from $600 to **$2,000** for payments from 2026.
  - **Charitable deduction for non-itemizers** from 2026: up to $1,000 single / $2,000 joint, cash only.
  - **Child tax credit** $2,200 per child.
  - **Dependent-care FSA** limit $7,500 from 2026.
  - **"No tax on tips"** up to $25,000 and **overtime** (the premium half) up to $12,500, both 2025–2028. These phase out above $150k MAGI, and self-employed tips are limited to net income of that trade.
  - **New-car loan interest** up to $10,000/yr (US-assembled, loans after Dec 31, 2024; phases out above $100k MAGI).
  - **Senior deduction** $6,000 (65+).
  - **HSA expansion** (row below).
  - **529 expansion:** K-12 limit $20,000 from 2026, plus credential costs.
  - **Trump Accounts:** $1,000 Treasury seed for US-citizen children born 2025–2028, elected on Form 4547. Contributions could start no earlier than Jul 4, 2026.
  - **Premium Tax Credit:** no cap on repaying excess advance credit from 2026 (verify).
- **Value:** Depends on the item. Example: a freelance bartender-designer with $8,000 of reported tips deducts $8,000 → ≈ $960 saved at 12%. A non-itemizer who gave $500 cash to charity saves $60.
- **Time to money:** at filing (2026 returns, filed in 2027)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes each (Schedule 1-A for tips, overtime, car interest and seniors)
- **Deadline or season:** tax return. Tips, overtime, car interest and seniors items expire after 2028.
- **Traps and caveats:** "No tax on tips" only covers occupations on the IRS list, and only tips voluntarily paid. Self-employed tips are limited to that trade's profit. The new deductions do not reduce SE tax. Expect scams promising "Trump Account" bonuses.
- **Why people miss it:** Too many changes at once, and headlines oversimplify them.
- **What we may say:** Explain each and flag which might apply based on the user's answers.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** For the 1099-K/NEC threshold, see the 1099-K row. Foreign clients don't issue 1099s anyway.

## Insurance & benefits

### Marketplace Premium Tax Credit (PTC) and the 400% FPL cliff

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** People buying their own insurance on HealthCare.gov or a state exchange, not offered affordable employer coverage, with household income from 100% to 400% of the federal poverty line. For 2026 coverage, a single person qualifies up to **$62,600** MAGI (400% × $15,650).
- **What it is:** A tax credit that pays part of your monthly premium. **The enhanced subsidies expired Dec 31, 2025.** The Senate rejected extensions on Dec 11, 2025, and a House-passed 3-year extension (Jan 8, 2026) was not enacted as of our sources. So for 2026, people at 300–400% FPL pay up to **9.96%** of income for the benchmark silver plan, and **above 400% FPL the credit is zero.** Lowering MAGI with pre-tax retirement or HSA contributions can bring you back under the cliff.
- **Value:** PTC = benchmark silver premium − (applicable % × MAGI). Inputs: age, ZIP, household size, MAGI. Illustrative example (benchmark premiums are hypothetical; look yours up): single freelancer, MAGI $55,760 → contribution 9.96% × 55,760 ≈ $5,550/yr. If the benchmark is $7,200 (age ~40), PTC ≈ **$1,650**. If $14,000 (age ~60), PTC ≈ **$8,450**. At MAGI $62,700 the PTC is **$0**, so $10k into a pre-tax Solo 401(k) can be worth the whole credit plus the tax saving.
- **Time to money:** monthly (advance credit) or at filing
- **Risk:** low (repayment if income is under-estimated)
- **Effort:** an evening; update income mid-year
- **Deadline or season:** **Open enrollment for 2027 starts Nov 1, 2026.** Under the 2025 CMS rule, HealthCare.gov's window was reported to shorten to Nov 1–Dec 15 from plan year 2027 (verify). Mid-year changes are allowed with a qualifying life event.
- **Traps and caveats:** From 2026, excess advance PTC must be **repaid in full** (OBBBA removed the caps; verify). Freelancers with lumpy income can cross the cliff in December. The interplay with the SE health insurance deduction is circular (Pub 974). The 2027 status of the enhanced subsidies is unknown. Scam version: fake "ACA" brokers selling short-term plans with no PTC.
- **Why people miss it:** They assume freelancers "make too much", or don't know MAGI can be managed.
- **What we may say:** Compute the user's credit and cliff distance with official benchmark data. Choosing a specific plan for them is insurance-agent territory (state licence).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** MAGI includes foreign earned income excluded under the FEIE. US residents report foreign-client income normally.
- **Sources:** KFF (updated Jan 14, 2026), https://www.kff.org/faqs/faqs-health-insurance-marketplace-and-the-aca/what-kind-of-recent-changes-to-the-marketplace-premium-subsidies-have-happened-that-i-should-know-about/. Health Affairs on the Senate failure, https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/senate-fails-extend-enhanced-aca-subsidies-focus-turns-house. NPR on the House vote Jan 8, 2026, https://www.npr.org/2026/01/08/nx-s1-5662625/house-vote-affordable-care-act-subsidies. Rev. Proc. 2025-25 (9.96%) via Current Federal Tax Developments, https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/7/18/rev-proc-2025-25-a-technical-review-of-2026-premium-tax-credit-and-affordability-adjustments. HHS 2025 poverty guideline $15,650 via secondary; ASPE page, https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines (not fetched).
- **Confidence:** The expiry is verified (KFF, news). The 9.96% figure and $62,600 (derived) are secondary. The repayment-cap removal, the OE dates and any 2026 legislative change after July: unknown, verify.

### Health Savings Account (HSA): triple tax advantage, wider in 2026

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** People covered only by an HSA-eligible high-deductible plan (2026 minimum deductible $1,700 self / $3,400 family). **New from Jan 1, 2026:** marketplace **bronze and catastrophic** plans count as HSA-eligible, and direct-primary-care memberships up to $150/mo (self) no longer disqualify you.
- **What it is:** Money goes in tax-deductible, grows tax-free and comes out tax-free for medical costs, the only account with all three. Unused money rolls over forever and can be invested. After 65 it works like a traditional IRA for non-medical use.
- **Value:** Contribution × marginal rate (× ~0.8 with QBI) + state tax + tax-free growth. **2026 limits: $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family**, +$1,000 at 55+. Example: $4,400 → ≈ $420 federal saved at $60k, and lowers MAGI by $4,400 for the PTC cliff.
- **Time to money:** at filing, then years
- **Risk:** market (if invested); low otherwise
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** contributions for 2026 can be made until **Apr 15, 2027**
- **Traps and caveats:** Self-employed people contribute directly, so there's no payroll FICA saving. Non-medical withdrawals before 65 cost tax plus 20%. California and New Jersey tax HSAs at state level. Some HSA providers charge fees or pay ~0% on cash.
- **Why people miss it:** They think of it as a spend account. Bronze plan buyers didn't know they became eligible in 2026.
- **What we may say:** Explain eligibility and compute the tax effect. Don't recommend specific investments inside the HSA.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** Rev. Proc. 2025-19 via KPMG, https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/05/tnf-rev-proc-2025-19-hsa-inflation-adjusted-amounts-for-2026.html, and Haynes Boone, https://www.haynesboone.com/news/blogs/irs-releases-2026-inflation-adjusted-amounts-for-hsas-and-hdhps. ### Low take-up benefits: SNAP, LIHEAP, Lifeline

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** Low-income households. SNAP has a gross income limit of about 130% FPL in most states, with higher limits in many states. LIHEAP is set by the state, often up to 60% of state median income. Lifeline covers people on SNAP, Medicaid or SSI, or with income ≤ 135% FPL. A freelancer in a slow year (low monthly income) can qualify, because eligibility uses *current* monthly income.
- **What it is:** SNAP pays monthly grocery money on a card. LIHEAP pays part of heating or cooling bills, and crisis help if you have a shut-off notice. Lifeline knocks **$9.25/mo** off phone or internet ($5.25 voice-only).
- **Value:** SNAP: the benefit depends on household size and net income (amount unknown here, verify on the state site). LIHEAP: one-time grants, state-set. Lifeline: $9.25 × 12 = **$111/yr**. Take-up: SNAP ~88% of eligible people in FY2022 (pandemic-era high; 78% in FY2020). LIHEAP reached **~17%** of eligible households in FY2024. - **Traps and caveats:** OBBBA tightened SNAP work requirements, including older adults and some parents (details not verified here). LIHEAP's federal funding and staff were cut back in 2025, so check whether your state's 2026–27 programme is open. ## Invest & cash

### High-yield savings vs big-bank savings, and how big a freelancer's emergency fund should be

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone holding cash, especially freelancers holding tax set-asides and an emergency fund.
- **What it is:** The FDIC national average savings rate is **0.38%** (Oct 19, 2026), while online high-yield accounts pay about **4.0–4.4% APY**. Both are FDIC-insured up to $250k. For freelancers, cash has two jobs: a **tax pot** (≈ 25–30% of each payment at $60k, covering SE + income tax) and an **emergency fund**. 6+ months of expenses is a common rule of thumb for irregular income; it is not an official standard.
- **Value:** Balance × (HYSA APY − current APY). Example: $10,000 at 4.0% vs 0.38% = **≈ $362/yr** more. A $3,000/mo spender holding $18,000 (6 months) plus a $3,000 tax pot ≈ $760/yr more.
- **Time to money:** monthly interest
- **Risk:** none (within FDIC limits); rates are variable
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Teaser rates drop. Check the bank is an FDIC member, not just a fintech app (the 2024 Synapse failure froze fintech customers' money). Interest is taxable. Some "4%+" rates require direct deposit or caps.
- **Why people miss it:** Inertia at their first bank.
- **What we may say:** Show the national average vs a user-entered rate. Comparison listings with paid placement need disclosure.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** A PayPal balance is not a bank deposit, though PayPal may offer pass-through insurance in some products (verify). Move cash out to an FDIC bank.

### Treasury bills and I bonds

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with an SSN and a US bank account. I bonds are capped at $10,000 per person per year (electronic).
- **What it is:** T-bills are short loans to the US government (4 to 52 weeks), bought at TreasuryDirect or a brokerage. They are **exempt from state and local income tax**. I bonds pay a fixed rate plus inflation, can't be cashed in for 12 months, and lose 3 months' interest if cashed before 5 years.
- **Value:** Rate × amount × time, plus state tax saved. Rates (Oct 30, 2026, coupon-equivalent): **4-wk 3.98%, 13-wk 4.08%, 26-wk 4.25%, 52-wk 4.36%**. **I bonds issued May–Oct 2026: 4.26% composite (0.90% fixed).** The new rate is announced Nov 1, 2026, tomorrow (unknown). Example: $10,000 in 26-week bills ≈ $212 in 6 months, and in a 5% state tax state saves ≈ $11 vs a HYSA.
- **Time to money:** weeks to months (bills); 1–5 years (I bonds)
- **Risk:** none for credit risk. I bonds carry liquidity risk.
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** I bond rates reset May 1 and Nov 1
- **Traps and caveats:** The TreasuryDirect site is clunky, and bills sold before maturity at a brokerage can lose a little. I bond interest is taxed federally when redeemed.
- **Why people miss it:** They think government bonds are for the rich or old.
- **What we may say:** Show current official rates and the user's math. Do not recommend an allocation.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** Treasury daily bill rates (Oct 30, 2026), https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_bill_rates&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610. ### Low-cost index and target-date funds, and the compounding example

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone investing for 5+ years through a 401(k), IRA, HSA or taxable account.
- **What it is:** Index funds own the whole market for a tiny fee. Target-date funds hold a mix that gets safer as the year in the name approaches. The fee you pay every year compounds against you, and starting early compounds for you.
- **Value:** Fee drag: $10,000 for 30 years at an assumed 7% gross, at 0.1% fees ≈ **$74,000** vs at 1.0% fees ≈ **$57,400**, so the fee gap costs ~**$16,600**. Starting early: $200/mo from 22 to 65 at an assumed 7% ≈ **$655,000**, starting at 32 ≈ **$309,000**. Ten years of delay halves the result. Asset-weighted average expense ratios in 2025: **index equity ETFs 0.14%, equity mutual funds 0.40%, bond funds 0.36%** (ICI). Target-date average: unknown, verify in the ICI report.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** an evening, then ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Returns are not guaranteed, and 7% is an assumption, not a forecast. Watch for front loads, 12b-1 fees and adviser wrap fees of 1%+. Selling in crashes is the real loss. "Guaranteed return" schemes are scams.
- **Why people miss it:** Fees look small as percentages.
- **What we may say:** Fee arithmetic, category averages and the user's own projection with stated assumptions. **Do not** name specific funds for the user (personalized securities advice).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** ICI release (Mar 25, 2026), https://www.ici.org/news-release/mutual-fund-and-etf-fees-remained-near-historic-lows-in-2025. Projections are our arithmetic.
- **Confidence:** Fees verified primary. The projections are illustrative math.

### Employer 401(k) match (for anyone with a W-2 job or side job)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Employees whose employer offers a match, including part-time W-2 jobs held alongside freelancing.
- **What it is:** Many employers add money when you contribute, e.g., 50¢ per $1 up to 6% of pay. Not contributing enough to get the full match leaves free pay on the table. Vesting rules may require staying 2–6 years for employer money.
- **Value:** Salary × match rate × matched percentage. Example: $40,000 salary, 100% match on the first 4% = **$1,600/yr** free, plus tax deferral. The average promised match was ~4.7% of pay (median 4.0%) in Vanguard's 2026 report (via secondary).
- **Time to money:** each paycheck (vesting may delay ownership)
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** some plans "true up" annually; enrol ASAP
- **Traps and caveats:** Vesting cliffs. The $24,500 employee limit is shared with any Solo 401(k). Hardship withdrawals cost tax plus 10%.
- **Why people miss it:** They don't enrol, or contribute below the match threshold.
- **What we may say:** Compute the user's unclaimed match.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** Vanguard How America Saves 2026, https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/has/2026/pdf/HowAmericaSaves2026.pdf (not opened; figure via 24/7 Wall St, https://247wallst.com/personal-finance/2026/06/19/the-average-american-leaves-2954-on-the-table-every-year-by-skipping-the-401k-match/). Limit from IRS IR-2025-111.
- **Confidence:** secondary only for the match statistic

### 529 plan for yourself (and the 529-to-Roth escape hatch)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone planning courses, degrees or, from OBBBA, recognized credential and licensing programs, including for themselves. Most valuable in states that give a state income-tax deduction for contributions.
- **What it is:** An investment account where growth is tax-free if spent on qualified education. You can name yourself as beneficiary. After the account has been open 15 years, up to $35,000 (lifetime) can roll into the beneficiary's Roth IRA, within the yearly IRA limit, $7,500 in 2026.
- **Value:** State deduction × state rate + tax-free growth. Example: in a state that lets you deduct $5,000 at a 5% rate, that's $250/yr (state rules vary; verify per state). Open a $50 account now to start the 15-year clock.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** many states use Dec 31 for the deduction, some use April
- **Traps and caveats:** Non-qualified withdrawals pay tax plus 10% on earnings, and states may recapture the deduction. The Roth rollover needs earned income and direct transfer, and contributions made in the last 5 years are excluded.
- **Why people miss it:** They think 529 means "for kids".
- **What we may say:** Explain and compute the state benefit. Do not choose the plan's portfolio.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** SECURE 2.0 §126 via Saving for College, https://www.savingforcollege.com/article/roll-over-529-plan-funds-to-a-roth-ira. OBBBA 529 expansion via Franklin Templeton, https://franklintempleton.com/articles-us/retirement/obbba-ten-tax-law-changes-taking-effect-in-2026.
- **Confidence:** secondary only (verify the credential-cost rules with IRS Pub 970)

## Stop the leak

### Credit card interest, minimum payments and late fees

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone carrying a card balance month to month.
- **What it is:** Cards charging interest averaged **22.36% APR** in Aug 2026 (Fed). Paying only the minimum keeps you in debt for years. Late fees of around $30–41 are still allowed after the $8 cap was struck down in 2025.
- **Value:** Balance × APR. Example: $5,000 at 22.36% ≈ **$93/mo, about $1,118/yr** in interest. A 0% balance transfer (typically 3–5% fee, anecdotal) on $5,000 costs $150–250 once instead. Your statement must show how long minimum-only payment takes (CARD Act disclosure).
- **Time to money:** immediate (saved interest)
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** an evening; autopay at least the minimum
- **Deadline or season:** promo APRs end on fixed dates
- **Traps and caveats:** Balance-transfer cards revert to a high APR. Deferred-interest store cards charge back all interest if not fully paid by the deadline. Debt-settlement firms charging upfront fees are illegal under the FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule.
- **Why people miss it:** The minimum payment feels like "the bill".
- **What we may say:** Show the user's interest cost and payoff timelines. Avalanche vs snowball is education.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Some cards charge a 3% foreign-transaction fee.
- **Sources:** Federal Reserve G.19 (Oct 7, 2026), https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm. Late-fee rule vacated, https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/04/16/federal-judge-voids-cfpb-credit-card-late-fee-rule/.
- **Confidence:** The APR is verified primary. The late-fee range is secondary.

### Overdraft and NSF fees

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with a thin checking balance, especially freelancers whose PayPal transfers land irregularly.
- **What it is:** Banks charge about $35 when you spend more than your balance, sometimes several times a day. Congress repealed the CFPB's 2024 overdraft rule ($5-type caps for large banks) in 2025, and fees rose again: over $12B in 2025 per NCLC.
- **Value:** Fees × frequency. Example: 1 overdraft a month at $35 = **$420/yr**. Many banks offer $0-overdraft accounts or decline-instead settings.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (opt out of overdraft "coverage" for debit cards, link savings, low-balance alerts)
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Opting out of debit overdraft is your right under Reg E. Checks and ACH can still overdraw. Some fintech "free overdraft" products charge "tips" or express fees.
- **Why people miss it:** Default settings, and the fees are spread out.
- **What we may say:** Show fees from the user's statements and explain the opt-out.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** PayPal-to-bank transfers can take 1–3 business days, and timing gaps cause overdrafts.
- **Sources:** CRS IN12513 (repeal), https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12513.html. NCLC brief (Jun 3, 2026), https://www.nclc.org/resources/overdraft-fees-rising-in-absence-of-cfpb-rule/. CFPB 2023 data, https://consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/trends-in-overdraftnon-sufficient-fund-nsf-fee-revenue-and-practices.
- **Confidence:** The repeal is verified (CRS). The $12B figure is secondary (advocacy group). The ~$35 typical fee is secondary (CRS).

### Expensive fast cash: payday loans, check cashing, tax refund advances

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** People short on cash before payday or tax time, especially the unbanked (4.2% of US households in 2023).
- **What it is:** Payday loans typically charge $15 per $100 for two weeks, a **391% APR**, and most borrowers roll them over. Check cashers take a cut of each check: 1–10% reported, with no primary source for an average. Tax "refund advances" and refund transfers add fees or push paid prep.
- **Value:** Cost avoided. Example: $400 payday loan rolled 5 times = $60 × 5 = **$300** in fees. Cashing $2,000/mo of checks at 2% = **$480/yr**. A free bank account or credit-union "payday alternative loan" (PAL, capped at 28% APR) avoids most of it.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** an evening (open a free account, join a credit union)
- **Deadline or season:** tax-season advances run Dec–Mar
- **Traps and caveats:** About 80% of payday loans are re-borrowed within 14 days (CFPB 2014). The CFPB weakened its 2017 payday rule in 2020. TurboTax's 2026 refund advance was 0% APR; others vary, so read the terms. Tribal and online lenders may ignore state caps.
- **Why people miss it:** Speed and no credit check feel like a lifeline.
- **What we may say:** Show the APR and fee comparison. Don't broker loans.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** PayPal Instant Transfer charges a fee (percentage, capped; verify current rates) that works like a small check-cashing fee.
- **Sources:** CFPB payday Data Point (2014), https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201403_cfpb_report_payday-lending.pdf (391% and rollover figures via secondary reports). FDIC 2023 unbanked survey via Orrick, https://infobytes.orrick.com/2024-11-15/fdic-reports-lowest-unbanked-rate-start-survey/. TurboTax refund advance, https://ttlc.intuit.com/turbotax-support/en-us/help-article/tax-refund/refund-advance/L52Mg0G5u_US_en_US.
- **Confidence:** Payday figures are primary (2014 CFPB, via secondary). Check-cashing rates are anecdotal. The PAL 28% cap is NCUA rule (not fetched, verify).

### Buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) late fees and stacking

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone splitting purchases into 4 payments or monthly plans.
- **What it is:** Pay-in-4 is often 0% interest, but missed payments bring late fees, and longer plans can carry 30%+ APR. Several plans at once hide how much you owe. The CFPB withdrew its 2024 rule treating BNPL like credit cards (May 2025), so dispute and refund rights are weaker. New York passed its own BNPL law.
- **Value:** Fees avoided. The per-provider late-fee level is unknown (it varies by provider and state, often capped by state law), so verify.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes (list all open plans, set autopay from a funded account)
- **Deadline or season:** holiday season peaks
- **Traps and caveats:** Autopay from an empty account causes overdraft plus a late fee. BNPL is increasingly reported to credit bureaus.
- **Why people miss it:** "0%" framing.
- **What we may say:** Aggregate the user's own BNPL obligations and show due dates.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** PayPal "Pay in 4" is a BNPL product too.
- **Sources:** CFPB BNPL compliance page (withdrawal May 12, 2025), https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/consumer-cards-resources/buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-products/. NCLC, https://www.nclc.org/resources/what-rights-do-buy-now-pay-later-purchasers-have.
- **Confidence:** Regulatory status verified primary. Fee levels: unknown.

### Auto-renewing subscriptions

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Everyone with streaming, software, gym or app subscriptions. Freelancers often pile up SaaS tools.
- **What it is:** Free trials roll into paid plans, and cancelling can be deliberately hard. The FTC's federal "click-to-cancel" rule was **vacated by the 8th Circuit on Jul 8, 2025**. Over half of states have their own auto-renewal laws (e.g., California's online-cancellation rule).
- **Value:** Sum of unused subscriptions × 12. Example: three forgotten $15/mo tools = **$540/yr**. Business-use subscriptions are deductible, but cancelling saves more.
- **Time to money:** next billing cycle
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening, then quarterly
- **Deadline or season:** before annual renewals
- **Traps and caveats:** Annual plans renew at full price after an intro rate. Use virtual card numbers or PayPal's automatic-payments page to cancel billing agreements.
- **Why people miss it:** Small amounts on autopilot.
- **What we may say:** Detect recurring charges from the user's own transaction data and show them.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign-currency subscriptions add conversion fees.
- **Sources:** Latham & Watkins on the vacatur, https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Eighth-Circuit-Vacates-FTC-Click-to-Cancel-Rule-Days-Before-Compliance-Deadline.pdf. National Law Review, https://natlawreview.com/article/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-cancel-rule.
- **Confidence:** The vacatur is verified (multiple law firms). Dollar example is illustrative.

### Rent-to-own and "buy here, pay here" cars

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** People with thin or poor credit who need furniture, electronics or a car.
- **What it is:** Rent-to-own stores rent you an item weekly until you own it, often paying 2–3× retail. Buy-here-pay-here car lots lend their own money at high rates, often with GPS trackers and quick repossession.
- **Value:** Total paid − retail price. Example (CRS): a rent-to-own deal of $16/week for 52 weeks = $832 total, which works out to about **254% APR**. CFPB research found BHPH borrowers paid ~13% vs ~9% at banks for a similar risk profile, and 25–40% of finance-company and BHPH borrowers were 60+ days delinquent within 3 years (vs ~15% at banks).
- **Time to money:** immediate (avoided)
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** a weekend (credit union pre-approval, buy used with cash)
- **Deadline or season:** tax-refund season is peak car-lot marketing
- **Traps and caveats:** Rent-to-own often isn't covered by credit laws in some states. BHPH loans may not report to credit bureaus, so on-time payments build no credit.
- **Why people miss it:** The weekly price looks small.
- **What we may say:** Show total cost and implied APR.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Nothing.
- **Sources:** CRS RS21467, https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RS21467.html. CFPB Data Point "Subprime auto loan outcomes by lender type" via JD Supra summary, https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/cfpb-data-point-studies-subprime-5802839.
- **Confidence:** The CRS example is primary (government report). The BHPH figures are secondary summaries of CFPB research.

## Get paid better

### 1099-K, W-9 and backup withholding for PayPal income (2026)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** US freelancers paid through PayPal, Venmo, Stripe and similar platforms.
- **What it is:** PayPal sends you and the IRS a Form 1099-K when you receive **more than $20,000 *and* more than 200 goods-and-services payments** in a year (federal; OBBBA restored this, applied back to 2022). Some states set lower thresholds, e.g. Illinois > $1,000 *and* 4+ transactions, and MD, MA, VT and VA have their own. US clients paying you directly by bank or check issue 1099-NEC only from **$2,000** (2026 payments). **All income is taxable whether or not a form arrives.** If PayPal lacks your correct SSN or EIN (from a W-9 or the tax-info page), it can withhold **24%** of payments ("backup withholding").
- **Value:** Avoid 24% backup withholding on every payment, and avoid double-reporting. Example: $5,000/mo of client payments with a missing TIN → $1,200/mo withheld until filing. A free EIN keeps your SSN off client W-9s.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (enter TIN in PayPal; get an EIN at IRS.gov)
- **Deadline or season:** 1099-Ks arrive by Jan 31
- **Traps and caveats:** The 1099-K shows **gross** amounts, including fees, refunds and personal "goods and services" payments mislabelled. Reconcile on Schedule C. Don't receive business income as "friends and family" (that breaks PayPal terms and loses seller protection, and is still taxable). A PayPal regional page still shows $600 for 2026; it is outdated.
- **Why people miss it:** They think "no 1099 = no tax", or panic about 1099-K amounts that include non-income.
- **What we may say:** Explain and reconcile the user's PayPal data against their 1099-K.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Foreign clients don't issue 1099s, but PayPal still counts their payments toward the 1099-K. Currency conversion fees (PayPal's spread, ~3–4%, verify) are deductible business expenses. Foreign clients may ask for a W-9. You owe US tax on the USD value when received.
- **Sources:** IRS "Understanding your Form 1099-K" (reviewed Oct 28, 2026; ">$20,000 in more than 200 transactions"), https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k. PayPal help article (fetch blocked 403; content via search snippet), https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/help543. 1099-NEC $2,000 via Patriot Software, https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/. Backup withholding 24% via Bank of America merchant help, https://merchanthelp.bankofamerica.com/Form-1099-K-and-Backup-Withholding.
- **Confidence:** The 1099-K threshold is verified primary. The state thresholds and PayPal FX fee are secondary or unknown.

### Deposits, milestone invoicing, late fees and the cost of late clients

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** Freelancers invoicing clients, especially new clients or projects over ~$1,000.
- **What it is:** Ask for 25–50% upfront and bill the rest at milestones, so a disappearing client can't take all your work. A late fee (e.g., 1–1.5%/month) is generally enforceable **only if it's in the signed contract** and under your state's usury or interest caps (state law varies; verify). Freelancer-protection laws now give you rights in some places: **New York State's Freelance Isn't Free Act** (effective Aug 28, 2024) requires a written contract for work ≥ $800, payment by the contract date or within 30 days, and allows **double damages** plus attorney fees. Illinois, NYC, LA, Seattle and others have similar rules (not verified here).
- **Value:** Late cost = invoice × your cost of money × days late / 365. Example: a $5,000 invoice paid 60 days late while you carry a card balance at 22.36% costs ≈ **$184**; at HYSA opportunity cost of 4% ≈ $33. A non-paying client without a deposit = a 100% loss vs 50% with a deposit.
- **Time to money:** immediate on next contract
- **Risk:** low (some clients push back)
- **Effort:** an evening (contract template)
- **Deadline or season:** before starting each project
- **Traps and caveats:** Late fees not agreed in writing are hard to collect. Excessive fees can be void as "penalties". Small-claims limits vary by state. PayPal invoices are subject to chargebacks; deliverables tracking helps seller protection (for goods; services coverage is limited, verify).
- **Why people miss it:** Fear of losing the client.
- **What we may say:** Templates, education on the user's state law (with links), and the user's cost-of-late math. Drafting a contract for a specific user may be legal advice; keep it a template.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** US state laws protect you against the hiring party only within their reach. Foreign clients are hard to sue, so deposits matter more. Invoice in USD to push FX risk onto the client.
- **Sources:** NY FIFA via Bond Schoeneck & King (Aug 27, 2024), https://www.bsk.com/news-events-videos/new-york-enacts-statewide-ldquo-freelance-isn-rsquo-t-free-rdquo-legislation, and Hinshaw, https://sitepilot10.firmseek.com/client/hinshaw/www/blogs-employmentlawobserver,new-york-freelance-is-not-free-act.
- **Confidence:** NY FIFA is secondary (law-firm summaries). Late-fee legality is general contract law; per-state caps unknown, verify. The math is ours.

# Wealth moves: universal and cross-border

Scope: moves that hold in most countries, plus the ones specific to people paid across borders. First user: a freelance designer resident in Japan, paid in USD by US clients through PayPal. Rows follow `TEMPLATE.md`. Checked Oct 31, 2026 unless noted. Where a source was cited from prior knowledge and not re-opened today, the row says "not re-opened today; verify".

## Summary (12 lines)

1. Stop paying revolving interest first: every yen or dollar of a 15–22% card balance repaid earns that rate with no risk (US banks: 22.36% APR on accounts charged interest, Aug 2026). Minutes of effort, largest guaranteed return.
2. Take every "free money" scheme you qualify for (employer match, tax-deductible pension or mutual-aid schemes, government top-ups): an instant 25–100% return on the amount put in. One evening.
3. Run the official unclaimed-money searches once a year (dormant accounts, lost pensions, unfiled refunds). Free, about 30 minutes, and the 3-year (US) or 5-year (Japan) refund windows close for good.
4. Automate "pay yourself first" on payday. It works through inertia rather than willpower: auto-enrolment lifted 401(k) participation from 37% to 86% in the classic study.
5. Size the emergency fund in months of essential spending, kept apart from money set aside for tax. Irregular income needs a bigger buffer, not a smaller one.
6. Once a year, phone or re-quote insurance, phone and internet, and cancel subscriptions you would not buy again today. An evening's work that keeps paying every month.
7. Use the protections you already have before buying new ones: the legal guarantee, card purchase protection, and refund and compensation rights such as EU261.
8. Cut the quiet drains with the worst odds: lottery (Japanese law caps prizes at 50% of sales), payday-style credit (~400% APR), BNPL stacking, and FX spreads on every conversion.
9. Cross-border 1 (cost): through PayPal Japan a USD invoice costs 4.10% + US$0.30 to receive, and 3.00% more to convert to yen. That is about 7.0% of a $1,000 invoice. Know this number before setting prices.
10. Cross-border 2 (paperwork): give US clients a Form W-8BEN, never a W-9. Design work done while physically in Japan is foreign-source income for the US, so nothing should be withheld. Any US tax wrongly withheld can be credited in Japan.
11. Cross-border 3 (Japan tax): services to overseas clients are generally free of Japanese consumption tax, either as export-exempt or as outside its scope. Book the income in yen at the TTM rate on the transaction date. Exchange gains or losses from holding USD are a separate item.
12. Unverified items are flagged in each row. Japan-specific deductions such as iDeCo, furusato nozei and blue-return are only referenced here; they belong in the Japan file.

## A. Cross-border freelancer money

### Give US clients Form W-8BEN, not W-9 (租税条約関連の米国様式 W-8BEN)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Non-US individuals (nonresident aliens) paid by US businesses or platforms. Companies use W-8BEN-E instead.
- **What it is:** US payers have to document who they pay. A W-8BEN tells them you are a foreign person, so they should not withhold US tax or send 1099 forms. If you give no form, the payer may withhold at 30% or at the backup-withholding rate. If you wrongly give a W-9, you are treated as a US person.
- **Value:** amount at risk = (US-source share of the payment) × 30%, or × the 24% backup rate. For services performed outside the US the US-source share is normally 0. Example: a $2,000 job wrongly withheld at 24% means $480 held back until you claim it.
- **Time to money:** days (avoids a hold; recovering wrongly withheld tax takes months)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** at onboarding. The form is valid until the last day of the third calendar year after signing (a form signed in 2026 lasts to Dec 31, 2029) unless your circumstances change.
- **Traps and caveats:** Line 6a asks for a foreign tax ID; for Japan this is usually the My Number (individual number), but verify current practice. Claim treaty benefits only if a US-source item really exists. Days worked while physically in the US become US-source income. Phishing emails that ask for a W-8BEN together with bank details are a known scam pattern, so send it only to a known client contact.
- **Why people miss it:** Clients send a W-9 by default, and the freelancer signs whatever arrives.
- **What we may say:** We may explain what the form does and the source rule. Filling in the form for the user, or deciding their treaty position, is individual tax advice; in Japan, tax-agent work is reserved to licensed tax accountants (税理士法).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This is the core case. With PayPal, US clients often skip the form; ask whether they need one for their accounts payable.
- **Sources:** IRS, Instructions for Form W-8BEN (Rev. 10/2021), https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben. IRS, "Nonresident aliens – source of income" ("where services performed"), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nonresident-aliens-source-of-income. 24% backup rate: IRS Backup Withholding page, https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding (not re-opened today; verify).
- **Confidence:** verified primary for validity and the purpose of the form. Secondary only for the 24% figure and the My Number-as-FTIN practice.

### Know your all-in PayPal cost per invoice (PayPal 受取手数料＋通貨換算手数料)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone receiving commercial (goods and services) payments by PayPal from another country.
- **What it is:** The receiver pays the fee, not the client. PayPal Japan charges a domestic rate plus a cross-border surcharge plus a fixed fee in the received currency. Converting USD to JPY then adds a markup over PayPal's base exchange rate. Added together, this is the real price of being paid.
- **Value:** cost = invoice × (3.60% + 0.50%) + US$0.30, then (net USD × base rate) × 3.00% when converted to yen. Withdrawals under ¥50,000 per session cost ¥250; instant withdrawal costs 2% (min ¥500, max ¥2,000). Example at an illustrative ¥150/US$: on a $1,000 invoice the fee is $41.30, leaving $958.70, which converts to about ¥139,491 instead of ¥150,000. Total cost about ¥10,509, or 7.0%. On US$40,000 a year that is about US$2,800.
- **Time to money:** immediate (it applies to every invoice)
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** ongoing. PayPal changes fees through policy updates; the Japan page was last updated Jan 22, 2026.
- **Traps and caveats:** If you refund a commercial payment, PayPal keeps the fee it charged you. Dispute fees (US$15 standard, US$30 high-volume) and chargeback fees (US$10) apply in USD. A refund received more than one day after the payment is converted at 4.00%. Withdrawing to a US bank account costs 3.00% plus conversion. The US fee page (US-registered receivers: 1.50% international surcharge, 3–4% conversion) does not apply to a Japan-registered account. Check which country's schedule binds you.
- **Why people miss it:** The fee and the FX markup appear at different times, and the markup is built into the rate instead of shown as a line item.
- **What we may say:** We may calculate the user's own cost from published schedules. Recommending a specific payment provider is a commercial claim, so keep comparisons factual and include each provider's own published fees.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies directly.
- **Sources:** PayPal Japan, ビジネス手数料 (receiving 3.60% + fixed fee; cross-border +0.50%; USD fixed fee 0.30 USD; conversion +3.00%/+4.00%; withdrawal ¥250 under ¥50,000; dispute and chargeback fees), last updated 2026-01-22, https://www.paypal.com/jp/business/paypal-business-fees and https://www.paypal.com/jp/webapps/mpp/merchant-fees. PayPal US merchant fees (invoicing 3.49% + $0.49; international +1.50%; conversion 3.00%/4.00%), last updated 2026-10-01, https://www.paypal.com/us/business/paypal-business-fees.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for all fee figures. The ¥150 rate is illustrative only.

### Choose the invoicing currency and decide who bears conversion (請求通貨の選択)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** Freelancers with foreign clients.
- **What it is:** Invoicing in the client's currency (USD) makes paying easy but leaves you with the exchange risk and the conversion cost. Invoicing in your own currency (JPY) moves both to the client but adds friction. A middle path is to invoice in USD with prices set to cover your fees, and to re-price at agreed intervals or when the rate moves past a set band.
- **Value:** required USD price = target net yen ÷ (base rate × (1 − conversion %)) ÷ (1 − fee %), plus the fixed fee. Example: to net ¥150,000 at ¥150/$ via PayPal Japan: 150,000 ÷ (150 × 0.97) = $1,030.93; ÷ 0.959 = $1,075.00; + $0.30 → invoice about $1,075.
- **Time to money:** weeks (applies from the next quote)
- **Risk:** market (exchange-rate moves between quote and payment)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** at contract renewal or new quote
- **Traps and caveats:** Some payment providers' terms limit adding a separate payment surcharge; building the cost into your rate is the safer option. Check the current PayPal user agreement (unknown, verify). A band clause such as "re-price if JPY/USD moves more than 5%" must be agreed in writing.
- **Why people miss it:** People quote the yen figure they want at today's rate and forget the roughly 7% that goes to fees and conversion.
- **What we may say:** Arithmetic and general options are fine. Exchange-rate forecasts or telling the user to hedge with financial products is investment advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies directly.
- **Sources:** PayPal Japan fee page (as above), https://www.paypal.com/jp/business/paypal-business-fees. The arithmetic is our own.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for fee inputs. The surcharge rule is unknown; verify.

### Hold the foreign-currency balance or convert, and how it is taxed in Japan (外貨のまま保有か円転か)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone holding a USD balance at PayPal or in a foreign-currency account.
- **What it is:** Holding USD postpones the conversion cost and keeps money ready for USD expenses such as software subscriptions. But the balance is exposed to the exchange rate and earns no interest at PayPal. For Japanese income tax, a foreign-currency sale is booked in yen at the TTM rate on the transaction date (other consistent methods are allowed). Gains or losses from later rate moves are a separate item, generally treated as miscellaneous income in practice.
- **Value:** conversion saved = (USD spent directly in USD) × 3.00%. Exchange result = USD held × (rate at conversion − TTM at booking). Example: $5,000 booked at TTM ¥150 = ¥750,000 of sales; converted later at ¥145 base (≈ ¥140.65 after 3%) = ¥703,250. Part of that ¥46,750 gap is fee and part is an exchange loss, and the tax treatment differs.
- **Time to money:** days to months
- **Risk:** market (currency)
- **Effort:** minutes per conversion; ongoing bookkeeping
- **Deadline or season:** year end (Dec 31) for booking exchange results
- **Traps and caveats:** Holding is not a hedge; it is a bet on the rate. Many small withdrawals under ¥50,000 each cost ¥250, so batch them. Check whether your account converts incoming USD automatically (account setting; verify). Holding does not delay tax: the income counts when earned, not when converted.
- **Why people miss it:** People think income is counted only when the money reaches the yen bank.
- **What we may say:** We may show the user's balance, the TTM-based income figure and their conversion costs. Advising when to convert or predicting rates is investment advice. The exact tax classification belongs to a 税理士.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies directly.
- **Sources:** NTA, 所得税基本通達 57の3 (外貨建取引の換算, TTM principle), https://www.nta.go.jp/law/tsutatsu/kihon/shotoku/11a/01.htm. NTA Tax College paper on individuals' exchange gains (2025, 雑所得 treatment in practice; Tokyo District Court 2023-03-09), https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/kenkyu/ronsou/114/01/index.htm. NTA Q&A on foreign-currency deposits used to buy assets, https://www.nta.go.jp/law/shitsugi/shotoku/02/40.htm. PayPal Japan fee page (as above).
- **Confidence:** verified primary for the TTM rule. Secondary (NTA research paper plus a court case) for classification of exchange gains.

### Compare receiving routes on all-in cost (multi-currency accounts) (受取経路の比較)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Freelancers whose clients can pay by bank transfer (ACH/wire) as well as PayPal.
- **What it is:** Multi-currency accounts and local-currency receiving details let a client pay by domestic transfer in their own country, and you convert at a published markup. Japanese banks receive SWIFT wires with lifting charges, intermediary-bank fees and TT spreads. PayPal costs more per payment but includes buyer and seller dispute handling. The right comparison is the all-in percentage on your typical invoice size.
- **Value:** all-in % = (fixed fees + % fees + FX markup × amount) ÷ amount, per route. Example: PayPal Japan on $1,000 ≈ 7.0% (row above). For other routes, plug in their published fee pages. Not computed here.
- **Time to money:** weeks (setup and client switch)
- **Risk:** low (provider and account-freeze risk; keep more than one route)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Japanese bank TT spreads are commonly ±¥1 per USD around TTM at major banks (unknown, verify per bank). Wires add receiving fees of a few thousand yen at some banks (verify). Account freezes and KYC reviews happen at every provider. Some clients' accounts-payable teams pay only by wire.
- **Why people miss it:** The first route a client offered becomes permanent.
- **What we may say:** Factual comparison using each provider's own fee page, with the user's numbers. Naming one as "best" is a commercial claim; in Japan, introducing or brokering a money-transfer service may touch 資金移動業 rules (verify).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies directly.
- **Sources:** PayPal Japan fee page (as above). Bank TT spread practice and other providers' fees: not verified today.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for PayPal only. Other providers unknown; verify.

### Double taxation basics: treaty first, credit second (二重課税と外国税額控除)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Anyone taxed at home on worldwide income who also has tax withheld abroad.
- **What it is:** Tax residents of Japan pay Japanese tax on worldwide income. Tax treaties usually let only the residence country tax business profits unless you have a fixed base or permanent establishment in the other country. If foreign tax is withheld anyway, you first try to get it refunded abroad. Otherwise you claim a foreign tax credit (外国税額控除) at home, up to a limit.
- **Value:** credit = min(foreign tax paid, Japanese tax × foreign-source income ÷ total income). Example: if a US payer wrongly withheld $480, it is recoverable either from the IRS (US return) or partly as a Japanese credit, depending on the limit.
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** Japanese return, Feb 16 – Mar 15. Refund claims: 5 years.
- **Traps and caveats:** The credit does not cover tax that should not have been withheld under the treaty, so the IRS route may be the only one; verify. Residents of two countries need tie-breaker rules.
- **Why people miss it:** Withholding looks final.
- **What we may say:** General rules and the user's own numbers. Deciding treaty eligibility or preparing the claim is tax-agent work in Japan.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** With a W-8BEN on file, withholding should be zero. This row is the fallback.
- **Sources:** IRS, Japan tax treaty documents, https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/japan-tax-treaty-documents (not re-opened today; verify). NTA タックスアンサー No.1240 外国税額控除, https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shotoku/1240.htm (not re-opened today; verify).
- **Confidence:** secondary only (rules stated from known primary documents, not re-read today)

### VAT, GST or consumption tax on exported services (輸出免税・国外取引)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Freelancers selling services to clients abroad, especially those registered for consumption tax or VAT.
- **What it is:** Most VAT and GST systems do not charge tax on services sold to foreign businesses. They are zero-rated (exempt with input credit) or outside the scope, and in the EU the business customer often self-accounts under the reverse charge. In Japan, services to non-residents are generally export-exempt (輸出免税). Some items are excluded, such as services consumed in Japan (transport, lodging, Japanese real estate). Services delivered online may count as out-of-scope (国外取引) instead.
- **Value:** consumption tax not charged = invoice × 10% (Japan standard rate). Example: a ¥1,500,000 annual overseas design income carries no ¥150,000 consumption-tax line. For registered (課税) businesses, the classification also affects how much input tax you can recover.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** low (misclassification)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** consumption tax return, by Mar 31 for individuals
- **Traps and caveats:** The ¥10 million exempt-business threshold is tested on taxable sales in the base period. Export-exempt sales count toward it, while truly out-of-scope sales do not (verify for your case). Registering as an invoice issuer (インボイス登録) makes you taxable even below ¥10 million. Clients with a branch in Japan can turn the sale taxable. From Oct 1, 2026, real-estate brokerage for non-residents is taxable.
- **Why people miss it:** "Export" sounds like physical goods.
- **What we may say:** General rules and the user's figures. Classifying a specific contract is tax advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This applies directly. US clients generally owe no Japanese consumption tax on your invoice.
- **Sources:** NTA タックスアンサー No.6567 非居住者に対する役務の提供, https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shohi/6567.htm. NTA No.6551 輸出取引の免税, https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shohi/6551.htm. NTA 国境を越えた役務の提供に係る消費税 Q&A (rev. June 2026), https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/pdf/cross-QA.pdf. NTA No.6501 納税義務の免除 (¥10m threshold; invoice registrants not exempt; as of 2026-04-01), https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shohi/6501.htm.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for the rules. The threshold treatment of export-exempt versus out-of-scope sales is from the statute's structure; verify.

### Deposits and milestone billing (着手金・分割請求)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** Anyone doing project work longer than a few weeks.
- **What it is:** Ask for a share up front (often 30–50%) and bill the rest at milestones instead of at the end. This cuts the money at risk if a client vanishes and smooths cash flow. It also reveals early whether a client pays at all.
- **Value:** maximum loss if the client never pays = contract × (1 − deposit %). Example: a $4,000 project with a 40% deposit caps the loss at $2,400 instead of $4,000. Exchange-rate risk also shrinks because part of the money arrives sooner.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** low (some clients refuse)
- **Effort:** minutes per contract
- **Deadline or season:** at contract
- **Traps and caveats:** Each partial payment pays its own PayPal fixed fee (US$0.30 each), which is small. Write down what happens to the deposit if the client cancels. Refunding a deposit does not return PayPal's fee.
- **Why people miss it:** Fear of seeming pushy with a new client.
- **What we may say:** Generic contract practice and the user's own numbers. Drafting enforceable contract terms is legal advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This matters more across borders, because chasing a foreign client in court is impractical.
- **Sources:** PayPal Japan fee page (fixed fee), https://www.paypal.com/jp/business/paypal-business-fees. Typical deposit shares: anecdotal (industry practice, no primary source).
- **Confidence:** verified primary for fees. Anecdotal for typical deposit percentages.

### Late fees and interest on invoices: what is enforceable (遅延損害金)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** Freelancers invoicing businesses.
- **What it is:** Several jurisdictions give businesses statutory interest on late B2B invoices even if the contract is silent. UK: 8% above Bank of England base rate, plus fixed recovery costs. EU: the reference rate plus 8 points, plus €40. Japan: the statutory rate is 3% a year (held at 3% for Apr 2026 – Mar 2029), and a contract can set a higher late-payment rate. In the US, enforceability depends on the contract and state law.
- **Value:** interest = invoice × annual rate × days late ÷ 365. Example (Japan statutory 3%): ¥300,000 paid 60 days late gives ¥1,479. The fee is small, so its main value is as a stated deterrent.
- **Time to money:** weeks to months
- **Risk:** low (relationship cost)
- **Effort:** minutes (one line on the invoice template)
- **Deadline or season:** put the term in before work starts
- **Traps and caveats:** Clauses not agreed in the contract are hard to enforce in most places. Japan's Freelance Act (in force Nov 1, 2024) makes commissioning businesses pay within 60 days of receiving the work. Whether it reaches a client abroad is unknown; verify with the JFTC. UK recovery costs are £40, £70 or £100 by debt size (not re-opened today; verify).
- **Why people miss it:** Freelancers assume interest needs a lawyer.
- **What we may say:** General statutory rates and arithmetic. Demand letters or legal action are legal services.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** For US clients, only the contract term realistically applies. Put the rate and the payment window in the PayPal invoice terms.
- **Sources:** Ministry of Justice, 法定利率 (3% for 2026-04-01 to 2029-03-31; base ratio 0.4%), https://www.moj.go.jp/MINJI/minji07_00366.html. GOV.UK, charging interest on commercial debt (8% + base rate), https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/charging-interest-commercial-debt. EU Directive 2011/7/EU (reference rate + 8 points; €40), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/7/oj (not re-opened today; verify). Japan Freelance Act, 60-day rule and FY2025 enforcement (1,135 payment-deadline cases among 1,542 guidance cases, per secondary summary): MHLW/JFTC leaflets, e.g. https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/kyoso_seisaku/20250909003.pdf (not re-opened today; verify).
- **Confidence:** verified primary for Japan's 3% and the UK 8% + base. Secondary for EU figures and Freelance Act enforcement counts.

### The cost of a late client, and getting paid faster (支払遅延のコストと早期回収)

- **Group:** get paid better
- **Who it is for:** Anyone invoicing on terms.
- **What it is:** A late invoice costs interest (you carry costs or borrow meanwhile), exchange-rate drift, and your time chasing. Reminders before and on the due date work at almost no cost. Early-payment discounts work but are expensive money: "2/10 net 30" means giving 2% to be paid 20 days sooner, about 37% a year.
- **Value:** late cost = invoice × your borrowing or opportunity rate × days late ÷ 365 + chasing hours × hourly rate ± exchange drift. Example: a $3,000 invoice 45 days late, while you carry a card balance at 15%, costs $55 in interest; 3 hours of chasing at $40/h adds $120, for about $175 (5.8%) before any exchange move. Discount cost = d ÷ (1 − d) × 365 ÷ (days paid sooner). 2/10 net 30 → 0.02/0.98 × 365/20 ≈ 37.2% a year.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (automated reminders)
- **Deadline or season:** set reminders at due date −3, 0, +7 and +14 days
- **Traps and caveats:** Offer a discount only if your alternative funding costs more than its annualised rate. Fake "overdue invoice" emails are a common phishing route, so confirm requests on known channels.
- **Why people miss it:** Delay feels free because nothing is charged.
- **What we may say:** Calculations on the user's invoices. Lending or factoring offers would be credit intermediation.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Exchange drift is extra and works in either direction. PayPal invoices can carry due dates and payment terms.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic is our own. Interest rate input example: Fed G.19 (US card APR 22.36%, Aug 2026), https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for the rate input. Formulas are standard arithmetic.

## B. Universal moves people underestimate

### Emergency fund sized for irregular income (生活防衛資金)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Everyone, and doubly anyone with variable income.
- **What it is:** Cash kept for surprises so you never borrow at card rates. With irregular income, the fund also covers slow months. A practical design keeps three pots: the tax set-aside (not yours), the buffer, and a "salary" you pay yourself each month from a holding balance.
- **Value:** target = essential monthly spending × months (commonly 3–6; more for variable income, which is a rule of thumb, not sourced). Example: ¥250,000 essentials × 6 = ¥1.5 million, held apart from the tax reserve. In the US, 63% of adults would cover a $400 surprise with cash, flat since 2022, so about 1 in 3 could not.
- **Time to money:** months (to build)
- **Risk:** behavioural (raiding it for non-emergencies)
- **Effort:** an evening, then ongoing automation
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Counting the tax set-aside as emergency money: in Japan, resident tax and 予定納税 arrive months after the income. Holding the buffer in volatile assets. Over-saving cash while carrying 15–22% card debt.
- **Why people miss it:** "I'll use the card" feels like a plan.
- **What we may say:** Showing the user's months of runway is information. Recommending specific deposit or investment products crosses into advice or intermediation.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Hold the buffer in your spending currency (yen). Late USD payments and exchange swings add to the variability.
- **Sources:** Federal Reserve, SHED data viz, unexpected expenses table (2025: 63%), https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/sheddataviz/unexpectedexpenses-table.html.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for the 63% figure. The month counts are a rule of thumb.

### Pay yourself first, automatically (先取り貯蓄・自動積立)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Everyone with any surplus.
- **What it is:** Move a fixed amount to savings the day income lands, before spending. Defaults beat intentions: when a US employer made 401(k) enrolment automatic, participation among new hires rose from 37% to 86%. "Save More Tomorrow", which commits future raises to saving, raised savings rates from 3.5% to 13.6% over about 40 months.
- **Value:** saved per year = transfer × 12. The value lies in the gap between what people plan and what they actually save. Example: ¥30,000 a month is ¥360,000 a year.
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** behavioural (set it too high, then reverse it)
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** set it on payday
- **Traps and caveats:** With irregular income, use a percentage of each receipt rather than a fixed sum. Do not automate into products with high fees or lock-ins (see insurance-as-investment).
- **Why people miss it:** People wait to see what is left at month end.
- **What we may say:** Describing the mechanism and suggesting a transfer amount based on the user's cash flow is information. Choosing the investment is regulated.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Trigger the transfer on each converted payout, not on a calendar date.
- **Sources:** Madrian & Shea (2001), "The Power of Suggestion", QJE 116(4), https://doi.org/10.1162/003355301753265543. Thaler & Benartzi (2004), "Save More Tomorrow", JPE 112(S1), https://doi.org/10.1086/380085 (figures from the papers' abstracts; not re-opened today).
- **Confidence:** secondary only (peer-reviewed studies cited from known abstracts; verify the exact figures)

### Compounding, shown honestly: real returns and fees (複利の正しい見方)

- **Group:** invest & cash
- **Who it is for:** Anyone saving for more than 5 years.
- **What it is:** Compounding is real but slow. It should be shown after inflation and after fees, which most charts skip. A 1-point annual fee looks small but removes a large share of the end result over decades.
- **Value:** FV = monthly amount × ((1 + r/12)^(12 × years) − 1) ÷ (r/12), with r = return − inflation − fees. Example: ¥30,000 a month for 30 years (¥10.8m paid in) grows to about ¥17.5m at 3% real, or about ¥14.8m at 2% real. One point of fees costs about ¥2.7m (15%) of the result.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market
- **Effort:** minutes to understand
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Charts using 7% nominal with no inflation and no fees. Past returns are not promised. Sequence risk near withdrawal.
- **Why people miss it:** Fees are shown as small percentages, never as money lost.
- **What we may say:** Neutral calculations with assumptions the user can change. Recommending products or forecasting returns is investment advice (in Japan, 投資助言 needs registration under 金商法).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Currency is another drag or boost if savings are held in USD.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic is our own (standard annuity formula).
- **Confidence:** standard arithmetic. Return inputs are illustrative.

### Pay down high-interest debt first (高金利債務の繰上げ返済)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone carrying a card, revolving or consumer-loan balance.
- **What it is:** Each unit of high-interest debt repaid "earns" its interest rate with certainty and after tax. Few investments beat a guaranteed 15–22%. Paying the highest rate first (avalanche) costs least. Paying the smallest balance first (snowball) costs more but keeps some people going.
- **Value:** interest avoided per year ≈ balance × APR. Example: US$5,000 at 22.36% is about US$1,118 a year. ¥300,000 at 15% (typical Japanese revolving rate; verify per card) is about ¥45,000 a year.
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening, then ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Keep a small emergency buffer so the card is not used again. Debt-consolidation offers can stretch the term and cost more. "Debt relief" firms charging upfront fees are a known scam.
- **Why people miss it:** Minimum payments make the cost invisible.
- **What we may say:** Showing interest cost and payoff timelines is information. Arranging refinancing is credit intermediation (Japan: 貸金業法; US: state licensing).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** A lump of foreign income arriving is the natural moment to clear a balance.
- **Sources:** Federal Reserve G.19, released 2026-10-07 (credit card plans, accounts assessed interest: 22.36%, Aug 2026; all accounts 21.19%), https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm. Japan revolving rate: secondary only.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for the US figure. Secondary for Japan's 15%.

### Recurring-bill reset: negotiate, re-quote, cancel and re-subscribe (固定費の見直し)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with insurance, phone, internet, streaming or software plans.
- **What it is:** Prices drift up for loyal customers. A yearly round of re-quoting insurance, switching or threatening to switch phone and internet, and cancelling subscriptions you would not buy again today removes money every month. Cancelling often triggers a retention offer, and re-subscribing later often comes with an intro price.
- **Value:** annual saving = Σ (current monthly price − new monthly price) × 12. Example: ¥2,000 off phone + ¥1,500 off internet + ¥2,500 of unused subscriptions = ¥72,000 a year.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** low (contract-break fees, lost loyalty perks)
- **Effort:** an evening a year
- **Deadline or season:** contract renewal month. Insurance re-quote 3–4 weeks before renewal.
- **Traps and caveats:** Early-termination fees. Cutting insurance cover when it should be re-priced. "Free trial" sign-ups that roll into paid plans (see D). Typical saving percentages from surveys: unknown, verify (only vendor and blog figures found).
- **Why people miss it:** Each bill is small and on autopay.
- **What we may say:** Listing the user's recurring charges and totals is information. Selling or comparing insurance products is insurance intermediation (Japan: 保険業法 募集).
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Software subscriptions billed in USD carry card FX fees. Paying them from a USD balance can avoid conversion.
- **Sources:** No primary data on negotiation success rates was found. Anecdotal.
- **Confidence:** anecdotal (mechanism only; no verified success-rate figure)

### The "free money" test: matches and government top-ups (マッチング拠出・所得控除)

- **Group:** tax
- **Who it is for:** Employees with employer matches; self-employed people with tax-deductible pension or mutual-aid schemes; anyone eligible for government co-contributions.
- **What it is:** If someone else adds money, or the tax system refunds part of what you put in, the instant return beats almost any investment. Test each scheme: what share of my contribution comes back at once, and what lock-in or loss of access do I accept for it?
- **Value:** instant return = (match + tax saved) ÷ own contribution. Example (UK): employer minimum 3% against employee 5% is 60% instantly before tax relief. Japan example: a deductible contribution of ¥840,000 a year to the small-business mutual-aid scheme (小規模企業共済), at a combined 30% marginal rate (income + resident tax), saves about ¥252,000 (verify the contribution limits).
- **Time to money:** months (tax refund) or immediate (match)
- **Risk:** low (lock-in; early-exit penalties in some schemes)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** tax-year end (Dec 31 in Japan; Apr 5 in the UK). Enrolment windows.
- **Traps and caveats:** Lock-ins (iDeCo cannot be withdrawn before 60). Early cancellation of mutual-aid schemes can return less than paid in. Deduction value depends on bracket, so a low earner gains less.
- **Why people miss it:** The schemes are spread across agencies and the benefit only appears at tax time.
- **What we may say:** Showing the user's estimated tax saving is information. Selecting products inside iDeCo is investment advice. Tax filing is tax-agent work.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Self-employed people in Japan have no employer match, so the deductions are the match. Details belong in the Japan file.
- **Sources:** GOV.UK, workplace pensions (employer 3%, total 8%, from Apr 2019), https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/what-you-your-employer-and-the-government-pay. SMRJ, 小規模企業共済 overview (limits not confirmed on fetched page), https://www.smrj.go.jp/kyosai/skyosai/about/index.html.
- **Confidence:** verified primary (UK). Japan contribution limit secondary; verify.

### Card protections you already pay for (カード付帯補償)

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** Credit card holders.
- **What it is:** Many cards include purchase protection (theft or damage soon after purchase), extended warranty, travel insurance and sometimes phone insurance. People buy the same cover again at the till. Price-protection benefits were cut by several US issuers around 2018–2019, so check what your card still offers.
- **Value:** value = premium of the duplicate cover avoided + claims made. Example: skipping a ¥5,000 extended warranty that the card already provides.
- **Time to money:** weeks (claims)
- **Risk:** low (claim denied on exclusions)
- **Effort:** an evening to read the benefit guide once
- **Deadline or season:** claim windows are often 60–120 days from purchase (varies; verify per card)
- **Traps and caveats:** Cover often needs the item bought on that card. Travel cover may require the trip to be paid on the card (利用付帯 vs 自動付帯 in Japan). Benefits change without much notice.
- **Why people miss it:** Benefit guides are long PDFs nobody reads.
- **What we may say:** Pointing the user to their card's benefit guide is information. Recommending a card for its insurance is financial or insurance intermediation.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:** No issuer-wide primary data found. Per-card benefit guides.
- **Confidence:** anecdotal (the mechanism is real; figures vary by card)

### Legal warranty rights people pay to duplicate (法定保証・契約不適合責任)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Consumers buying durable goods.
- **What it is:** The law already makes sellers responsible for defects. In the EU you always get at least 2 years' legal guarantee. If a defect shows within 1 year, the seller must prove it was not there at delivery. Commercial warranties and paid extended warranties cannot reduce this. Similar rights exist under Australian consumer guarantees, the UK Consumer Rights Act, US implied warranties under state law, and Japan's civil-code liability for non-conforming goods (契約不適合責任).
- **Value:** saving = price of the extended warranty not bought + repairs claimed from the seller. Example: €120 for a "3-year protection plan" on a laptop that already has 2 years of legal cover.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** low
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** EU: within 2 years of delivery. Japan: notify the seller within 1 year of discovering a defect (民法566条; verify for consumer cases).
- **Traps and caveats:** Sellers redirect you to the manufacturer. Legal-guarantee scope differs from "accidental damage" cover. Second-hand goods can have shorter periods in some EU states.
- **Why people miss it:** Shops sell extended plans at the till and never mention the legal guarantee.
- **What we may say:** General description of statutory rights is information. Advising on a specific dispute is legal advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** For work equipment bought abroad, rights follow the seller's jurisdiction.
- **Sources:** European Commission, Your Europe, guarantees and returns (2-year minimum; 1-year reversed burden; commercial guarantee cannot reduce rights), last checked 2026-10-05, https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/guarantees-returns/index_en.htm. Japan 民法566条 via e-Gov (not re-opened today; verify).
- **Confidence:** verified primary (EU). Secondary for other countries.

### Sell or rent out what you already own (不用品の売却・シェアリング)

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with unused electronics, gear, clothes, a parking space or a spare room.
- **What it is:** Idle goods lose value every month they sit. Selling early turns them into cash at the best price. Renting out (equipment, parking, a car on a sharing platform) creates income without new capital.
- **Value:** cash = resale price − platform fee − shipping. Value of selling now rather than later = price × monthly depreciation × months. Example: a camera that resells for ¥60,000 now but loses about 2% a month (illustrative) loses ¥14,400 if kept a year unused.
- **Time to money:** days
- **Risk:** low (fraud on marketplaces; damage when renting)
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** before new-model launches for electronics
- **Traps and caveats:** In Japan, selling personal-use goods is generally tax-free, but selling as a business, or selling valuables over ¥300,000 each, can be taxable (verify). Rental income is taxable. Check lease and insurance rules before renting out a room or car. Overpayment scams on marketplaces are common.
- **Why people miss it:** Sunk-cost attachment ("I paid a lot for it").
- **What we may say:** Estimates from public resale data are information. Tax treatment of business-like selling is tax advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Old work equipment may be a business asset. If it was depreciated, its sale is business income, not tax-free personal sales; verify.
- **Sources:** Depreciation rate illustrative. Japan tax treatment: not re-verified today.
- **Confidence:** anecdotal for rates; tax treatment unknown, verify

### Skill-based side income with no capital (スキル副業)

- **Group:** quick money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone with a teachable or sellable skill (language, design, tutoring, translation, code).
- **What it is:** Selling hours or small productised services needs no money up front, only time. The underrated version is productising: templates, fixed-price packages, or teaching what you already do.
- **Value:** net = hours × rate × (1 − platform fee %) − tax at your marginal rate. Example: 4 h/week × ¥3,000/h × 48 weeks = ¥576,000 gross, before fees and tax.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** behavioural (burnout; it can crowd out higher-value main work)
- **Effort:** ongoing
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Employment contracts may ban side work. In Japan, side income over ¥200,000 for salaried people triggers filing (verify current rules). "Courses that teach you to make money online" are a common scam.
- **Why people miss it:** People think side income needs a product or capital.
- **What we may say:** Calculating effective hourly rate is information. Platform recommendations should be neutral.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Raising rates or adding a productised offer for existing USD clients usually beats a new side job.
- **Sources:** No primary source; arithmetic only.
- **Confidence:** anecdotal

### Buy used for depreciating goods, and time big purchases (中古購入と購入タイミング)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone buying cars, phones, laptops, furniture or cameras.
- **What it is:** Goods that lose value fast lose most of it early, so the second owner pays far less for most of the useful life. Timing helps too: model changeovers, fiscal year-end sales (March in Japan), and buying off-season.
- **Value:** saving = new price − used price − extra repair risk. Example: a 2-year-old car bought for 70% of its new price (illustrative; actual depreciation curves unknown, verify) saves 30%.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** low (hidden defects; seller fraud)
- **Effort:** a weekend
- **Deadline or season:** model changeover; fiscal year-end
- **Traps and caveats:** Some private sales give fewer legal rights than buying from a dealer. Safety-critical items (helmets, car seats) should be bought new.
- **Why people miss it:** Status, and fear of hidden defects.
- **What we may say:** General information. Financing offers on purchases are credit intermediation.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Business equipment bought used also changes depreciation for tax (Japan uses a shorter useful life for used assets; verify).
- **Sources:** Car depreciation figures: unknown, verify (AAA "Your Driving Costs" is the usual US source; not opened).
- **Confidence:** anecdotal

### Libraries, community schemes and shared resources (図書館・公共サービスの活用)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Everyone.
- **What it is:** Public libraries lend books, magazines, audiobooks, and often software, databases, tools and study space for free. Municipal programmes cover sports facilities, tool libraries and free legal or tax consultation days. Many paid subscriptions duplicate what the library already provides.
- **Value:** saving = Σ (price of item or service × times used). Example: 2 books + 1 magazine a month at about ¥3,000 is ¥36,000 a year.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Availability varies by municipality. Waiting lists.
- **Why people miss it:** People picture libraries as only paper books.
- **What we may say:** Information.

## C. Unclaimed money, everywhere

### Dormant bank accounts and unclaimed property (休眠預金・未請求財産)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone who has moved, changed jobs or banks, or inherited.
- **What it is:** Money left untouched (deposits, final paychecks, refunds, utility deposits, uncashed checks) is moved to a government holder after a set period. You can usually still claim it. Search is free on official tools.
- **Value:** amount found (varies). Scale: US states returned over $4.49 billion in FY2024. Australia's ASIC held A$2.7 billion in unclaimed money (May 2026). In Japan, deposits untouched for 10+ years (last transaction from Jan 2009) move to the Deposit Insurance Corporation, and you can still withdraw them through your bank with no deadline.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes (search), an evening (claim)
- **Deadline or season:** none for Japan's 休眠預金 (no deadline). Varies elsewhere.
- **Traps and caveats:** "Finder" firms charging a percentage for what you can claim free. Phishing sites imitating official searches.
- **Why people miss it:** People don't know that holders other than banks (utilities, employers) also send money to the state.
- **What we may say:** Linking to official search tools is information. Claiming on the user's behalf may be regulated agency work.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Old PayPal or foreign bank balances are not covered by Japan's scheme. Check the foreign country's own tool.
- **Official search tools:** US: MissingMoney.com (multi-state, NAUPA-endorsed) and https://unclaimed.org; savings bonds: TreasuryDirect "Treasury Hunt". UK: https://www.mylostaccount.org.uk (not re-opened today; verify). Canada: Bank of Canada Unclaimed Properties, https://www.bankofcanada.ca/unclaimed-properties/ (not re-opened today; verify). Australia: Moneysmart unclaimed money search (ASIC). Japan: no central search; ask each bank (FSA explainer).
- **Sources:** NAUPA (FY2024 returned >$4.49bn, via unclaimed.org), https://unclaimed.org/. ASIC media release (A$2.7bn unclaimed, 2026), https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/australians-reminded-to-check-for-lost-money-as-2-7-billion-sits-unclaimed/. FSA, 休眠預金等活用法 (10 years; no withdrawal deadline), https://www.fsa.go.jp/policy/kyuminyokin/kyuminyokin.html.
- **Confidence:** verified primary (FSA, ASIC). Secondary for the NAUPA total (seen in search summary). Japan's annual transfer amount (≈¥169.4bn FY2024) comes from a blog only, so it is anecdotal; verify with DICJ.

### Lost pensions and retirement accounts (年金記録・消えた年金)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Anyone who has had several employers, used old names, or worked abroad.
- **What it is:** Old workplace plans and unlinked pension records are a large pool of forgotten money. Japan still has about 16.52 million pension records with no identified owner (Mar 2026), searchable on ねんきんネット by name and birth date. Australia holds A$18.9 billion of lost and unclaimed super (Jun 30, 2025). The US Department of Labor opened a Retirement Savings Lost and Found database in Dec 2024.
- **Value:** amount found plus the lifetime pension increase. Example: an unlinked 2-year employee-pension record raises a lifetime annuity (amount depends on salary; compute with ねんきんネット).
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none for searching. Back-payment limits apply (Japan: pension claims generally lapse after 5 years per payment; verify).
- **Traps and caveats:** Pension "recovery" scams. US Lost and Found is incomplete (it relies on plan data) and needs Login.gov.
- **Why people miss it:** Old records sit under maiden names, misspelled katakana, or past employers.
- **What we may say:** Information and links.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Past work in the US, UK or Australia may have left pension credits. Japan's totalisation agreements can count periods across countries (verify per country).
- **Official search tools:** Japan: ねんきんネット 持ち主不明記録検索, https://www.nenkin.go.jp/denshibenri_kojin/n_net/utilization/unknownrecord.html. US: DOL EBSA Retirement Savings Lost and Found (launched 2024-12-27). UK: Pension Tracing Service, https://www.gov.uk/find-pension-contact-details. Australia: ATO lost super via myGov.
- **Sources:** 日本年金機構, 未統合記録 (≈16.52m remaining, Mar 2026), https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/yougo/magyo/mitogokiroku.html. ATO, "$18.9 billion lost and unclaimed super", https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/for-superannuation-professionals/super-funds-newsroom/18-9-billion-lost-and-unclaimed-super. DOL launch: secondary (PlanSponsor, Dec 2024), https://plansponsor.com/dol-launches-database-for-retirement-savings-lost-and-found.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for Japan and ATO (seen via search result text from official domains). Secondary for DOL.

### Tax refunds you never filed for (還付申告)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** People with tax withheld who did not file: part-year workers, students, people with medical costs, and side earners with withholding.
- **What it is:** Many tax systems refund over-withheld tax only if you file. The US gives 3 years: about $1.2 billion of 2022 refunds (1.3 million people, median $686) became Treasury property after Apr 15, 2026. Japan allows a refund-only return for 5 years from Jan 1 of the following year, so the 2021 tax year can still be claimed until Dec 31, 2026.
- **Value:** refund = tax withheld − tax due. Example: in Japan, a 10.21% withholding on a ¥300,000 domestic freelance job (¥30,630) may be partly refundable if total income is low.
- **Time to money:** weeks to months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** Japan: 2021 tax year by Dec 31, 2026. US: tax year 2023 by Apr 15, 2027.
- **Traps and caveats:** Special elections such as the 65万円 blue-return deduction still require filing by the normal deadline. Refund scams that ask for bank details by SMS.
- **Why people miss it:** "I didn't have to file" is mistaken for "I can't get money back".
- **What we may say:** Flagging a possible refund from the user's data is information. Preparing the return is tax-agent work in Japan.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** USD-paid work has no Japanese withholding, but Japanese clients withhold 10.21% on design fees, so mixed income can mean a refund.
- **Sources:** IRS IR-2026-37 (2026-03-20), https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/time-is-running-out-to-claim-1-point-2-billion-in-refunds-for-tax-year-2022-taxpayers-face-april-15-deadline. NTA タックスアンサー No.2030 還付申告, https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shotoku/2030.htm. NTA Q&A, https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/shinkoku/qa/02.htm.
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### Benefits with low take-up (申請主義の給付)

- **Group:** insurance & benefits
- **Who it is for:** Low and middle earners, carers, parents, people with high medical costs, and people after a job loss.
- **What it is:** Many benefits are paid only on application, and studies across OECD countries repeatedly find large shares of eligible people never claim. Examples in Japan include the high-cost medical expense refund (高額療養費, claimable for 2 years) and national-pension premium exemptions (国民年金保険料免除). The UK has Pension Credit and the US has the EITC.
- **Value:** benefit × probability eligible. Example: in Japan, a hospital stay above the 高額療養費 ceiling is partly refunded if claimed.
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** 高額療養費: 2 years from the month after treatment (verify).
- **Traps and caveats:** Rules change yearly. Paid "benefit finder" services.
- **Why people miss it:** Stigma, complexity, and not knowing.
- **What we may say:** Eligibility screening against published rules is information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Freelancers in Japan are on 国民健康保険 and 国民年金, where exemptions and reductions apply on application during low-income years.
- **Sources:** Cross-country non-take-up estimates: OECD work exists but was not opened today (unknown, verify). UK Pension Credit take-up: unknown, verify.
- **Confidence:** secondary only. Figures unknown; verify.

### Class actions and regulator refunds (集団訴訟・行政による返金)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Consumers of any company that settled with a regulator or a class.
- **What it is:** Settlements set aside money for affected customers, sometimes paid automatically and sometimes only on a claim form. The US FTC sent $337.3 million in refunds in 2024 and lists open cases. Japan has a consumer-group collective redress system (消費者裁判手続特例法) with few cases.
- **Value:** settlement payout per claimant (varies; often small, sometimes hundreds of dollars).
- **Time to money:** months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes per claim
- **Deadline or season:** each settlement has its own claim deadline
- **Traps and caveats:** Fake settlement sites ask for card numbers; real ones never charge a fee. Only file claims you are honestly eligible for.
- **Why people miss it:** Notices look like spam.
- **What we may say:** Linking to official refund lists is information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** US settlements sometimes include non-US customers of US services; check the eligibility text.
- **Official search tools:** US: https://www.ftc.gov/refunds. Japan: 消費者庁 特定適格消費者団体 case list (not opened; verify).
- **Sources:** FTC press release (2024 refunds $337.3m), https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-report-shows-ftc-returned-3373-million-consumers-2024.
- **Confidence:** verified primary (FTC, via official release text in search results)

### Deposits, overpayments and insurance claims never made (敷金・過払い・保険金の請求漏れ)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Renters, anyone who closed a utility account, and anyone with insurance (including card-attached cover).
- **What it is:** Rental deposits, final utility credits and insurance benefits are often left unclaimed. In Japan the 2020 Civil Code requires landlords to return the deposit minus unpaid rent and tenant-caused damage; normal wear and tear is the landlord's cost. Insurance claims usually expire after 3 years under Japan's Insurance Act. Many people never claim hospital riders, card travel cover or phone damage cover.
- **Value:** deposit − justified deductions; overpayment refunds; claim amounts. Example: a ¥200,000 deposit with ¥80,000 of wear-and-tear charges is challengeable under the MLIT guideline.
- **Time to money:** weeks
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** insurance: 3 years from when the claim could be made (保険法95条; verify). Deposits: on move-out.
- **Traps and caveats:** Signing move-out cost sheets on the spot. Special clauses (特約) in leases can shift some costs if clearly agreed.
- **Why people miss it:** People assume deductions are standard and don't know claims run out.
- **What we may say:** Explaining the rules is information. Disputing a specific deposit is legal advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:** 民法622条の2 (敷金) and 保険法95条 via e-Gov; MLIT 原状回復ガイドライン (none re-opened today; verify).
- **Confidence:** secondary only (statutes cited from knowledge)

### Flight delay and cancellation compensation (航空便の遅延補償)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Flyers on routes covered by EU, UK or Canadian rules. US flyers for refunds.
- **What it is:** EU rules pay €250, €400 or €600 (by distance) for arrival delays of 3+ hours or short-notice cancellations, unless the cause was extraordinary. The UK copy uses similar amounts in pounds. Canada sets minimums for large airlines of C$400 (3–6 h), C$700 (6–9 h) and C$1,000 (9 h+). The US requires automatic cash refunds when a flight is cancelled or significantly changed and you don't travel. A proposed US delay-compensation rule was withdrawn in Nov 2025. Japan has no statutory compensation.
- **Value:** fixed amount per passenger by distance or delay. Example: a Paris–Tokyo flight arriving 4 hours late on an EU carrier: €600 per person (unless extraordinary circumstances).
- **Time to money:** weeks to months
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** national limitation periods (vary by EU country).
- **Traps and caveats:** Claim agencies take 25–35% (anecdotal); claiming directly is free. Weather and air-traffic-control causes are excluded. Taking a voucher can replace your cash right.
- **Why people miss it:** Airlines offer meal vouchers and say nothing about cash.
- **What we may say:** Information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Business trips to clients count the same.
- **Sources:** European Commission, Your Europe air passenger rights (last checked 2026-09-21), https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm. Canada, Air Passenger Protection Regulations s.19 (current to 2026-09-30), https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2019-150/section-19.html. US status: secondary (news, Nov 2025), e.g. https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/nov/17/transportation-department-ditches-rule-require-airlines-pay/.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for EU and Canada. Secondary for US status.

### Unused gift cards and expiring points (ギフトカード・ポイント失効)

- **Group:** unclaimed money
- **Who it is for:** Everyone.
- **What it is:** Unused balances are pure loss once they expire or the merchant fails. In the US, 43% of adults had at least one unused gift card, averaging $244 (Bankrate, Aug 2024). US federal rules bar gift-card expiry within 5 years of issue or last load and limit inactivity fees. Japan has no general expiry protection for loyalty points.
- **Value:** Σ balances × probability of use. Example: ¥8,000 in points expiring this quarter.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** check expiries quarterly
- **Traps and caveats:** Gift-card payment demands are a top scam signal; no agency or company takes payment in gift cards.
- **Why people miss it:** Balances are spread across many apps.
- **What we may say:** Information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:** Bankrate gift card survey 2024 (secondary; YouGov fieldwork Aug 19–21, 2024), https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/news/gift-cards-survey/. US Regulation E §1005.20 (5-year expiry floor; dormancy fees only after 12 months of inactivity) (eCFR not reachable today; verify).
- **Confidence:** secondary only

## D. Wealth destroyers across countries

### Revolving credit (リボ払い・クレジットカードの残高繰越)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone carrying a card balance or on revolving repayment.
- **What it is:** Revolving lets you pay a small fixed amount while interest accrues on the rest. At 15–22% a year, balances barely fall.
- **Value (cost):** annual interest ≈ balance × APR. Example: US$5,000 × 22.36% ≈ US$1,118 a year. US banks' rate on accounts charged interest was 22.36% (Aug 2026).
- **Time to money:** immediate saving once stopped
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Some Japanese cards default to リボ, or nudge with point bonuses. The one action that stops it: switch the card to pay-in-full (一括払い) and repay the revolving balance highest-APR first.
- **Why people miss it:** Fixed payments feel like a budget.
- **What we may say:** Showing interest cost is information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Do not use revolving credit to bridge late USD invoices; deposits and milestones do that more cheaply.
- **Sources:** Fed G.19 (2026-10-07), https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm.
- **Confidence:** verified primary (US). Japan rate secondary.

### Buy now, pay later stacking (後払い・BNPL)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** BNPL users.
- **What it is:** Splitting purchases into instalments, often interest-free, hides the total owed when several plans run at once. The CFPB found about 63% of BNPL borrowers held multiple simultaneous loans during 2022, and 33% used more than one lender. BNPL users were more likely to carry higher balances on other credit.
- **Value (cost):** late fees + overdraft knock-ons + extra spending caused. Example: unknown per user; track the total of open instalments against monthly free cash.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Many BNPL loans were not reported to credit bureaus, so lenders and you can't see the total. One action: keep a single list of all open instalments, and never take a new one while any is open.
- **Why people miss it:** Each instalment looks small.
- **What we may say:** Summing the user's obligations is information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:** CFPB press release (2025-01-13), https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-research-reveals-heavy-buy-now-pay-later-use-among-borrowers-with-high-credit-balances-and-multiple-pay-in-four-loans/.
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### Overdrafts and payday-style loans (当座貸越・短期高利ローン)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** People with thin buffers.
- **What it is:** Overdraft fees turn a small shortfall into a fee comparable to a large loan. Payday loans charge a typical $15 per $100 for two weeks, which the CFPB says works out to nearly 400% APR. US banks still earned $5.83 billion in overdraft and NSF fees in 2023, down 51% from 2019. The 2024 US overdraft rule was repealed by Congress in 2025 (secondary; verify).
- **Value (cost):** fee ÷ amount borrowed × 365 ÷ days. Example: $15 on $100 for 14 days ≈ 391% APR.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** Rollovers multiply fees. The one action: opt out of overdraft "coverage" for debit card purchases and build a one-week buffer.
- **Why people miss it:** The fee is presented as a service.
- **What we may say:** Information. Offering credit is regulated.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** A late client is the classic trigger; see deposits and milestone billing.
- **Sources:** CFPB, payday loan costs, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-are-the-costs-and-fees-for-a-payday-loan-en-1589/. CFPB data spotlight (2023 overdraft/NSF $5.83bn), https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/data-spotlight-overdraft-nsf-revenue-in-2023-down-more-than-50-versus-pre-pandemic-levels-saving-consumers-over-6-billion-annually/.
- **Confidence:** verified primary for fees and revenue. Secondary for the 2025 repeal.

### Insurance sold as investment (貯蓄型保険・外貨建て保険)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone sold whole-life, endowment or foreign-currency savings policies.
- **What it is:** These products bundle protection and saving. Commissions and charges are deducted early, so surrender values are often below premiums paid for years. Foreign-currency policies add exchange risk and conversion costs. Japan's FSA and consumer centres have repeatedly warned about mis-selling of foreign-currency insurance (figures not verified today).
- **Value (cost):** cost = premiums paid − surrender value at year N, compared with term insurance + separate saving. Example: unknown; compute from the policy's surrender-value table.
- **Time to money:** years
- **Risk:** market and behavioural
- **Effort:** an evening (read the surrender table)
- **Deadline or season:** before signing. Japan cooling-off period: 8 days (verify).
- **Traps and caveats:** Cancelling an old policy can lose more than keeping it. Compare the surrender value today against what continuing would produce. One action: never buy before seeing the surrender-value table year by year.
- **Why people miss it:** Sold as "saving", with tax deductions as the hook.
- **What we may say:** Explaining surrender tables is information. Telling a user to cancel a specific policy is insurance or financial advice.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** USD-denominated policies are often pitched to people with USD income.
- **Sources:** FSA and 国民生活センター warnings: not opened today (unknown, verify).
- **Confidence:** secondary only. Figures unknown.

### Lifestyle inflation (生活水準の上昇)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone whose income rises.
- **What it is:** Spending rises to meet each raise, so savings never grow. The quiet version is fixed costs (rent, car, subscriptions) that are hard to reverse.
- **Value (cost):** lost saving = raise × share spent × years. Example: a ¥50,000 monthly raise fully spent for 10 years is ¥6 million not saved.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** each raise or rate increase
- **Traps and caveats:** One action: auto-save at least half of each raise the month it starts ("Save More Tomorrow" logic).
- **Why people miss it:** Gradual increases don't feel like decisions.
- **What we may say:** Information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** A weak yen inflates yen income from USD clients. Treat FX-driven gains as temporary, not as a raise.
- **Sources:** Thaler & Benartzi (2004), https://doi.org/10.1086/380085.
- **Confidence:** secondary only (mechanism); the example is arithmetic

### Lottery and gambling (宝くじ・ギャンブル)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Everyone; it weighs most on lower incomes.
- **What it is:** Lotteries pay back far less than they take. Japanese law caps total prizes at 50% of sales (plus carry-overs), so the expected loss is at least half of every ticket. Research finds lottery spending crowds out other household spending and takes a larger income share in lower-income households.
- **Value (cost):** expected loss ≥ spend × (1 − payout ratio). Example: ¥3,000 a month on Japanese lotteries means an expected loss of at least ¥18,000 a year.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** year-end jumbo lottery season (Dec in Japan)
- **Traps and caveats:** Spending by income band: no primary per-band figure verified today (unknown). One action: redirect the same amount automatically into savings on the same day.
- **Why people miss it:** Small tickets, dreams of winning.
- **What we may say:** Information about payout ratios.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:** 当せん金付証票法 第5条 (prizes ≤ 50% of sales), e-Gov API, https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/323AC0000000144. Kearney (2005), "State lotteries and consumer behavior", Journal of Public Economics 89(11–12) (not re-opened today; verify).
- **Confidence:** verified primary (Japan cap). Secondary for the behavioural evidence.

### Bank and account fees (銀行手数料)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Everyone with a bank account.
- **What it is:** Maintenance fees, ATM fees, transfer fees, paper-statement fees, and fees on dormant accounts add up. Often a free alternative at the same bank exists (online banking tier, ATM rules).
- **Value (cost):** Σ (fee × times a year). Example: ¥220 ATM fee × 4 a month = ¥10,560 a year.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** One action: list every fee on the last 3 months of statements and switch to the bank's fee-free tier.
- **Why people miss it:** Fees are small and deducted silently.
- **What we may say:** Information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** Incoming wire fees, and PayPal's ¥250 fee on withdrawals under ¥50,000. Batch withdrawals.
- **Sources:** PayPal Japan (¥250 fee), https://www.paypal.com/jp/business/paypal-business-fees. Japanese bank ATM fee levels: anecdotal; verify per bank.
- **Confidence:** verified primary (PayPal). Anecdotal (bank fees).

### Exchange-rate spreads (為替スプレッド)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone converting currency: travellers, online shoppers, international earners.
- **What it is:** Most conversions hide a markup inside the exchange rate. The global average cost of sending $200 abroad was 6.49% in Q1 2025, against a UN target of 3%. Card networks, banks and wallets each add their own markup.
- **Value (cost):** amount × (markup % + fixed fees ÷ amount). Example: converting US$40,000 a year through PayPal's 3.00% markup costs US$1,200 a year in spread alone.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** none
- **Effort:** an evening
- **Deadline or season:** none
- **Traps and caveats:** "No fee" offers that carry the cost in a worse rate. Choosing your home currency at a foreign card terminal (dynamic currency conversion) usually adds another markup. One action: always compare the rate received against the mid-market rate on the same day.
- **Why people miss it:** There is no line item for it.
- **What we may say:** Calculating the user's realised spread is information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** This is the largest recurring leak after the receiving fee; see the all-in cost row.
- **Sources:** World Bank, Remittance Prices Worldwide Issue 53 (Q1 2025; global average 6.49%), https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/rpw_main_report_and_annex_q125_1_0.pdf. PayPal Japan (3.00% markup), as above.
- **Confidence:** verified primary

### Free-trial and subscription traps (定期購入・サブスクの罠)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Online shoppers.
- **What it is:** "Free" or ¥500 trials roll into full-price plans or multi-month commitments, and cancelling is made hard. Japan amended its Specified Commercial Transactions Act (from Jun 1, 2022) to require clear final-confirmation screens for subscription purchases and to let buyers cancel after misleading ones. The US FTC's "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated by a federal appeals court in July 2025, so US protection now rests on existing law (secondary; verify).
- **Value (cost):** monthly price × months until noticed. Example: ¥4,980 a month noticed after 5 months = ¥24,900.
- **Time to money:** immediate
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** set a cancel reminder the day you start a trial
- **Traps and caveats:** One action: start trials only with a calendar reminder, or with a card or virtual number you can cap. Report to your local consumer centre (Japan 188).
- **Why people miss it:** The commitment is in small print on the last screen.
- **What we may say:** Information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** USD software trials also carry FX fees.
- **Sources:** 消費者庁, 特定商取引法 2021 amendment (effective 2022-06-01) (not re-opened today; verify). US: Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC (8th Cir., July 2025) (secondary; verify). 国民生活センター 定期購入 consultation counts: page not reachable today (unknown, verify).
- **Confidence:** secondary only

### Multi-level marketing (マルチ商法・連鎖販売取引)

- **Group:** stop the leak
- **Who it is for:** Anyone approached to "start a business" by buying stock and recruiting.
- **What it is:** Income depends on recruiting and buying, not on selling to real customers. The FTC states that most people who join even legitimate MLMs make little or no money, and some lose money.
- **Value (cost):** startup kit + monthly purchase requirements + events and travel − commissions. Example: unknown per scheme; compute from the scheme's income disclosure if any.
- **Time to money:** immediate (stopping)
- **Risk:** behavioural
- **Effort:** minutes
- **Deadline or season:** Japan cooling-off for 連鎖販売取引: 20 days (verify).
- **Traps and caveats:** Pressure to buy inventory to "qualify". One action: ask for the income disclosure statement and the median earnings, and use the cooling-off period.
- **Why people miss it:** Offers come through friends and are framed as entrepreneurship.
- **What we may say:** Information.
- **Freelancer paid from abroad:** nothing
- **Sources:** FTC consumer advice, "Multi-Level Marketing Businesses and Pyramid Schemes" (Jul 2022), https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes. AARP 2018 MLM survey figures: not verified today (unknown).
- **Confidence:** verified primary (qualitative). No verified loss statistics.

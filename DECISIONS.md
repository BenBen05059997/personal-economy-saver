# Decisions

The current decisions, and only those. Other documents (the brief, PLAN.md, the hub artifact) describe the project; when they disagree with this file, this file wins and they get fixed.

How it is kept: when a decision changes, its row is rewritten to the new decision (or deleted if it no longer applies). Nothing old is kept. Claude updates this file in the same turn a decision is made, then fixes any document that still says the old thing.

Last updated: Nov 1, 2026.

### The product

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | We are entering the **PayPal AI Hackathon** (Devpost). The only date that matters: submit the **evening of Nov 12, Tokyo** (closes Nov 13 05:00 Tokyo = Nov 12 12:00 PT). No milestones. | The owner: milestones are useless; the final date is what counts. |
| D2 | **What it is:** a daily money assistant for freelancers paid across borders, built on PayPal. A **live planner and executor** that answers, with the user's real numbers: *Where am I?* *Can I proceed, or should I hold off?* *If I use credit, how do I pay it back from what's coming in?* Then it does the part it can through PayPal. | The owner's definition. Daily life, not abstract finance. |
| D3 | **The daily loop is the product.** Events from the inbox drive everything: a bill arrives; a card charge arrives (at home or travelling); a client pays; a card statement arrives; the runway shows a shortfall; a due date is near. Each shows what it means for the next 30 days and offers one action. | Help at the moment survives; lessons fade (research/users). |
| D4 | **Credit is part of "where am I".** Cards are typed in at setup (statement day, due day; no card login). Card-use emails and receipts become spend on that card; spend rolls into the coming statement, placed on the runway at its due date, with "covered / not covered" from expected income. "Can I proceed?" on a card is the room on that card: how much more it can take and still pay its statement and everything else known, in full, worst case, capped by the card limit. A cash shortfall on another day is shown separately and is not blamed on the card. | Travel and daily spending: users need to see what they've charged, when it's due, and whether incoming money covers it. |
| D5 | **Reminders, not lectures.** Before a due date: the amount, the date, whether it's covered, and one action (pay in full from the bank, or invoice first if short). | The owner: "just remind." |
| D6 | **Wealth moves are off the first screen.** The catalogue (Japan 20, US 16, verified) stays in the engine and appears only as one "what's next" line in a surplus month, never on the home screen for Nov 12. Job-change moves, subsidies, self-back, IPO lotteries and other regions are parked. | Research Nov 1 (`research/hackathon/what-wins.md`, `research/users/what-freelancers-want.md`): they blur the one-problem story for judges and users; loved products removed one uncertainty, not many. |
| D25 | **One screen, one sentence, one action.** Phone-first. Desktop is a normal web-app shell (research/ux/desktop-web-layouts.md, Mercury measured): a left sidebar of about 230px (Home, To check with a count badge, Activity, Cards, Clients; Settings and the person at the bottom); a top bar with the ask field and mic in place of search, and one Add button (forward a bill, upload a receipt, new invoice); Home centred at about 1000px: the sentence as the heading, a row of action buttons led by the fix, then a two-column grid of four panels (next 30 days with safe to spend and the runway, To check, Cards with Covered ✓, Coming up). Activity, Cards and Clients are full-width lists; a row opens a right-hand panel of about 420px with its own link; approving an invoice is its own page. Top: a sentence with a date and a client name ("You're fine if Acme and Northwind pay as usual. If they're late, you're ¥18,780 short on the 27th."), then safe to spend **including unbilled card spend and the next statement**. Below: the events in date order, past and future in one scroll, each a row; where the app needs you, the row carries the one button. Two drill-ins only: a card (statement, covered by what, charges, trip total) and a client (invoices, drafts, how fast they pay). Settings hold cards, bank balance and the forwarding address. No tabs, no charts as decoration (the runway stays as the one picture under the sentence), no demo buttons or persona switch on screen (the demo runs from a hidden switch). Rows, not boxes; one ink, one accent for "needs you"; no pills except Covered ✓. | Research Nov 1: daily opens are five-second checks; Simple's Safe-to-Spend was loved for the feeling and broke for card users; 2025–26 winners are narrow tools that finish a job with one approval, not dashboards. |

A typed fallback always exists. Sending anything stays a tap. | Typing is the first reason people quit money apps; 33% want to approve every transaction. |

### How it works

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D10 | **Inputs:** a forwarding address and photo/PDF upload. One reader for bills, card-use emails and receipts. Every read goes through a confirmation card; corrections become rules. No Gmail OAuth, no bank or card login; the only connection is PayPal. | Privacy; Gmail read scope needs review and caps test users. |
| D11 | **Runway:** 30 days. Cash = PayPal balance + typed bank balance. Out = bills and card statements on their due dates. In = invoices at expected (sent + the client's median days to pay) and worst case (sent + their slowest). Alerts fire on the worst case. | Believable, computed from PayPal history; fits one screen. |
| D12 | **The PayPal action is getting paid, not paying bills:** invoice finished work, remind late clients. The agent holds only draft/read tools (the toolkit's draft/send split is the approval gate); send and reminders run server-side after the user taps Approve. A verified webhook closes the loop. | Billers aren't PayPal merchants; getting paid is where PayPal is strongest. |
| D13 | **Agent Toolkit used directly** (`PayPalAPI.run()` + tool definitions), not its AI SDK wrapper. | The wrapper is pinned to an old AI SDK. |
| D14 | **Sandbox only, enforced in code.** The app refuses to start unless `PAYPAL_ENVIRONMENT=SANDBOX`; the only PayPal host in the code is the sandbox; the toolkit is created with `sandbox: true`. | Safety; the toolkit defaults to live. |
| D15 | **Webhooks are verified** with PayPal's signature check before anything changes; the browser is never trusted for payment state. | Trust. |
| D16 | **PayPal fee framing:** help the user keep more of each payout (price the fee in, invoice currency, convert in bulk). Never "leave PayPal". | Biggest single cost for a foreign-paid freelancer; PayPal-judged contest. |
| D17 | **Sandbox setup:** the app "Personal Economy Saver" is on the **Japan Business** sandbox account (Ben); the **US Personal** sandbox account is the client who pays. | How the owner's dashboard is set up. |
| D18 | **Stack:** Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript), zod 3, Drizzle ORM, PayPal Agent Toolkit 1.11, Claude as the model. Hosting and data are D31–D33. | Toolkit uses zod 3; Drizzle is thin TypeScript and emits zod schemas the engine already speaks. |
| D31 | **Data lives in Supabase** (free tier): Postgres through Drizzle, magic-link login through Supabase Auth, uploads (receipt photos, PDFs, forwarded emails) in Supabase Storage. Tables: households → members (income sources, cards; D28) → users; paypal_connections, clients, invoices, bills, cards, card_statements, charges, documents (the raw upload or email), readings (what the AI extracted, awaiting confirmation), rules (corrections), events (everything that happened; this table *is* the Activity page), webhook_deliveries, api_tokens (MCP), fx_rates. Every row carries `household_id`; money is integers in minor units plus a currency column, never floats; the FX rate is stored on each conversion; webhook_deliveries is unique on PayPal's event id; "the 27th" is the household's time zone. One PayPal merchant via env keys for Nov 12 (per-user Connect with PayPal needs partner approval); `paypal_connections` is per member so that is a column later, not a rewrite. A delete-my-household route exists from day one. | One free account gives database, login and files; no card needed. Integer money and idempotent webhooks are the two bugs that cannot be fixed after the fact. |
| D32 | **The app runs on Cloudflare Workers** through the OpenNext adapter; Cloudflare Email Routing turns the owner's domain into the forwarding address (a Worker posts each forwarded bill to the app); a Cron Trigger pings the app daily so the Supabase project never pauses (free projects pause after 7 idle days) and runs reminders. First step before any screen code: a deploy spike of the health and webhook routes. If the adapter fights Next 16's `cacheComponents`, we drop `cacheComponents`, not Cloudflare. The owner runs `wrangler login` and sets Email Routing in the dashboard; Claude never holds a Cloudflare token. The free plan's 10 ms CPU per request means no server-side image or PDF processing: files go to Claude as they are. | The owner already uses Cloudflare; it never sleeps, inbound email is free, and it is the long-term home. Vercel would be lower-risk this week, Render sleeps on the free tier. |
| D33 | **AI keys are the user's.** Settings has an Anthropic API key field; the key is sent once over HTTPS, encrypted at rest with a server secret, shown afterwards only as prefix…suffix, never logged or returned. The owner's key in `.env.local` is the **server default with a daily per-household cap**, so the demo works for judges without a key. MCP reads (D27) use the caller's key or the default. This is also the monetisation line (O8): free with your own key, paid plan when we carry the AI cost. | Pay-per-use AI is the only real cost; users of a money app expect to control it. |
| D34 | **Login and first run.** Email → magic link → httpOnly session cookie, 30 days, refreshed silently; no passwords anywhere. The public link offers **Try it**: one tap makes an anonymous session with a fresh copy of Ben's household (each judge gets their own; approve, watch the webhook land, reset); adding an email turns it into a real account. Onboarding minimum: bank balance and one card (label, closing day, due day, limit optional); PayPal is the env merchant for Nov 12 and its invoice history supplies clients and days to pay; forwarding address and AI key are shown but skippable. A partner joins a household through a magic link carrying the household id (table now, screen after Nov 12). | Under a minute to the sentence; a shared demo collides between judges. |
| D35 | **Updates are in-app for Nov 12.** Every change is an `events` row (also the Activity list); rows that matter carry `notify = true` and a `read_at`, and a bell in the top bar shows the unread count and the list. Home polls `/api/events?since=` every 5 s only while something is in flight (an invoice out, a bill being read) and otherwise refreshes on focus. Notified events: invoice paid (verified webhook), a new To check row, statement closes today, bill due in 3 days, client past their usual date, the sentence flipping between fine and short. Quiet by default: one notification a day unless money moved. No email digest. Web Push after Nov 12. | Polling is robust on Workers and needs no RLS for Realtime; the owner chose in-app over email. |
| D36 | **Reading pipeline.** Upload or forward → private Storage bucket → `documents` row `queued` → the request returns at once and the row shows "Reading…" → in the background (`waitUntil`) Claude reads against a zod schema → `readings` row `to_check` → confirm or correct → `bills` or `charges`. A correction writes a `rules` row keyed by biller, applied before the next reading is shown. Sonnet 5.5 for photos and PDFs, Haiku 5.5 for plain card-use emails; every read counts against the household's daily cap. | Nothing enters the numbers unconfirmed; the user never waits on a model call. |
| D37 | **Freshness and safety details.** PayPal balance and open invoices are pulled on open at most every 5 minutes and stored with `fetched_at`; webhooks push the rest; if PayPal is down the sentence still renders and the button says nothing was sent. Approve carries an idempotency key per draft. Mutating routes check the origin; cookies are same-site. MCP tokens are made in Settings, shown once, stored hashed, revocable, read and draft only. Per-household rate limits on `/api/ask` and on reads. The cron generates `card_statements` rows on each card's closing day from the charges. The FX rate used is stored on each row: PayPal's own when it converts, otherwise a daily reference the cron fetches (source: O9). A hidden reset exists on Try-it households only; the video is recorded on one. | The bugs that are invisible in a demo and fatal in a product. |

### How we work

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D19 | **How we build:** sample data first, then real sources. Sonnet subagents build, Opus subagents research, Claude (lead) writes contracts, monitors, reviews and assembles. Each stream owns its files. | Speed with no collisions; the owner asked for it. |

| D22 | **Commits only when the owner asks.** The repo is private at https://github.com/BenBen05059997/personal-economy-saver (branch `main`, commits under the GitHub no-reply address); making it public and the Cloudflare deploy need the owner's OK first. `reference/`, browser profiles and every `.env` file except `.env.example` are ignored. | Outward-facing actions. |
| D23 | **Current state only, everywhere.** No backup copies, no change logs, no "replaced" lists. This file holds current decisions; PLAN.md holds current work. Both are updated in the same turn things change. | The owner asked. |
| D24 | **Not for Nov 12:** live bank or card connections, Gmail inbox scan, personalised investment advice, paying bills through PayPal, transfer advisor, miles optimisation, daily chat, what-if simulator, other regions' catalogues. | Scope for the date. |

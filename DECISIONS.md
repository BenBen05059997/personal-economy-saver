# Decisions

The current decisions, and only those. Other documents (the brief, PLAN.md, the hub artifact) describe the project; when they disagree with this file, this file wins and they get fixed.

How it is kept: when a decision changes, its row is rewritten to the new decision (or deleted if it no longer applies). Nothing old is kept. Claude updates this file in the same turn a decision is made, then fixes any document that still says the old thing.

Last updated: Nov 3, 2026.

| D2 | **What it is:** a daily money assistant for freelancers paid across borders, built on PayPal. A **live planner and executor** that answers, with the user's real numbers: *Where am I?* *Can I proceed, or should I hold off?* *If I use credit, how do I pay it back from what's coming in?* Then it does the part it can through PayPal. | The owner's definition. | D3 | **The daily loop is the product.** Events from the inbox drive everything: a bill arrives; a card charge arrives (at home or travelling); a client pays; a card statement arrives; the runway shows a shortfall; a due date is near. Each shows what it means for the next 30 days and offers one action. | Help at the moment survives; lessons fade (research/users). |
| D4 | **Credit is part of "where am I".** Cards are typed in at setup (statement day, due day; no card login). Card-use emails and receipts become spend on that card; spend rolls into the coming statement, placed on the runway at its due date, with "covered / not covered" from expected income. "Can I proceed?" on a card is the room on that card: how much more it can take and still pay its statement and everything else known, in full, worst case, capped by the card limit. A cash shortfall on another day is shown separately and is not blamed on the card. | Travel and daily spending: users need to see what they've charged, when it's due, and whether incoming money covers it. |
| D5 | **Reminders, not lectures.** Before a due date: the amount, the date, whether it's covered, and one action (pay in full from the bank, or invoice first if short). | The owner: "just remind." |

### How it works

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D10 | **Inputs:** a forwarding address and photo/PDF upload. One reader for bills, card-use emails and receipts. Every read goes through a confirmation card; corrections become rules. No Gmail OAuth, no bank or card login; the only connection is PayPal. | Privacy; Gmail read scope needs review and caps test users. |
| D11 | **Runway:** 30 days. Cash = PayPal balance + typed bank balance. Out = bills and card statements on their due dates. In = invoices at expected (sent + the client's median days to pay) and worst case (sent + their slowest). Alerts fire on the worst case. USD households' invoice forecasts round to cents, not whole dollars. | Believable, computed from PayPal history; fits one screen. |
| D12 | **The PayPal action is getting paid, not paying bills:** invoice finished work, remind late clients. The agent holds only draft/read tools (the toolkit's draft/send split is the approval gate); send and reminders run server-side after the user taps Approve. A verified webhook closes the loop. Lint enforces the split: only `src/app/api/approve/route.ts` may import the send-capable `runTool` and `deleteSmokeDraft` (`no-restricted-imports` in `economy-saver/eslint.config.mjs`). | Billers aren't PayPal merchants; getting paid is where PayPal is strongest. |
| D13 | **Agent Toolkit used directly** (`PayPalAPI.run()` + tool definitions), not its AI SDK wrapper. | The wrapper is pinned to an old AI SDK. |
| D14 | **Sandbox only, enforced in code.** The app refuses to start unless `PAYPAL_ENVIRONMENT=SANDBOX`; the only PayPal host in the code is the sandbox; the toolkit is created with `sandbox: true`. | Safety; the toolkit defaults to live. |
| D15 | **Webhooks are verified** with PayPal's signature check before anything changes. A verified delivery, the fully paid invoice transition and its notification commit together. Deliveries are unique on PayPal event id. Partial or pending payments leave the invoice open. The browser never sets payment state. | Trust. |
| D16 | **PayPal fee framing:** help the user keep more of each payout (price the fee in, invoice currency, convert in bulk). Never "leave PayPal". | Biggest single cost for a foreign-paid freelancer; PayPal-judged contest. |
| D17 | **Sandbox setup:** the app "Personal Economy Saver" is on the **Japan Business** sandbox account (Ben); the **US Personal** sandbox account is the client who pays. | How the owner's dashboard is set up. |
| D18 | **Stack:** Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript), zod 3, Drizzle ORM, PayPal Agent Toolkit 1.11, Claude as the model. Hosting and data are D31–D33. | Toolkit uses zod 3; Drizzle is thin TypeScript and emits zod schemas the engine already speaks. |
| D31 | **Data lives in Supabase** (free tier), in Northeast Asia (Tokyo, `ap-northeast-1`) through the transaction pooler on port 6543, because the users and Cloudflare's nearest edge are in Japan: Postgres through Drizzle, magic-link login through Supabase Auth, uploads (receipt photos, PDFs, forwarded emails) in Supabase Storage. The current schema has 18 tables: households, members, users, paypal_connections, clients, invoices, bills, cards, card_statements, charges, documents, readings, rules, events, webhook_deliveries, api_tokens, fx_rates and request_buckets. Household data queries require `household_id`; verified deliveries for an unknown invoice can be recorded without a household and do not affect money state. Money is integer minor units plus currency, never floats; the FX rate is stored on each conversion; webhook_deliveries is unique on PayPal's event id; "the 27th" is the household's time zone. One PayPal merchant via env keys for Nov 12 (per-user Connect with PayPal needs partner approval); `paypal_connections` is per member so that is a column later, not a rewrite. A delete-my-household route exists from day one. | One free account gives database, login and files; no card needed. Integer money and idempotent webhooks are the two bugs that cannot be fixed after the fact. |
| D32 | **The app runs on Cloudflare Workers** through the OpenNext adapter; Cloudflare Email Routing turns the owner's domain into the forwarding address (a Worker posts each forwarded bill to the app); a Cron Trigger pings the app daily so the Supabase project never pauses (free projects pause after 7 idle days) and runs reminders. First step before any screen code: a deploy spike of the health and webhook routes. If the adapter fights Next 16's `cacheComponents`, we drop `cacheComponents`, not Cloudflare. The owner runs `wrangler login` and sets Email Routing in the dashboard; Claude never holds a Cloudflare token. The free plan's 10 ms CPU per request means no server-side image or PDF processing: files go to Claude as they are. | The owner already uses Cloudflare; it never sleeps, inbound email is free, and it is the long-term home. Vercel would be lower-risk this week, Render sleeps on the free tier. |
| D33 | **AI keys are the user's, stored in Supabase Vault.** Settings has an Anthropic API key field; the key is sent once over HTTPS and stored with `vault.create_secret`, so it is encrypted with a key Supabase keeps outside the database; `households.ai_key_secret_id` points at it and `ai_key_hint` shows only prefix…suffix. It is never logged or returned to the browser. The owner's key in the server secrets is the **default with a daily per-household cap**, configured explicitly by positive `ANTHROPIC_DAILY_READ_LIMIT`. A household row lock reserves one call before each app-side Claude request, using the actual date in the household timezone even for a date-anchored demo. Own-key calls do not consume this allowance. Missing key/quota settings fail clearly. Vault ownership is checked by household-bound secret name, and reset, upgrade and deletion remove the household key and private documents with retryable database pointers. The current reader uses Claude; deterministic typed questions and MCP engine tools do not invoke Claude or consume the AI quota. This is the monetisation line (O8): free with your own key, paid plan when we carry the AI cost. Built last (after Nov 5); judges never need it. | Pay-per-use AI is the only real cost; users of a money app expect to control it. Vault covers a leaked dump or backup with no extra secret to manage; it does not hide keys from someone holding the database's master credentials, which an app-held key would; acceptable for now. |
| D34 | **Login and first run.** Email → magic link → httpOnly session cookie, 30 days, refreshed silently; no passwords anywhere. The public link offers **Try it**: one tap makes an anonymous session with a fresh copy of Ben's household anchored at Nov 14, 2026 for a reproducible runway (each judge gets their own; approve, watch the webhook land, reset); adding an email turns it into a real account. Onboarding minimum: bank balance and one card (label, closing day, due day, limit optional); PayPal is the env merchant for Nov 12 and its invoice history supplies clients and days to pay; forwarding address and AI key are shown but skippable. A partner joins a household through a magic link carrying the household id (table now, screen after Nov 12). | Under a minute to the sentence; a shared demo collides between judges. |
| D35 | **Updates are in-app for Nov 12.** Every change is an `events` row (also the Activity list); rows that matter carry `notify = true` and a `read_at`, and a bell in the top bar shows the unread count and the list. Home and To check poll every 5 s while a household has a sent invoice with a PayPal ID or a queued/active reader job. Focus and visibility changes share a feed check, and only a changed feed refreshes the page. Notified events: invoice paid (verified webhook), a new To check row, statement closes today, bill due in 3 days, client past their usual date, the sentence flipping between fine and short. Quiet by default: one notification a day unless money moved. No email digest. Web Push after Nov 12. | Polling is robust on Workers and needs no RLS for Realtime; the owner chose in-app over email. |
| D36 | **Reading pipeline.** Upload a photo/PDF or forward to the household’s opaque address. Store the original in a private bucket, return after queuing, and read it in the background through the request’s lifetime hook. `ANTHROPIC_DOCUMENT_MODEL` names the model for photos, PDFs and mail with attachments. `ANTHROPIC_TEXT_MODEL` names the model for plain mail. Validate extracted fields and show them in To check. Confirmation creates a bill or card charge. Rule patches carry forward corrected names, categories, entry types and cards. Amount and date edits apply to the individual document. Default-key reads consume the daily household allowance, and all reader invocations are rate-limited. | Nothing enters the numbers before confirmation. Model selection and the forwarding domain require explicit settings. |
| D37 | **Freshness and safety details.** Home refreshes only locally associated sandbox invoice IDs, with a five-minute cooldown that includes failed attempts. Only the designated non-demo household (`PAYPAL_BALANCE_HOUSEHOLD_ID`) can pull the merchant’s available balance. Other households use their own verified credits. Report timestamps and verified payment timestamps prevent stale reports from erasing receipts or counting them twice. A read can close a cancelled invoice. Only a verified webhook marks it paid. Home renders stored amounts with an explicit status when refresh fails. Demo receipts keep the provider’s timestamp in Activity and use a business date no earlier than the scenario date or the invoice send date. Approve carries an idempotency key per draft. Mutating routes check the origin; cookies are same-site. MCP tokens are made in Settings, shown once, stored hashed, revocable, read and draft only. Per-household rate limits on `/api/ask` and on reads, on drafts (20 a minute) and on approvals (10 a minute); Try it is capped app-wide at 60 new demo households an hour. PayPal toolkit calls time out after 20 s so a hung call cannot hold approval row locks; `PayPal-Request-Id` makes the retry safe. Sign-in links return to `APP_URL` (required setting), never to the request's Origin header; a missing `APP_URL` raises a clear error. The webhook route does not tell anonymous callers why a delivery was rejected; the reason goes to the server log. The AI-key route caps its body at 4 KiB by reading the stream, not by trusting Content-Length. The daily job processes only UUIDs explicitly configured in `CRON_HOUSEHOLD_IDS` and refuses a missing list; it never enumerates every household. It generates `card_statements` rows on each card's closing day from confirmed charges. Confirming a late charge updates its unpaid closed-cycle statement exactly once under the same household lock, including reader confirmations. The FX rate used is stored on each row: PayPal's own when it converts, otherwise a daily reference the cron fetches (source: O9). A hidden reset exists on Try-it households only; the video is recorded on one. | The bugs that are invisible in a demo and fatal in a product. |
| O9 | **FX references use the [ECB daily euro reference XML](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml).** USD/JPY is JPY-per-EUR divided by USD-per-EUR, stored as integer micro-units with the ECB publication date and household id. Onboarding and the daily job store each real household’s snapshot; demo households keep their fixed fictional rate. Missing a dated snapshot is a clear error. | [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html) publishes working-day informational rates, not executable conversion prices. |

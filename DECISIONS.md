# Decisions

This file holds the design: what the product does, for whom, and why. When another document disagrees with it, this file wins and the other gets fixed. It holds no code facts (paths, names, routes, tables, test counts); for those, read the code, which is the truth.

How it is kept: when a decision changes, its row is rewritten in place to the new decision, or deleted if it no longer applies. Nothing old is kept.

## The shape of the system

- One Next.js app on Cloudflare Workers over one Supabase project in Tokyo (D31, D32). Every household's data is scoped by its id, with a database role and row-level security as a second wall (D38).
- The engine is plain code with no model in it. Every number on every face comes from it: the sentence, safe to spend, card room, the split, what-ifs, goal pace, the checks on a bill and the daily findings (D2, D61, D64).
- The model understands what was typed, said or uploaded, picks tools and words the result. Code chooses the kind of reply, confirms every amount against the tool results and makes every write, so any model can be swapped in and only reading and tool-calling quality vary (D40, D56, D60).
- What the agent, MCP or the reader proposes waits as a pending row, a draft or an offer until the person taps (D12, D39, D61, D67). A payment link is made only on the Approve tap, a payment is taken only after PayPal reports that the client approved it (or, in a Try it household, when the judge plays the client with the sandbox test card), and payment state comes only from a verified webhook (D12, D15).
- One tool catalogue serves Ask and MCP (D27, D40). A daily pass in code, with no model, finds what is worth saying and can remind by email or push (D64, D65).
- Data comes in five kinds: record, memory, canvases, journal and documents. Memory, canvases and the journal can be kept on one device instead of the cloud (D59). Files go to the model as uploaded, and the Worker does no image or PDF processing (D32).

### How it works

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D10 | **Inputs:** a forwarding address and photo/PDF upload (one or several files at a time). One reader for bills, card-use emails and receipts. Every read goes through a confirmation card (To check, or the card under a file attached in Ask, D61); corrections become rules. No Gmail OAuth, no bank or card login; the only account connection is PayPal (the model a household chooses is D56). | Privacy; Gmail read scope needs review and caps test users. |
| D11 | **Runway:** 30 days (to the last statement when that is later); running balances are rounded to the currency's minor unit. Cash = PayPal balance (read from PayPal for a connected household, D76) + typed bank balance. Out = bills and card statements on their due dates. In = invoices at expected (the day the payment link was made + the client's median days to pay) and worst case (that day + their slowest). Alerts fire on the worst case. USD households' invoice forecasts round to cents, not whole dollars. | Believable, computed from the household's own payment history; fits one screen. |
| D12 | **The PayPal action is getting paid, not paying bills: a payment link for finished work, offered again to a late client.** The agent and MCP hold only read and draft tools. The person taps Approve on the invoice, and fipa asks PayPal for an order for the invoice's amount and currency, paid to the household's connected PayPal (a Try it household's to fipa's own sandbox account). The person gets one fipa link per invoice, "Copy link for <client>", which never expires. Opening it shows only the item and the amount, naming neither the household nor an invoice number. Tapping Pay first asks PayPal where the invoice's order stands: if the client has already approved or paid it, the page says the payment is being confirmed and no second order is made; otherwise the client goes to PayPal on the live order, or on a fresh one for the approved amount when the last one has lapsed or its payment was declined. Every sent invoice has such a link, a Try it household's included. Nudging a late client means copying the same link again: fipa sends nothing to a client, and PayPal invoices and PayPal reminders are not used. "Send the link again" always leads to that invoice's page, the earliest-expected one when several are late, from Home's button, the options list, the suggestion and the canvas starter, in the same words. The owner can end a sent invoice's link ("End this link"): it leaves the forecast, the public link says it has ended and Activity records it; a payment that still completes on an ended link marks the invoice paid, because the money arrived, and so does one that completes on an order the link has since replaced, when PayPal's event names that invoice and its amount, currency and receiving account all match. The payment is taken only when a verified webhook reports that the client approved it, and the invoice is marked paid only by the verified completed payment (D15). A Try it household's judge can play the client without a PayPal login: a sent, unpaid invoice offers "Pay as the client (sandbox test card)", and fipa pays the invoice's amount into its own sandbox account through PayPal's Orders API with PayPal's published sandbox test card. This is allowed for a Try it household only and is never offered or accepted for any other; the invoice is still marked paid only by the verified webhook (D15). Lint enforces the split: making an order may be imported only where Approve and the link's Pay run, taking a payment only where the webhook runs, and the test-card payment only where the Try it pay-as-the-client action runs. | Billers aren't PayPal merchants; getting paid is where PayPal is strongest. Judges have no PayPal sandbox buyer login, and the loop (approve, the client pays, the webhook lands, the sentence changes) must be playable without one. PayPal keeps multiparty invoicing for selected partners, so invoices from a person's own account are out of reach. An order lapses three hours after it is made unless it is captured, so a link a client opens weeks later must make its order when it is used. |
| D13 | **PayPal through its REST APIs, through one sandbox client.** Orders, partner onboarding, seller status, transaction search, balances and webhook verification are plain REST calls; the Agent Toolkit is not used, because its calls cannot carry the partner attribution and act-for-seller headers that multiparty calls need. Every call carries fipa's partner attribution code, read from a server setting; a call for a household's PayPal account also carries the assertion naming that account. Orders have no due date, so an invoice's due date is fipa's own and the expected payment date is the day its link was made plus the client's observed days to pay. PayPal's error words are shown as PayPal gives them. | One client keeps the sandbox guard, the timeouts and the partner headers in one place; the toolkit had no partner, balance or webhook calls and pinned an old zod. |
| D14 | **Sandbox only, enforced in code.** AGENTS.md rule 1; every PayPal call goes through the one client, which talks only to the sandbox host and refuses any other environment, and a stored connection can only be a sandbox one. | Safety; a platform app's live keys would move real money for other people. |
| D15 | **Webhooks are verified** with PayPal's signature check before anything changes. A verified client approval is answered by taking that payment once; a repeated delivery or a second approval never takes it twice, and PayPal's answer is read for its own status: a payment PayPal declines is not recorded as a payment and the link opens again so the client can pay with a fresh order, and a verified denial that arrives later for a payment fipa had recorded as pending does the same. Only a refusal PayPal names as final (the payer declined, the order lapsed or is already completed, and the like) is treated as final; anything else, including fipa's own sign-in to PayPal being refused, is refused back to PayPal so it sends the event again. The check and the taking of a payment happen under one lock on the invoice, so "Paid another way", "End this link" or the person's Yes arriving at the same moment cannot count the money twice. The signature check sends PayPal the event exactly as it arrived. A verified completed payment marks its invoice paid only when it belongs to that invoice's order, or to an earlier order of the same link whose amount, currency and receiving account match, and matches the invoice's amount and currency, also when the link has been ended; the paid transition, the PayPal balance credit, the pots and the notification commit together. A link payment is counted once however it is seen first: if the PayPal read records it before the webhook, the webhook closes the invoice against what the read recorded and credits nothing again. A verified onboarding or revoked-consent event confirms or removes the household's connection (D76). Deliveries are unique on PayPal's event id. A pending payment or a different amount leaves the invoice open. The browser never sets payment state; the client's return page only says PayPal is confirming. The one exception is the person's own statement about a payment PayPal itself reported (D76): they may say it is the payment for an open invoice, and nothing else is trusted from the browser. | Trust. |
| D16 | **PayPal fee framing:** help the user keep more of each payout (price the fee in, invoice currency, convert in bulk). Never "leave PayPal". | Biggest single cost for a foreign-paid freelancer; PayPal-judged contest. |
| D17 | **Sandbox setup:** fipa's sandbox app is a PayPal Platform app on fipa's own sandbox business account, which also receives Try it payments; a separate sandbox business account stands for Ben's own PayPal and connects through onboarding (D76); the **US Personal** sandbox account is the client who pays. | How the owner's dashboard is set up; a platform cannot onboard its own account. |
| D18 | **Stack:** Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript), zod 3, Drizzle ORM, PayPal's REST APIs through one sandbox client (D13), and any model through one call (D56). Hosting and data are D31–D33. | Drizzle is thin TypeScript and emits zod schemas the engine already speaks. |
| D31 | **The record lives in Supabase** (free tier), in Northeast Asia (Tokyo) through the transaction pooler, because the users and Cloudflare's nearest edge are in Japan: Postgres through Drizzle, magic-link login through Supabase Auth, uploads (receipt photos, PDFs, forwarded emails) in Supabase Storage. Memory, pinned canvases and conversations live there too unless the household keeps them on one device (D59). Household data queries require the household id; verified deliveries for an unknown order, invoice or PayPal account can be recorded without a household and do not affect money state. Money is integer minor units plus currency, never floats; the FX rate is stored on each conversion; webhook deliveries are unique on PayPal's event id; "the 27th" is the household's time zone. Each household connects its own PayPal through PayPal's partner onboarding (D76); the connection is the household owner's and holds the PayPal balance. Deleting an account exists (D59). | One free account gives database, login and files; no card needed. Integer money and idempotent webhooks are the two bugs that cannot be fixed after the fact. A distant region adds about 0.3 s of network per query. |
| D32 | **The app runs on Cloudflare Workers** through the OpenNext adapter, with the read-only static-assets incremental cache (the default dummy cache hangs the prerendered page). Cloudflare Email Routing turns the owner's domain into the forwarding address (a Worker posts each forwarded bill to the app); an hourly Cron Trigger calls the app to keep Supabase active and dispatch due daily passes and reminders in each household's time zone (D64). The owner runs the Cloudflare login and sets Email Routing in the dashboard; Claude never holds a Cloudflare token. The free plan's 10 ms CPU per request means no server-side image or PDF processing: files go to the model as they are. | The owner already uses Cloudflare; it never sleeps, inbound email is free, and it is the long-term home. Vercel would be lower-risk, Render sleeps on the free tier. |
| D33 | **AI keys are the household's, stored in Supabase Vault; the owner's connection is capped per household per day.** A household that chooses its own model (D56) gives its key in Settings → Your AI model; it is sent once over HTTPS, **sealed** in the Worker under a Worker-only secret (the household id is bound in, so a sealed text copied to another household does not open) and stored in Vault, so it is encrypted with a key Supabase keeps outside the database as well. Settings shows only its last four characters, and only for a key of 20 or more characters. It is never logged or returned to the browser. The owner's connection in the server settings is the **default** where one is set, and the owner pays for it, so each household on it (a Try it household included) has a bounded daily allowance: 30 questions, which Ask and voice fills share, and 10 document reads, counted per calendar day (UTC) and spent before the model is called, once per question or document. Past the allowance the person is told in words to ask again tomorrow or add their own key. An upload is refused where the person sees it, before the file is stored; forwarded mail is kept in To check as not read, to retry later. A household using its own key (D56) is never capped or counted. Missing settings fail clearly. A key saved before sealing existed is refused with "Your saved AI key predates sealed storage. Save your key again in Settings.", never read as a key. Reset, upgrade and deletion remove the household key and private documents. Document reads and Ask call the model; MCP engine tools do not. This is the monetisation line: free with your own model, a paid plan when we carry the AI cost. A Try it household's key is dropped after 24 hours with no activity. **The allowance shows only for a household on the owner's connection, never for one with its own key, and stays quiet until it matters:** Ask shows one line under the box when 5 or fewer questions are left today, pointing to Settings for a key with no limit, and at the limit the refusal links to Settings; Upload shows the same when 3 or fewer document reads are left. While the owner's connection is in use, Settings → Your AI model says in plain words which model and provider answer, the host questions and documents go to, and the daily allowance, and that the person's own key removes the limit; it never shows server setting names. | Pay-per-use AI is the only real cost; users of a money app expect to control it. Vault covers a leaked dump or backup; the second seal, whose key lives only in the Worker, also hides keys from someone holding the database's master credentials. |
| D34 | **Login and first run.** Email → magic link → session cookie, 30 days, refreshed silently; no passwords anywhere. The public link offers **Try it**: one tap makes an anonymous session with a fresh copy of Ben's household anchored at Nov 14, 2026 for a reproducible runway (each judge gets their own; approve, pay the link as the sandbox client, watch the webhook land, reset; Reset sits beside the Try it banner on every page, and at phone width in the account menu); adding an email turns it into a real account. Signing out, signing in with email and the account page are reachable from the person at the bottom of the menu on every screen. The Try it cookie is a random token; the household stores only its SHA-256 hash, so the household id is never a bearer value in logs, page script or the device store. A Try it household's saved AI key is removed after 24 hours with no activity. Onboarding minimum: a name; the bank balance and a card come later (D47); PayPal is connected later by the owner in Settings → PayPal (D76); a Try it household cannot connect, and its payment links pay into fipa's own sandbox account; forwarding address and AI model are shown but skippable. A partner joins through an invitation (D68). One login belongs to one household. | Under a minute to the sentence; a shared demo collides between judges. |
| D35 | **Updates are in-app, with optional reminders outside it (D65).** Every change is an Activity event, and the bell in the top bar is the one place to see them: Activity, newest first, each row linking to its bill, invoice, client or pot, older ones on request; rows that matter are marked to notify and carry a read time, and only they count on the bell and show as unread. With nothing yet it says what will appear there (a payment link made or paid, a bill read, a change made). There is no separate Activity page. Home and To check poll only while something is in flight. Notified events: invoice paid (verified webhook), a payment received in the connected PayPal, PayPal disconnected, a new To check row, statement closes today, bill due in 3 days, the sentence flipping between fine and short. Quiet by default: one notification a day unless money moved. | Polling is robust on Workers and needs no row-level subscriptions; the owner chose in-app first. |
| D36 | **Reading pipeline.** Upload a photo/PDF or forward to the household's opaque address. Store the original in a private bucket, return after queuing, and read it in the background. The connection's document model (or the household's own, D56) reads photos, PDFs and mail with attachments; its text model reads plain mail. Validate extracted fields, run the checks of D61 on them, and show both in To check, and in Ask when the file was attached there. Confirmation creates a bill or card charge. Rule patches carry forward corrected names, categories, entry types and cards. Amount and date edits apply to the individual document. Reads on a household's own key are not capped, and on the owner's connection they have their own daily allowance (D33), spent when the file arrives so a refusal is seen, not silent; forwarded mail and retries are counted when read. Uploads have no per-minute limit: a per-minute limit once refused a person's seventh file instead of waiting, and forwarded emails shared it, so a burst of card emails could lose a bill. What guards a file is its size (6 MiB), the storage cap (D37), a household session or the Worker's secret, and one document per identical file. Upload and Ask's Attach a bill take several files at once; each is sent on its own, one after another, and a refused file does not stop the others. | Nothing enters the numbers before confirmation. Model selection and the forwarding domain require explicit settings. |
| D37 | **Freshness and safety details.** Home reads a connected household's PayPal (D76), its balance and the payments received since the last read, with a five-minute cooldown that includes failed attempts; the read works forward from the last read in spans of about a month, keeping each finished span, so a long gap skips nothing, and each read looks again at the last seven days, so a payment that was pending and cleared later is picked up once; one open of Home waits about ten seconds at most and then says "Still catching up on older PayPal payments. Showing what is read so far."; a balance in a currency fipa does not hold is left out, and a payment whose payer address fipa cannot keep is skipped and the read carries on. No other household reads PayPal on open, and one that is not connected, Try it included, keeps the balance its own verified payments built. A balance report for a PayPal account other than the connected one is refused, and report and payment timestamps prevent stale reports from erasing receipts or counting them twice. Only a verified webhook marks a link payment paid. Home shows stored amounts with an explicit status when the read fails. Demo receipts keep the provider's timestamp in Activity and use a business date no earlier than the scenario date or the day the link was made. The person's Approve tap never holds the household's records locked while PayPal answers; if the invoice was edited while its link was being made, Approve refuses with "This invoice changed while its link was being made. Tap Approve again." Approve carries an idempotency key per draft, and every order and every payment taken carries its own, so a retry never makes a second order or takes a payment twice.

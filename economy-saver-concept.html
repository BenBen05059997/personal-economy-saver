1 · The daily loop

Something happens, the app says what it means, you tap once

The daily loop is the product. Events from the inbox drive everything. Each one shows what it means for the next 30 days and offers one action.

One turn of the loop

  1. ArrivesAn eventForwarded email, photo or PDF, a client payment, a due date
  2. ReaderConfirmation cardEvery read is confirmed; corrections become rules
  3. Runway30 days recomputedExpected line and worst-case line
  4. SuggestionOne actionThe user's own number and a date, with the source behind a tap
  5. You decideApprove or not nowApproved actions run through PayPal

The 30-day runway

Cash is the PayPal balance plus a typed bank balance. Out is bills and card statements on their due dates. In is each invoice at its expected date (sent plus the client's median days to pay) and at its worst case (sent plus their slowest). Alerts fire on the worst case.

Rules for every suggestion

  • One per real event, with the user's number, currency and a date.
  • A one-line why, with the source behind a tap.
  • One action behind a confirm, plus "not now" and "never for this".
  • Cash is checked before any "pay now". Never リボ, minimum payments or a loan as a fix.
  • Information only: no product to buy, no one person's tax position, anything paid is labelled.

Inputs

A forwarding address and photo or PDF upload. One reader handles bills, card-use emails and receipts. There is no Gmail OAuth and no bank or card login; the only connection is PayPal.

2 · Credit and travel

What is charged, when it is due, and whether incoming money covers it

Credit is part of "where am I". The app reminds; it does not lecture.

Cards at setup

The user types each card in: a name, the statement day and the due day. No card login.

Charges roll into the statement

Card-use emails and receipts become spend on that card. The spend rolls into the coming statement, which sits on the runway at its due date.

Due-date reminders

Before a due date: the amount, the date, covered or not covered from expected income, and one action. Pay in full from the bank, or invoice first if short.

Paying back from incoming

The statement is matched against invoices still to be paid. If the worst case does not cover it, the app drafts the invoice or reminder that would.

4 · PayPal at the centre

PayPal data in, AI reasoning, PayPal action out

The PayPal action is getting paid, not paying bills. Billers are not PayPal merchants, and getting paid is where PayPal is strongest.

  1. Agent draftsInvoice draftcreate_invoice makes a draft only
  2. You approveTap ApproveOn the confirmation card
  3. PayPal sendsInvoice sentsend_invoice, run by the server
  4. Client paysPays in the sandboxThe US Personal sandbox account
  5. Signed webhookPAID eventINVOICING.INVOICE.PAID, signature verified
  6. Runway updatesGap closesThe invoice shows Paid and the shortfall clears

APIs and tools, and why

PieceUsed for
Agent Toolkit 1.11Used directly (PayPalAPI.run() plus its tool definitions), not its AI SDK wrapper, which is pinned to an old AI SDK.
Draft and read toolsThe agent holds create_invoice, list_invoices, get_invoice and search_invoicing. They give the draft and each client's paid dates, so "Acme pays 9 days late" is real.
Send toolssend_invoice and reminders run server-side after the user taps Approve. The toolkit's draft/send split is the approval gate.
BalanceDirect REST call to /v1/reporting/balances, because the toolkit has no balance tool.
WebhooksThe toolkit has none. The server checks each event with PayPal's verify-webhook-signature before anything changes; INVOICING.INVOICE.PAID updates the runway.
Sandbox accountsThe app "Personal Economy Saver" is on the Japan Business sandbox account (Ben). The US Personal sandbox account is the client who pays.

Sandbox only, enforced in code

The app refuses to start unless PAYPAL_ENVIRONMENT=SANDBOX. The only PayPal host in the code is the sandbox, and the toolkit is created with sandbox: true because it defaults to live.

Verified webhooks

PayPal's signature check runs before any state changes. The browser is never trusted for payment state.

The fee framing

For a foreign-paid freelancer the PayPal fee is the biggest single cost. The app helps the user keep more of each payout: price the fee into the invoice, invoice in the right currency, convert in bulk. It never says "leave PayPal".
